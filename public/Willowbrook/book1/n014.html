Night 14

Mama's Cup

Month 1: Arrival

There is a rule they say on airplanes, and it is really a rule about parents. Put your own mask on first. Mama was healing. Her body had done an enormous thing, and enormous things are not tidied up in a weekend. Yet somehow her own lunch kept not happening, and her water glass lived, permanently, in the other room.

It was Miss Rosa who set it straight, on the phone from her own couch, eleven days behind with a new baby of her own. "You feed her," she said. "So who feeds you?" There was a pause on the line while both babies were checked. Neither mother had an answer.

There were new rules after that, small as vitamins. A full glass of water at every feed, the baby drinking and Mama drinking, the two of them at the same quiet work. Real food before noon. A nap ahead of any chore. And in the first weeks, a walk to the mailbox counted as a marathon, because it is one.

Mama drank the whole glass down, standing at the sink, looking out at the muddy yard, at nothing. Olivia, tucked in the wrap against her chest, rose and fell like a small boat on a kind sea.
