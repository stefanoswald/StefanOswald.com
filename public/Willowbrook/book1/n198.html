Night 198

Her Cupboard

Month 7: Explorers and Guardrails

The latches went on the kitchen cupboards in one long evening of Papa saying words to a screwdriver. Every low door got one, the bin, the knife drawer, the one with the plastic bags. The cleaning things under the sink moved up high anyway, latch or no latch. The medicines had lived on the top shelf of the bathroom since March and stayed there.

One cupboard was left alone. The low one with the pots.

It is hers. Everything in it can be banged, chewed, sat in, or worn as a hat, and none of it can hurt her. She found it the first morning and emptied it by lunch, and has emptied it every day since, lid by lid, with the seriousness of someone moving house.

The cat's dish moved too. It had been on the floor by the back door since before she was born, and a crawler will eat a cat's dinner faster than the cat will. The dish went up on the washing machine. The cat sat under it and looked at Mama about that.

The kitchen got loud. A pot lid on a tile floor is a sound you can hear from the road.

"That's her," Mama said to Rosa on the phone, over it. "She's cooking."

Olivia put the biggest lid on her head and sat under it, cooking.
