Night 63

The Window Watcher

Month 3: Rhythm

Between one sleep and the next, every baby has a window, a stretch of good awake time before the gears begin to grind. In the third month Olivia's ran about ninety minutes, give or take a mood. Mama had mostly stopped watching the clock and started watching the baby, but she kept the old hourglass on the shelf turned over anyway, sand running, as a second opinion.

Olivia woke from her nap at two. She kicked, she chatted with the ceiling, she swatted a felt star with great feeling. Around three-fifteen the window began to close: the long stare, the ear rub, the first yawn drifting past like a petal on the brook.

Miss the window and everybody pays. The overtired baby fights sleep hardest exactly when she needs it most, and the second wind is all storm. Catch it, and sleep comes easy as a door swinging shut on good hinges.

So the afternoon ran on window time. The walk went early, when she was brightest. The quiet play came as it narrowed. The trip to the crib happened while it was still open a crack.

Ninety minutes is not a rule. It is her number for now, and it will stretch as she grows, into whole afternoons.

The last of the sand ran out at three-thirty. Olivia was already down.
