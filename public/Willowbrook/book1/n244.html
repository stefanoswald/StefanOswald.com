Night 244

The First Step

Month 9: One Step, One Word

It came on a Wednesday, before lunch, with the last leaves letting go past the window, and nobody had a phone out.

Olivia stood at the couch as she had stood for a month, one hand on the cushion, the other busy with nothing. Mama sat on the floor three feet away folding socks and held out her arms, as she had most days since October.

The hand let go. The weight tipped. The right foot swung out and landed, and the rest of her lurched after it into Mama's hands.

One step. Then the sit.

"Oh," said Mama, to the socks.

The first thing to know about a first step is that it is one step. The second came on Saturday. Then three and a catch, three and a floor, for a week, while the crawl kept the long routes. Nine months sits early in the range, and the range is nine to eighteen, and the ones who wait until seventeen months arrive at the same sandbox on the same legs.

That night Papa stood at the rail trying the sentence for fit. "She walked."

"She did," said Mama. She wrote STEP in the notebook by the kettle, in capitals, and underlined it twice.

Outside, the wind took the leaves down toward the brook. Upstairs, the walker slept with one foot through the bars.
