Night 231

The First Shoes

Month 8: The Velcro Weeks and the Leaves

October raised the question the barefoot rule had put off all summer. The outdoors now had a citizen who cruised its benches and would, one of these months, walk its ground, and the ground, unlike the living room, kept gravel and cold.

Indoors the rule stood: bare feet. The outdoor pair exists for protection. Pavement, splinters, wet leaves, the customs of shops. It is equipment, not therapy.

The old fitter at the shoe shop knew the order before it was given. Soft-soled. Flexible enough to fold nearly in half. Nothing in the way of how the foot itself works, a thin barrier and no more, light, and fastened well enough to survive the era's second business.

Because the first business of all first shoes is their removal. The inaugural pair rode home in their box, were taken off twice on the way from feet they had never been on, and took their place by the door beside the household's larger shoes.

That evening, barefoot at the window per rule, Olivia pointed at her own shoes on the mat and gave them her entire relevant vocabulary.

"Uh-oh."

Accurate, Mama agreed, as usual.
