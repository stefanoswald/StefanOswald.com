Night 350

The Square on the New Calendar

Month 12: The Brook Comes Unstuck

The new calendar started with March, because their year did. Mama drew it at the kitchen table with Olivia on her lap and one crayon each. A candle at the top. A row of squares. In the first square, the birthday, and in a square the week after, the doctor.

"One-year visit," said Mama, writing it. "They'll weigh you. They'll measure you. You'll hate the paper on the table."

The doctor's card was already under the lemon magnet on the fridge. The visit had shots in it, the one-year ones, and questions, the kind that had a range for an answer: does she walk, does she point, does she wave, does she say two words, does she pick up small things, does she drink from a cup.

And the milk. After the birthday, and after the doctor said so, the formula tin would go and whole milk would come, the full-fat kind, in a cup and not a bottle. Not before the birthday. Under one, the milk was still Mama's and the tin's, and that was the rule.

Under the doctor, a square for the dentist, who wanted a look at the six teeth by one.

Olivia put her crayon down in the birthday square and pressed. It made a green mark that went off the edge.

"You're in it," said Mama.
