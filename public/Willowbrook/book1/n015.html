Night 15

The Team of Two

Month 1: Arrival

Two shipwrecked sailors cannot both bail the boat every hour of the night. They will only sink slower. Mama and Papa worked out shifts. Mama went to bed at eight and slept through the first stretch. Papa took the ten o'clock feed with a bottle, so one of them got one long unbroken piece of dark.

Weekends they traded mornings, one sleeping shamelessly late while the other held a very quiet party downstairs with Olivia and the toaster.

It did not always go smoothly. At three one morning there was an argument in the hallway, held in hisses so as not to wake the person it was about. "It's your turn." "I did the last one." "You did the one before the last one." A pause. "Sorry." "Me too." A fair count cannot be kept at three a.m. Counting anything at that hour turns mean.

They made one more rule, the hardest. Say the number out loud. "I'm at a two tonight," one of them would admit, and the other would answer, "I've got her. Go to bed."

The handoff in the hallway became a thing of its own. A pass of the warm bundle, a kiss on a tired cheek, a whispered "we're doing it." Papa carried her down the stairs. Mama was asleep before the door closed.
