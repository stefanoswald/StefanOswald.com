Night 274

The Word-a-Week Notebook

Month 10: First Snow

The notebook by the kettle began as a list of feeds and became a list of everything. On the first of December Mama gave it a clean page and a heading, Words, and copied in the only entry so far. Duck, with its November date, underlined twice, since the first one gets underlined.

The page filled slowly. A word a week would be a flood at this age, and most weeks brought half of one.

"Dah," said Olivia at the window, at Mrs. Henderson's old gold dog going by in the snow.

"Dog," said Mama. "That's two."

"That's a sound," said Papa from the toaster. "Duck was a word."

There is no winning an argument about whether a baby said a word. The household made a rule instead, over cold toast. A word counts when it comes twice, aimed at the thing, before anyone has said the thing first. Dah came again on Thursday, at the dog, and once more at Papa's slipper, entered in the margin as a near miss.

The list of what she followed was longer. Papa tried it on the facing page and gave up at the bottom.

By the end of the month the page held two words, one argument, and a smudge where the kettle had steamed it.
