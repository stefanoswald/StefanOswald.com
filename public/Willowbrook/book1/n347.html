Night 347

The Boy Next Door

Month 12: The Brook Comes Unstuck

Two porches down lived Mateo, who was two, and who had shouted BABY at Olivia across the lane in November. Now he was in his yard with a shovel, piling what was left of the snow into a lump, and his mother was on their side of the fence, and Mama and Olivia were on theirs.

"Fort," said Mateo. Then, seeing Olivia: "Baby."

He came to the fence with a stick. He held it out. Olivia reached for it, and he took it back, and looked at it, and kept it.

"He doesn't share yet," said his mother. "It's not personal."

"She doesn't ask yet," said Mama. "They're a good match."

The fort was more of a heap, grey and going to slush at the bottom, but Mateo patted it with the shovel anyway. Olivia watched every pat. Watching a bigger child is a whole job at one, and she did it with her mouth open.

"Fort," said Mateo again, and gave the heap a kick, and it stayed a heap.

Olivia bent down, took a handful of the grey snow, and ate it.

Mama fished it out. February snow is not for eating. It had not come up.

Over the fence, Mateo watched her, with the stick, and she watched him back, until somebody's nose needed wiping and it was hers.
