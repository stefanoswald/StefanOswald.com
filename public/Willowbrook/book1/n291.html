Night 291

The Tractor's Passenger

Month 10: First Snow

The tractor lives in the barn from the first snow to the thaw. Every Sunday since summer it has been patted on the way past, and this Sunday the flank was cold under the mitten.

Grandpa Joe stood looking at it for a while, with her on his arm. Then he lifted her up onto the seat.

"Careful of the," said Papa from the doorway.

"It's not going anywhere till April," said Grandpa Joe.

The seat was cracked and enormous. Her mittens found the wheel and the wheel turned, freely, going nowhere at all, and she turned it the whole way one direction and the whole way back. The levers were looked at. A big hand rested on them, and they were not touched, and that was the whole conversation about levers.

The horn got one toot. Henrietta complained from the far end of the barn.

That seat held Grandpa Joe at four, and Papa at four. Now it held her, at ten months, in somebody's arms, and a machine met parked, with permission, is one a child need not be frightened of later.

He lifted her down into the straw. She gave the tire two slaps, the way it is done, and her breath went up in the cold light.
