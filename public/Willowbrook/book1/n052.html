Night 52

The Bottle Learns Its Manners

Month 2: Smiles and Sleep

The bottle joined the family slowly, at whatever speed the baby set.

What was in it mattered less than how it came. Mama's pumped milk here, formula in plenty of other houses, both of them love in a bottle. Paced feeding, the nurse had called it. Bottle talk for letting the baby drive.

He held Olivia snug and half upright, never lying flat, and kept the bottle nearly level, so the milk came by her effort and not by gravity's. Every so often he tipped the nipple down and let her breathe, look around, and decide whether she wanted more. A bottle held straight up pours faster than a baby can say when. Held level, her own full gauge stays in charge, the tummy is spared the too-fast ache, and a feed stays what it is, being held by someone whose whole job is noticing you.

The first bottles had been refused with feeling. Babies are traditionalists. The cure, once, had been Mama leaving the room entirely. Traditions bend faster when the founder is out of the house. By now she was allowed back, if she read her book and did not comment.

Olivia took her evening bottle at her own pace, watching Papa throughout, and pausing twice to check who was holding it. He passed. She burped like someone much larger. She was asleep before the bottle was rinsed.
