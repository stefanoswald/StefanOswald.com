Night 322

The One-Nap Feint

Month 11: Mittens and the Early Dark

The two-nap day had held since August, through teeth and colds and one whole snowstorm. In the third week of January it sent up a rumor that it might be over. The morning nap was refused four days running, loudly, standing up.

There was a case for listening. She did seem done with it. There was a stronger case for the calendar. One nap usually comes later than this, and a baby who suddenly hates the morning nap nearly always has something else going on: a molar, a new skill running hot, a week of grey light that has shoved the day sideways. Most of these pass inside a fortnight.

So the shape of the day stayed the shape of the day. The nap was offered at its hour. The blind went down. Mama sat in the chair with a book and made the same boring visits to the rail every few minutes. "Still nap time. Lie down. I'm here." Nothing about the day was rewritten on one week's weather.

Four mornings of standing. On the fifth she stood, and complained, and sat, and lay down mid-complaint, and slept an hour and a half.

The two-nap day stood where it was.

Mama put the book down, unread, and sat in the quiet for as long as it lasted.
