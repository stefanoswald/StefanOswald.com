Night 317

Snow at the Window

Month 11: Mittens and the Early Dark

A flurry came through at dusk, small and dry, not meaning to stay. Olivia saw the first flake cross the lamplight and pulled herself up at the sill so fast the curtain came with her.

Both hands on the glass. Face on the glass. Her breath fogged a round patch that she looked through, puzzled, then wiped with her nose.

Mama crouched beside her and pointed, which is all there is to do at a window. "Snow. Look, there goes one. There goes one."

Olivia's finger followed a flake down the whole pane and then looked for it at the bottom. Gone. She checked the floor. She checked Mama. Where do they go? Neither of them knew.

A baby and her mother pointing at the same flake is a small thing. It is also most of how words get built, one look at a time.

The flurry blew off toward the farm. Overnight the cold did the rest, and by morning the same pane had grown ferns of frost from the corners in.

Her finger went to a fern. It melted where she touched, a clear little hole. She touched another. Another hole.

Mama drew a circle with her own finger. Olivia put a wet dot in the middle of it.

The frost was gone by the time the kettle boiled.
