Night 222

Feet First

Month 8: The Velcro Weeks and the Leaves

Olivia was found on the couch.

Nobody had put her there. The route was worked out afterward by the arriving authorities: the floor pillow, fallen against the couch's side, climbed. The cushion's edge, seized with the same grip that hauled her up the crib rail. One knee, then the other. Then the top, looked around from with the calm of a mountaineer who has no plan to explain herself.

Up had entered the household, and the house rules needed their last big revision. The windows came first. Screens hold no weight. Guards or locked sashes on anything above the ground floor, and no climbable furniture beneath any window, ever, the one rule here with no supervised exceptions. The bookshelf anchors were checked again, since the shelf was now not only pullable but boardable.

The couch itself was conceded. It would be climbed a thousand times, and the concession beat the argument. The floor stayed clear around it, and the way down was taught like the stairs: backward, feet first, every time.

The ceiling, Papa noted, was at least fixed in place. It was the only thing in the house that was.

She was watched at all elevations after that. That evening she reached the cushion's edge, turned herself around without being asked, and went down backward, feet first, correct.
