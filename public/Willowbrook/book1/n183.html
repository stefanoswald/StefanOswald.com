Night 183

The Door Problem

Month 7: Explorers and Guardrails

Mama went into the bathroom and shut the door, and this time Olivia screamed.

Not the hungry cry. Not the tired one. A new one, aimed at the wood, and under it, plain as a sentence: I know you are in there.

In June a door closing had made Mama vanish, and a vanished thing is not worth crying for. Now Olivia had enough of the world in her to know Mama was continuing, on the other side, without her. Knowing that is the whole point of three months of dropped spoons and peekaboo, arriving in its harder half.

"I'm here," said Mama, through the door. "I'm brushing my teeth."

She kept talking. Teeth, the tap, the towel, the whole dull business narrated to a hallway. On the other side the crying thinned to a complaint, then to a listening. The door opened. Olivia was on her hands and knees at the threshold, wet-faced, and took hold of Mama's ankles with both hands as if they might leave again.

Say where you are going. Keep the voice coming through the wood. Come back. Every door in the house is a small practice.

By the end of the month the crying at the door was shorter. Not gone. Shorter.

Mama brushed her teeth with the door open anyway, most days.
