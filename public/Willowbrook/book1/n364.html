Night 364

The Candle

Month 12: The Brook Comes Unstuck

The match took, and the room went quiet the way a room does for a small flame.

Olivia looked at it. Mama had her hands in both of hers, which was the rule, and Olivia let them stay there, which was luck.

Then the song, in every voice in the room, the aunts high and Grandpa Joe under it somewhere, and at "dear Olivia" she looked round at all of them as if she had just been told her own name.

Papa blew it out. Her turn comes next year.

The cake went down on the tarp and she went down beside it, and put one fist in, and ate what came up, and then looked past it at the box the cake had come in.

The party ended when Mama said it would, an hour and a half. Jars of applesauce went out the door in every hand. Grandpa Joe went last, with the tenth balloon on its long string, for somebody at the farm who likes red.

Olivia was asleep on Papa's shoulder by then, in the sweater.

Mama went out onto the porch. The sun had come out on the wet boards, and on the rail, in the one warm patch, a butterfly, dark-winged, edged with pale gold, opened and closed. On the first of March.

When she looked again it was gone. The rail was warm.
