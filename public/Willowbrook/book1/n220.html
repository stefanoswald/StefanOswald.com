Night 220

The Food Strike

Month 8: The Velcro Weeks and the Leaves

In the third week the sweet potato was refused. Head turned, lips sealed, the plate looked at the way you look at a stranger's dog. The next night the scramble went the same way.

There were reasons. The whether and the how much were the only powers the high chair granted her, and she was using them because they were hers. New teeth were on the way, and sore gums want cold and soft. And the first year's headlong growth was easing on its own schedule, so the appetite was easing honestly with it.

The portions were honest too. A serving for her was about a tablespoon of each food. Eyes trained on grown-up plates reliably double the size of the crisis.

On the third night Mama caught herself with the spoon at Olivia's lips, pushing, and a voice coming out of her that said "Just one." She put the spoon down.

"Sorry," she said. "That's yours to decide." She moved the plate back to where it belonged, within reach and unmentioned, and ate her own dinner.

The plate is a standing offer, and the appetite is weather. Hold the offer and skip the fight.

The sweet potato sat on the tray, was not discussed, and left the table exactly as it had arrived. Olivia had eaten most of the bread.
