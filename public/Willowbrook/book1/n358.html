Night 358

Mama's Letter

Month 12: The Brook Comes Unstuck

The house was asleep on every floor, and Mama sat at the kitchen table with the lamp low and wrote the first one.

Dear Olivia,

You are asleep upstairs with both arms over your head, the way you have slept since the swaddle came off, and in a week you will be one. This goes in a drawer until you ask for it.

You came home in a cold rain. I did not know how to do any of it. Nobody does. I stood in this kitchen at three in the morning more times than I will admit to your father, sure I was getting it wrong, and sometimes I was, and I came back, and you let me, every time, as if nothing had happened. That is the most generous thing anyone has ever done for me, and you did it before you could sit up.

You smiled at me over a diaper on a Tuesday. You walked into my hands on a Wednesday. You said duck, and I wrote it down, and I have written everything down since.

Next week I will say goodbye to my baby and hello to whoever you are turning into. I have been waving at both of you all year.

Mama

She read it once, folded it, and put it in the top drawer of the hall desk, emptied that afternoon for it.
