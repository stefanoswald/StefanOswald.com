Night 313

The Weaning Page

Month 11: Mittens and the Early Dark

This page is quieter than its neighbors. It is for Mama, and for anyone who kept a baby fed this year, however it was done.

The dusk feed ran long that night. Nothing needed it to. The room was dim on purpose, as it has been since spring, and Olivia's hand opened and closed on the cardigan, and the old stare went up, the one that has no name.

The weaning was coming. Not yet. But it had earned a page.

Here is the advice. Twelve months opens a door and orders nothing through it. Nursing on past the birthday is fine. Winding down is fine. The baby's pace is fine, and so is the mother's. The only rule is gentleness, owed to the girl, and owed just as much to the mother, whose body has its own weather to cross.

And no guilt. Not an ounce. Bottle families read this page with a clear conscience, and so does everyone who stopped early, or never started. Fed is fed.

What does not retire is the lap. The dim room, the stare, the settling, all of it becomes the lap the stories get read from, for years.

The feed ended when it ended. Mama carried her to the crib, and came back, and sat a while in the chair with her arms empty and warm.
