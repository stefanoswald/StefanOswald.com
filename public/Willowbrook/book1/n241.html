Night 241

The Quiet Ten Minutes

Month 8: The Velcro Weeks and the Leaves

It is about ten minutes at the end of a day, and it may be the one the book most hopes gets kept.

An ordinary Thursday. Dinner done, the bath still ahead. Olivia at the basket, deep in the stacking rings, working for herself and needing nothing.

And Mama sat down on the floor against the couch, three feet off, and did not do anything. Not the phone. Not the folding. Not even the narration. Not the next thing.

Ten minutes in the same room as her child's play, watching it the way you would stand at a window in weather you love. Present, off duty, unneeded.

The year is a doing year, and the doing is the job. But doing has its own momentum, and a parent can spend a whole devoted day beside a child and never once simply be with her.

The ten minutes turned up this. The play went deeper when nobody helped: four full cycles of the rings, long chains of talk to herself. And near the end of it a ring was held up, not for help, for company. Shown to the watcher, because the watcher was there.

The ring was shown. Nothing else happened. The dusk managed itself.

The cat was asleep in the laundry basket, on the clean side.
