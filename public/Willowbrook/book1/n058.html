Night 58

One Photo, One Line

Month 2: Smiles and Sleep

On the porch steps, the first warm evening, Mama sent the aunts the month, one photo and one line each, Olivia in her lap.

The Tuesday smile, blurred, a second late. Aimed at me. The ceiling fan got the next one.

Papa and the baby nose to nose. Discussing the laundry. She won.

A bear in midair. He flies now.

Bathwater, mostly on the wall. She found the sea.

A library card, signed with a fist.

A bottle, shot from the hall. It worked once Mama left the room.

Asleep in the car seat, a bandage on one thigh. Two shots. Over by the parking lot.

Two hands holding each other. Introduced this week.

Half past six, in daylight. Six and a half hours. Nobody did anything.

"You left some out," said Papa.

"There aren't any of those."

He said them anyway. The day she missed every sign until the yawn and said sorry to a baby, and the one time in three. The night he was too loud and went out to the porch, and came back, and said sorry. The gray afternoon, and the true number.

"We know her now," Mama said.

"She knows us back," said Papa. "Send them that."

The string lights came on down Maple Lane. Olivia yawned at them. Bath was in ten minutes. It always was.
