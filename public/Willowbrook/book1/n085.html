Night 85

The Off Day

Month 3: Rhythm

Some days come with no headline. This was one.

Olivia woke fussy and stayed fussy. Not sick, no fever, feeding fine, the fridge list read twice. Not teething, probably. Just off: clingy, whimpery, refusing the mat and the bouncer, wanting only arms and the window and the slow bounce.

The plans died. The mother Mama had meant to be that day, showered by ten, went too.

At three o'clock, with the warm rain ticking on the glass, Mama heard her own voice say, "What do you WANT," in a tone she did not like. She laid Olivia in the crib, safe on her back, and drank a glass of water in the kitchen. Then she came back. "Sorry. Hello. I'm here."

Babies have off days, some small weather in the nervous system with no name and no fix but company. There is nothing to solve on such a day. The job is to lower the bar until it rests on the floor, and keep her company down there.

So they took the window and the slow bounce. An old song, hummed forty times. The cat sat on the sill and watched the rain with them, unimpressed by everybody.

By the bath, whatever it was had passed through, unexplained. Olivia kicked and grinned up from the water, forgiving everybody.

Mama, soaked to the elbows, forgave it back.
