Night 83

Bobby Bear's Second Letter

Month 3: Rhythm

On the dresser, across the room from the crib, the honey-brown bear kept the night watch. If bears could write, his second letter would go like this.

Dear Olivia. Three months into the job, and I have news.

You laughed. I was there. It was a Tuesday, and the belly kiss did it. I have heard many first laughs from many dressers, and I rank yours first.

You rolled. Twice the first day, and daily since, mostly toward things you are not supposed to have. The corners of the mat, formerly the edge of your known world, have fallen. Somebody had better clear the lower shelves.

You found your hands, and then you found that they obey you. Everything you will ever hold, every ball, every crayon, every hand, was promised this month by those two fists.

Your parents have steadied, in case you had not noticed from your altitude. They laugh more at midnight and check your breathing slightly less. That is what healing looks like on grown people.

As for me, the dresser smells of lilac now and the honor is total. They say that when you are one, I move in with you. I am practicing being soft.

Grow on, little one. I have the watch. B. Bear.

The moon leaned in at the open window and read the whole thing over his shoulder.
