Night 100

One Hundred Nights

Month 4: Rolling and Laughing

Papa had been keeping count on the kitchen calendar, one small pencil star each night, and tonight he drew the hundredth and stood back like a man who has finished a fence.

One hundred nights of a story read to somebody who could not follow a word of it. Some had been silk. Some had been sandpaper. A dozen had been abandoned mid-song for emergencies of digestion. He had drawn a star for those too, since the star was for turning up, not for finishing.

By now the order of things was nearly as old as she was. Bath, quiet, book, song, lights out. The same short hallway every night, ending in the same soft room. Olivia did not follow the story. She followed the voice, up its hills and down, and lately she turned toward it the way a plant turns toward a window.

Moonlight came through the leaves and lay over the ceiling in small pale coins, more than anyone cared to count.

"One hundred," Papa said, closing tonight's book.

"You'll still be counting at a thousand," Mama said.

"I'll still be counting," he agreed.

They stood over the crib longer than they needed to. Outside, Stella sat low over the roof, where she had been all hundred nights, and Maple Lane went on being an ordinary street with one lamp still burning on it.
