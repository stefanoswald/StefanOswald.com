Night 312

The Bottle's Notice

Month 11: Mittens and the Early Dark

The bottle is the oldest piece of equipment in the house. It ran the three a.m. kitchen. This winter it got its notice, gentle and long.

The plan is to wind bottles down somewhere between twelve and eighteen months, with the teeth in mind, once a cup is going well, and to do it slowly, one feed at a time, never all at once. So nothing has to end before the birthday, and nothing gets forced. The practice starts now.

The mid-morning feed was chosen because nobody is attached to it, not even Olivia. The same milk went into the cup instead of the bottle, at the same hour, on the same tray. She looked at the cup. She looked at Mama. She looked at the drying rack, where the bottles stood in a row, none of them coming.

The protest lasted four minutes, the going rate this winter. Then the cup was drunk from, badly, and then not badly.

The bedtime bottle keeps its place for now. It belongs to the quiet step of the routine and will get its own slow turn after the birthday. The one iron sentence stayed where it has been since the first tooth: never a bottle in the crib.

Mama rinsed the cup. On the rack the bottles dried in their row, one of them, for the first time, not needed today.
