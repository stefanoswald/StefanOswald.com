Night 145

The Kitchen Notebook

Month 5: Nearly Sitting, Nearly Everything

Week two, in Mama's hand: Growth spurt. Nobody warned us. We now warn everyone.

The notebook had started as a list of feeds in the blurred first weeks, written down because writing it down was the one thing they could do. Tonight, with Olivia asleep across his knees like a warm paperweight, Papa read it back.

Week five, in capitals, underlined through the paper: SHE SMILED.

Week six: Crying peaks now, the book says. The book is right. Tell the book.

Week ten: The real laugh. Papa takes credit.

Week fourteen: Nobody sleeps. Week fifteen: Ba. Still nobody sleeps. Week seventeen: Everybody sleeps.

Week twenty-one, his own, tonight: Under the coffee table. Indignant.

It is not the baby book with the doves on the cover. It is the working one, kept by the kettle, written in whichever hand was free. Its uses pile up. It cuts the fog, because when memory swears the last bad patch ran two months, the notebook knows it was three weeks. It catches patterns, like the fussy Thursdays traced, once, to Wednesday's late nap. It keeps the questions for the doctor. And it is the deep memory, since the first year erases itself as it goes, and this is the camera that catches the ordinary.

He wrote the entry. The paperweight sighed and held all the pages down at once.
