Night 110

Peekaboo, Version One

Month 4: Rolling and Laughing

The oldest game in the world came to Maple Lane in its earliest edition. Mama went behind the burp cloth and was gone, and the world was briefly short of its center.

Then, "Peekaboo!" and the world came back, smiling, right where it ought to be.

Olivia climbed the same ladder each go. The stare. The held breath. And at the reveal, the jolt of delight, joy with a jump in it.

Under the giggles, the cloth is teaching. At four months, gone is gone. A face that vanishes has been subtracted from the universe, and its return is news every time. The knowing that hidden things go on existing is under construction all this half of the year, and peekaboo is the scaffolding. Disappear, wait, return. A thousand gentle rehearsals of the shape that will someday hold at a daycare door, where leaving is only half of it.

Version one has three rules. Short hides. Big reveals. Stop while she is laughing. The later editions, where she pulls the cloth herself, hides herself, and finally hides a toy and finds it, would arrive over the coming months, each one a new switch thrown.

Six goes tonight. At the fourth, she reached up and yanked the cloth away early, unable to wait for the ending.

"Peekaboo," Mama conceded, found.

The center of the world laughed, and grabbed the cloth again.
