Night 307

The Dark at Four O'Clock

Month 11: Mittens and the Early Dark

In January the day quits at four. The light goes thin after lunch and is gone before the nap is properly over, and the streetlamps come on while Mama is still deciding what supper is.

It is a long stretch, the four-to-bath. Everybody who has kept a baby through a winter knows it by name. The morning had been spent outside on purpose, in whatever light there was, because the rule is to chase the daylight early and keep the two anchors of the day, the wake-up and the bedtime, where they were. The rule works. It does not shorten the four-to-bath by one minute.

On Thursday Mama snapped at a cupboard door, then at the cat, then, in a smaller voice, at Olivia, who had pulled the whole recycling out again. Then she stopped in the middle of the kitchen with her hand on the counter and let one slow breath go all the way out.

"That wasn't yours," she said, crouching. "I'm tired of the dark. Come and look at it with me."

At the window the lane was lighting up, porch by porch. Olivia's finger went to the glass, and each new light got a tap.

"Yes. There's Mrs. Henderson's. There's the corner one."

They stood until every porch was in. The recycling stayed on the floor. Outside, the corner lamp buzzed on last.
