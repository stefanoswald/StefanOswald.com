Night 361

The Party Eve

Month 12: The Brook Comes Unstuck

The last night of the first year was a Friday, and it was soup.

They ate at the table for three with the aunts, so it was a table for seven, and the aunts talked and Olivia banged the spoon and the soup went where soup goes. Then bath, and the yawn at the sound of the water, and the quiet, and the bear-and-button book, and the song, and lights out, in that order, since April. Nothing was different. That was on purpose.

Downstairs the cake was still at Rosa's, the candle was still in its box on the shelf, and the star sweater was folded on the nursery chair for the morning. The balloons were a promise at a stall.

Mama and Papa stood in the doorway.

"Last night of a baby," said Papa.

"Maybe," said Mama.

"Maybe?"

"She might stay a baby a while. I've heard they do."

"True number?"

"Tired. Happy. A bit like the night before a test."

"I never studied either."

He opened the window an inch, to let the room hear the brook. It was still going, out there in the dark, and would be all night.

They stayed a minute more than they needed. Then a second. Then they left the door open a hand's width, as always.
