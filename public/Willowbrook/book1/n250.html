Night 250

Hot

Month 9: One Step, One Word

Standing put a whole new shelf of the world in reach: the table's edge, the counter's first three inches, and the radiator, which had come on that week with a clank.

So the house moved up a shelf. Mugs went inland. Pot handles turned in. The knife block went to the far country of the counter. The radiator got its cover back, and the bedroom's little heater went to the closet, since a walker and a space heater cannot share a room. The shallow drawers she could reach now got latches, the toilet lid got one too, the bathroom shelf moved up, and the shoe pile by the door went flat, because a pile is a step. The standing rule: hot behind, sharp behind, near the stove.

But you cannot fence heat. So Mama taught the word.

The mug was warm, not scalding, tested on her wrist. The reaching hand came in, and Mama's voice went low and level: "Hot. That's hot."

The hand stopped. A small recoil, self-issued. The mug was studied from a new distance. Then a nod, grave, on both sides.

All week it spread. The radiator, approached, got a wide berth and a warning look.

Mama said it the same way to Papa on Thursday, about the pan, and he stopped too.

The kettle steamed on under its warning.
