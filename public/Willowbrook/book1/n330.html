Night 330

Oliver Owl's Almost-Question

Month 11: Mittens and the Early Dark

On the oldest branch of the Great Willow, a month before the candle, Oliver Owl settled his spectacles and looked down at the town.

"Did you see them today?" he asked. "The three who came up the hill?"

"I saw," said Stella. "The small one put her mitten on the bark."

"And what does a tree do with a mitten?"

"Keeps it," said Stella. "Along with everything else."

The Sleepy Moon hummed, low, the sound of somebody agreeing without opening its eyes.

Oliver was quiet for a while. An owl's quiet is long. "So here is my almost-question," he said. "A birthday is a candle and a cake. Does it close the book?"

"No," said Stella. "It turns the page. The morning after, she wakes up hungry, and the bear is allowed into the crib at last, and everybody starts finding out who she is at one."

"And the two who carried her up the hill? Will they miss the year?"

"Some of it," said Stella. "They can miss it and light the candle. Both fit."

The Moon hummed again, a little sadder this time, and a little pleased.

Oliver did not write anything down. Below them the nursery lamp went out, and the town was all snow and porch lights.
