Night 155

The Allergen Calendar

Month 6: The Feast Begins

When Mama was little, families were often told to delay foods such as peanut and egg. The guidance had changed, and there was a calendar on the fridge to prove it, one check per new food.

Once Olivia was developmentally ready for solids, usually around six months, common allergens could join the menu in small, infant-safe amounts. Delaying them did not prevent allergies and could increase the risk for some children. Because Olivia did not have severe eczema or a known food allergy, her pediatrician said they could begin at home. A baby with severe eczema, an egg allergy, or a previous reaction should have an individual plan from a pediatrician or allergist first.

They started in the morning with a little smooth peanut butter thinned into cereal. Whole nuts and thick spoonfuls stayed far away. Those were choking hazards. Another day brought well-cooked egg. Later came yogurt, wheat, sesame, soy, fish, and the rest, one new food at a time in textures Olivia could safely manage.

Mama watched for hives, swelling, repeated vomiting, coughing, wheezing, or trouble breathing. Breathing trouble meant emergency help. Foods that went well returned to the menu regularly instead of disappearing after one taste.

Peanut day passed without drama. Mama made a check on the calendar.

Olivia wore a little on her cheek and opened her mouth for more.
