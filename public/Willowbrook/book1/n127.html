Night 127

Sunday at the Fence Line

Month 5: Nearly Sitting, Nearly Everything

Every Sunday now: the drive out, the too-much lunch, and Grandpa Joe's walk along the fence line, this summer with Olivia on his arm.

The chickens met them at the wire and the introductions ran formal.

"That's Henrietta. Don't lend her money."

Olivia stared at Henrietta with the entire front of her head.

"The speckled one's the Duchess. The Duchess situation is complicated."

Each bird got considered at length by Olivia, whose whole reply was two pumping arms and one long vowel.

A farm in July is the loudest, best-smelling room there is, and it has no walls. Cicadas in the hedge. The tractor got patted, warm from the sun like a big red animal that had lain down.

Under all of it ran the old man's voice, slow, narrating the world at a walking pace. Mama's voice was music and Papa's was a cello, and this was a third instrument, and hers to keep. What a farm hands a baby is mostly this: talk poured out slowly, and new things at a dose that feeds and does not flood.

At the corner post her head went heavy on the flannel shoulder.

"That's enough politics for one Sunday," Grandpa Joe told the chickens, who disagreed at volume.

He walked back the long way, and she slept the whole slow way against his collar.
