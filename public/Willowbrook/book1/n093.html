Night 93

The Regression That Is Progress

Month 4: Rolling and Laughing

It came in like weather off the sea. Olivia, who had found her long stretch and kept it for weeks, was up at eleven. Then at half past one. Then at three, wide-eyed in the crib at the foot of the bed, ready to discuss the day.

People call it the four-month sleep regression. It is really a rebuilding. Around now a baby's sleep takes on its grown-up shape, light stretches and deep ones, with a little surfacing between. Grown-ups surface too, check that the pillow is still a pillow, and sink back without remembering. A baby surfacing asks one thing: is everything how it was when I fell asleep? If she went down in arms and woke in a crib, it is not, and she says so.

So the house went back to the old habits. Down drowsy but awake, whenever that worked. When it did not, a hand on her chest, a low voice, no lights, no games, and back out. Boring nights, on purpose.

At three, Papa mumbled "want me to," and Mama said "I've got it" with an edge on it. She stood over the crib and took one slow breath before reaching in. "Sorry," she said toward the bed. "Not you. The clock."

Three weeks, this one took. On the far side, a sleeper with grown-up shape and the same cheeks.
