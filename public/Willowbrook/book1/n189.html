Night 189

Harry in the Leaf Pile

Month 7: Explorers and Guardrails

The big oak in the backyard let go of its first leaves in the windy week, not many, enough for a pile if you were not fussy about the pile. Papa raked it on Saturday. Olivia sat on the blanket at the edge of the yard with a leaf in each fist and supervised.

The pile was as tall as the rake when it rustled. Out came a hedgehog.

Caramel, round, a yellow leaf riding his back like a small sail, on some urgent errand, and he stopped four inches from a baby's nose. Both of them froze. Olivia looked at Mama. Then back at the hedgehog. Then her arm went out, slowly, as it had at the dog.

The hedgehog thought about rolling up. You could see him think it. Then he looked at the blanket, and the large slow people, and the small loud one, and decided, this being Willowbrook, that she was weather and not a threat. He went on across the yard with the leaf aboard and under the fence, and the arm followed him all the way.

"Hedgehog," said Mama. "Harry," she added, without deciding to, since that is how names arrive in that town.

The pile was left where it was, in case he wanted it.

On Monday the leaf was seen from the window, still on him, going the other way.
