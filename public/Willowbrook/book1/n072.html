Night 72

Thumb or Silicone

Month 3: Rhythm

The great debate reached the little house on Maple Lane. The thumb or the pacifier? The loud aunts of the world have opinions on both.

Olivia, who had read none of it, had lately discovered her thumb, while staying loyal to the pacifier at sleep times.

The house takes the calm position. Sucking is not a vice. It is one of the first tools a baby owns for settling herself, and she practiced it in the womb, before anyone offered her anything.

The pacifier at sleep times carries a quiet bonus in the first months, a small extra safety margin the sleep doctors nod at, with the usual manners: offered once feeding runs smooth, never tethered to strings or clips in bed, never dipped in anything, retired without drama if it starts costing more wake-ups than it saves. Putting it back in at three in the morning is a chauffeur job, and chauffeur jobs end careers.

The thumb needs no putting back and cannot fall on the floor. If it costs anything, the bill comes years later, at the dentist. Neither one is a disaster.

Comfort is the point, and nearly all of it is outgrown before anyone is old enough to be embarrassed.

Olivia slept with the pacifier in and the thumb in reserve, resting by her cheek like a spare key.
