Night 334

Stella at the Sill

Month 11: Mittens and the Early Dark

On the last night of January, at the hour the town turns its lights down to embers, Stella came low.

Stars mostly keep their distance. They read a window from the willow branch. But it had been a long month of snow, and she came down until her gold touched the frost on the nursery sill and turned it silver, fern by fern.

Inside, the crib stood at its lowest setting. The bear sat on the shelf across from it, out of reach, not in, one ear down. The sleeper lay on her back with her arms flung wide, one fist caught in the seam of the sleep sack, breathing the deep breath of somebody who has been pulled round a yard on a sled.

Stella did not say much. Stars say things through glass in a way nobody has ever caught.

"I saw you let go," she said. "I saw the two steps. I'll be here for the rest of them."

The fist opened on something in a dream, and closed.

Stella twinkled once, for yes, and rose back to where a star belongs.

Above the roofs the Sleepy Moon had watched the whole visit with its eyes shut, and hummed.

The frost on the sill stayed silver a while after she had gone.
