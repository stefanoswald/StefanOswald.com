Night 80

The First Cold

Month 3: Rhythm

Olivia's first cold arrived with a stuffy nose, a small cough, and two parents who checked the thermometer three times.

The refrigerator list helped. For a baby three months old or younger, a rectal temperature of 100.4 degrees Fahrenheit, 38 Celsius, or higher means calling the pediatrician immediately. For an older baby, the right number for a call depends on age and the doctor's guidance. The thermometer matters, and so does the baby in front of you.

Mama and Papa also watched for hard or fast breathing, bluish lips, unusual sleepiness, trouble waking, repeated vomiting, poor feeding, or too few wet diapers. Any of those meant calling promptly. So did the quiet inner alarm that said something was not right.

Olivia had none of those signs. She was uncomfortable but alert, drinking milk and protesting every nose wipe with impressive strength. Saline drops, gentle suction, extra feeds, upright cuddles, and a clean cool-mist humidifier made the hours easier. Honey would wait until after her first birthday.

By the third night, she could breathe through her nose again. Papa slept beside the crib anyway, just to hear the soft, ordinary rhythm return.
