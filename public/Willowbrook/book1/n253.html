Night 253

The Last Frog

Month 9: One Step, One Word

The Saturday walk reached the brook, hand in hand the whole way down. The water was grey and quick and full of leaves going somewhere. Papa picked up a pebble and tossed it in.

Plip. A ring. Then the finger, and the look back up at him.

Again, said the look. He obliged. Again. Again. Papa understood that he would be doing this until spring.

Her hand stayed in his at the edge the whole time, and the reach rule ran under everything: a baby near water is never farther than an arm, brook or bath, at any depth, for any reason, and the person whose arm it is knows it is their turn.

On a wet stone downstream sat Freddy Frog, lily-pad hat pulled low. "THE SPLASH IS THE BEST PART," he announced to the water at large. "I HAVE ALWAYS SAID SO." Then, more quietly, that he was going down into the mud for the winter and would be back for the peepers, and that the pebble work should continue in his absence.

Papa heard a frog. Olivia looked at the stone for a long time.

The brook took the leaves and the pebbles and went on.

On the way home the stone was empty.
