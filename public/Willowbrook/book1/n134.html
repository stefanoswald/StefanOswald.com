Night 134

Dinner Theater

Month 5: Nearly Sitting, Nearly Everything

Olivia had a spoon of her own now, loaded with nothing, and she conducted with it from the high chair.

Dinner had turned into theater, and in July the audience joined the cast. Talk went across the table and the spoon followed it, waving at each speaker in turn. When laughter broke out she joined at volume, cause unknown, timing perfect.

When a lull fell, she filled it. "Ba-ba-ba. Ma-ma-ba." Delivered like a toast.

The babble had gone social. No longer practiced on the lamp alone, but aimed into the gaps of the table. She had the tune of a conversation before any of its words: the pause that means your turn, the rising note that asks, the beat where the joke goes.

The table's job is to leave her the pauses, answer the toasts, and let her hold the floor when she takes it.

"Ba?" said Papa. "A bold position. Papa disagrees."

"Ba-ba," said Olivia, louder.

"She's got you there," Mama said.

Food stayed the subplot. Tonight's potatoes were tracked from bowl to fork to mouth with a reach that never landed. Milk was still all of dinner. The feast was a month off, close enough to smell.

At the end of the meal the spoon hit the tray with a bang that called curtain, and Papa, who had been waiting all evening, bowed.
