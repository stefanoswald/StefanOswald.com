Night 84

The Saturday Adventure

Month 3: Rhythm

Saturday morning belonged to the farmers market, and this Saturday the stalls got a new critic.

Papa wore Olivia through them in the carrier, close enough to kiss, her face out for stretches now that her head could hold itself up and swivel at will. And swivel it did. The bunches of radishes got a long look. The flower stall, all lilac this week, got a longer one. The bell on the fish stall, the bread smell, the general roar of neighbors selling to neighbors, all of it went straight into a brain doing its favorite thing, which is everything at once.

For a baby, an outing like this is not errands. It is the opera. New light, new faces at every distance, a hundred sounds layered up, and like any rich meal, best in sensible portions.

The one rule Papa carried was to watch for the signs of enough. The stare going glassy. The fuss climbing. The head burying itself in his chest, the critic calling for intermission. Then a quiet side street, a swaying walk, the show turned down to one rumbling voice and a heartbeat.

Forty minutes was the number today. Papa whistled a little on the way home, and the fiddler at the corner stopped to check his strings.

Olivia went under before the end of the street, overcome by the arts, one radish richer.
