Night 30

One Month Old Tonight

Month 1: Arrival

One month. Thirty days, or was it three hundred? They marked it with a candle stuck in a muffin, blown out by Papa on Olivia's behalf, since her only party trick was hiccups.

Then Papa held her up at the window and told her the story of her month, and got most of it wrong on purpose.

"First you came home and slept through the night." "She has never once slept through the night," Mama said. "First you came home and nobody knew which cry was which. Then we did, mostly." True. The hungry cry short, the tired one whimpery, the rest read like weather maps.

"Then you found Mama's face and held on to it with your eyes." True. "Then you heard your name and stopped crying." "Sometimes," Mama said. "Sometimes counts. Then you gained a pound." "A pound of pure future," said Mama, who had been saving it.

He left some out. A month ago neither of them could fold a swaddle. The shifts that ran like a small railroad. The whistling, which had not improved. The night that was terrible at four, and the five o'clock after it, when they were somehow still kind to each other.

The rain had quit. The dusk went gold, then blue. The first star came out over the roof, the same one that had found the window a month ago.
