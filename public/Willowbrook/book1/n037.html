Night 37

The Evening Buffet

Month 2: Smiles and Sleep

From five o'clock on, Olivia wanted dinner. Then a second dinner. Then a small third dinner, a snack, and dinner again.

Cluster feeding, the books call it. Grandpa Joe's name for it was stocking the cellar, and it made more sense: little meals packed close together all evening, like laying in wood before a cold night. Babies who fill up at dusk often pay it back with their longest sleep of the night afterward. It is not a milk shortage, and it is not fussing for nothing. It is a small mammal getting ready for the dark, as small mammals always have.

So the family stopped fighting five o'clock and furnished it instead. Mama took the corner chair with the good pillow, feet up, water glass in reach, phone charger in reach, everything in reach. Papa ran the kitchen and brought her dinner a plate at a time, matching Olivia course for course. From the doorway it looked like a very small restaurant with one table and one regular.

"She's on the cheese course," said Papa.

"She's on the everything course," said Mama.

Around nine the regular was done. Milk-drunk, chin shining, one hand open on Mama's collar. Papa lifted her off and carried her to the crib, and the house went quiet for four hours straight.
