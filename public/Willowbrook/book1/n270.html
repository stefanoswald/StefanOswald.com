Night 270

Harry Goes Under

Month 9: One Step, One Word

The leaf pile by the back fence had been left standing since September, on purpose, and on the last cold Tuesday of the month the purpose showed up.

Dusk, the yard already blue, Papa carrying her out to bring in the wagon. At the edge of the pile, small and prickly and slow, a hedgehog, with one leaf stuck on his back.

Papa stopped. She had already seen him. The finger went out. The look came back up: is this allowed?

"Hedgehog," said Papa, very quietly. "He's going to bed. For the whole winter."

Harry looked at the baby for a while. "Somebody left this," he said, to her only, in the small voice grown-ups never hear. "I'd have managed on my own. But it's a good pile."

He went in as a hedgehog goes into leaves, slowly and then all at once, and the pile settled over him, and the leaf on his back was the last of him to go.

"Bye," said Papa, to the pile. Olivia waved at the pile. Then she waved at the wagon, in case.

The pile will stay until spring and not be jumped in, which will be hard by March.

The first stars were out over the bare maple. They stood there until the cold found her ears.

The wagon stayed out one more night.
