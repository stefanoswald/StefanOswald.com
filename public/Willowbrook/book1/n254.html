Night 254

The Garden Put to Bed

Month 9: One Step, One Word

The frost had finished the tomatoes on Tuesday night, and by Saturday the vines hung black off their stakes like wet string. The garden had to be put to bed.

So Olivia was put in the corner of it, dressed for ruin, in a heap of leaves raked there for the purpose, and the work went on around her at whatever pace her help allowed.

The vines came out, and she was handed one, and held it up to the sky, then to her mouth, and it was taken away. The stakes came out of the ground with a sucking sound she found excellent. The leaves went into both fists and came out in pieces. One was tasted. The face was long.

Under the last vine Mama found the last tomato, small and green and hard as a stone, and gave it to her, and she carried it all afternoon in one fist.

Then the straw, forked over the bed, the leaves raked in on top, and Mama patted it down. "Night night, garden." Olivia patted it too, the way she pats things.

Mama said next year she would plant less. She says this every November, and it is written nowhere.

The tomato went in the bath, and up to bed.

It sat on the dresser by the bear, going nowhere.
