Night 104

An Elephant, Briefly

Month 4: Rolling and Laughing

Sunday at Honeywell Farm ran its usual round. Chickens viewed. Tractor patted. Grandpa Joe's straw hat borrowed for one photograph and returned with drool on the brim. Then, at the far fence, the Sunday went somewhere no Sunday had gone before.

Beyond the back pasture, where the willows start, something large and gentle moved through the gold light. Grey-blue. Unhurried. Ears like patient flags. And, Papa squinted, a red balloon tied to its tail, riding the evening air.

An elephant. Behind Honeywell Farm.

Nobody at the farm found this remarkable. Grandpa Joe, asked, said, "That'd be Ellie," in the voice he uses for weather and second cousins, and went on with the gate.

Olivia, from the carrier on Papa's chest, stared with everything she had. The great head swung toward her across the distance. Something passed between them that Papa was not part of, and Ellie dipped her trunk once, like a small bow, the balloon bobbing, before the willows folded her back in.

Babies this age cannot say what they see, and Willowbrook has always quietly relied on it.

"You saw that," Papa said. It was not quite a question.

"Ba," said Olivia, firmly, which meant yes.

The balloon stayed visible over the willows a while, going home. Somewhere over the barn, a star came out early to see it off.
