Night 255

The Worm

Month 9: One Step, One Word

Turning the last corner of the bed for the straw, Mama's fork brought one up: a worm, pink and slow, out in the cold air with no opinion about it.

The crouch came first, the deep squat of the walking era, the best looking posture she owns. Then the finger, at four inches. Then the look back up: are you seeing this, what is this, may I.

"A worm," said Mama. "Gentle."

The watching lasted longest, the worm's whole slow traverse tracked inch by inch, a way of moving with no legs in it. Then the finger. One finger, laid on the worm as she had laid it on the hen's back and the cat's ear, the fence-line lesson of the whole year: the finger, not the fist. The worm did not care for it and said so by going under.

"Bye, worm," said Mama. The wave was issued at the place where the worm had been.

Every small thing she met that week got the same. The woodlouse under the stake rolled up, was pronounced broken, unrolled, and got the wave as well.

"Duh," she reported at lunch, the entire morning in it.

"You met a worm," said Mama, building it out. "He was going home. You used one finger."

The bed got its straw. The worm, somewhere under it, got the winter.
