Night 66

Grip

Month 3: Rhythm

The rattle was offered. The rattle was seized.

Not the old newborn grab, that automatic curl around anything that touched the palm. This was on purpose. The fingers opened in advance, aimed, and closed around the wooden ring with the satisfaction of a door latching.

Hers now. All things were hers now, potentially, and all things had one destiny, which was the mouth. The mouth is not a bad habit. At this age it is the best instrument she owns for finding out what a thing is. Lips and tongue read finer detail than fingertips for now, so everything she can hold goes in to be tested for texture, temperature, chewability, and general character.

The grown-up job is not to stop the testing but to choose what gets tested: big enough to never swallow (the toilet-paper-tube test, through the tube means too small), clean enough, nothing with button batteries or loose parts, ever. Wooden rings, silicone leaves, cloth blocks, the eternal burp cloth. All tried. All found delicious.

Olivia gummed the rattle with the focus of a jeweler.

"Any good?" Papa asked from the doorway.

She shook it. It rattled. She startled magnificently, recovered, and shook it again, on purpose this time. Cause, meet effect.

She was still shaking it when the light went orange.
