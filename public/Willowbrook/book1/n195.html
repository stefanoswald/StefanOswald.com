Night 195

The Video Call Grandfather

Month 7: Explorers and Guardrails

The rain kept them off the farm road on Sunday, and so the farm came to them, propped against the fruit bowl: Grandpa Joe in his kitchen, straw hat on indoors as ever, filling the screen like weather.

There are no screens for her at this age, with one exception, and this is it. A show does not answer. A grandfather does. He looks where she looks, waits for her sound, makes his, and the game the family has played since her first smile goes on down the wire, only slower, and with more hat.

He did not say much. He never does. He held up Henrietta, who was unimpressed by broadcast. He held up an egg. He turned the tablet slowly round his kitchen, the stove, the window, the dog asleep under the table, and turned it back, and looked at her, and waited.

She looked at Mama. Then at him. Then she put her flat palm on his flannel chest, on the glass, and left it there.

He put his own hand up to hers on his side, big as a plate, and the two hands stayed like that a while, forty miles apart.

"Well now," he said, at the end, which in Grandpa Joe means: seen you. Sundays soon.

The screen went dark. She patted it twice more, in case.
