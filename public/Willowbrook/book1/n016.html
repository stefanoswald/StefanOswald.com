Night 16

What the Doctor Said

Month 1: Arrival

At the checkup, everything about Olivia turned into a number. Ounces, inches, the distance around her marvelous head. Every number lived on a curve, and the curve lived in a folder, and Mama looked at the folder as if it were her own exam results.

"Don't marry the percentiles," the doctor said. "A small baby growing along her own curve is doing every bit as well as a big one."

Mama unfolded her list of questions. She had written them down at home, because questions evaporate the moment a doctor walks into a room. How many wet diapers? Six or more a day meant plenty of milk. That rattly newborn sneeze? Normal. The flaky skin, the eyes that crossed, the hiccups that shook her like an engine? Normal, normal, normal. Papa asked whether the noise she made in her sleep, like a pigeon in a drainpipe, was normal. It was.

There is no foolish question in that room. Pediatricians have heard everything, twice, before lunch.

Back home, out of her hat and both blankets, Olivia slept off the indignity of the scale, one fist by her ear.
