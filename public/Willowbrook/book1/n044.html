Night 44

The Narrator

Month 2: Smiles and Sleep

"Now we open the refrigerator, cold, cold. Here is a red apple, round red apple. Now we put it back, because we came for the butter."

Mama had been talking like this since breakfast, to one listener, and the listener had not looked away once.

Talking to a baby feels foolish at first, a speech to someone who answers in vowels. But this is how words arrive, and there is no other door. Thousands of ordinary sentences poured over ordinary things, sock, water, up we go, warm, until the words and the world grow together. She will gather every syllable for months before she spends one.

And the voice parents fall into without deciding to, higher, slower, stretched long on the vowels, is not baby talk. It is real speech with a tune in it, and babies turn toward it as they turn toward a window. Mama used to make fun of people for it. Mama now did it in the cereal aisle.

"And now the thrilling conclusion. Butter, located. Butter, on the counter."

"Ooh," said Olivia, right on the beat.

Papa, from the hall: "Is she asleep?"

"She's waiting for the butter."

Olivia's eyes stayed on Mama's mouth, watching the words come out of it one at a time, like a slow magic trick.
