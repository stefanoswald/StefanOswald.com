Night 344

The Plant-House Party

Month 12: The Brook Comes Unstuck

The plant house had its own way of doing a birthday, worked out over years and a great many one-year-olds, and it was small on purpose.

One cupcake, the no-honey kind, no candle. "Candles are for home," said the woman who ran it, who had a rule for most things and this one written on the wall. One paper crown, which Olivia wore for the time it takes to lift a hand. One song, sung by five grown-ups and nobody else, since the other four had cupcakes and were busy.

Olivia looked at her cupcake. She looked at the four others eating theirs with the total attention of people who have never been given a cupcake before. Then she put both hands into her own, and it was, by the look of her, a good decision.

Then she wanted the box it had come in. The box was better. The box had a lid.

Mama came at four, and heard about it at the door, with a wet cloth.

"Small," said the woman. "Small is the size they like. The big ones are for the grown-ups, and that's allowed too."

The crown rode home in the diaper bag. The box rode home in Olivia's lap, with the lid on and off and on, and was still going when they turned onto Maple Lane.
