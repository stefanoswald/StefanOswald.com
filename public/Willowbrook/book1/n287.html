Night 287

The Laundry Apprentice

Month 10: First Snow

The dryer is the warmest place in the house in December, and the load that comes out of it is the best thing before lunch.

Olivia has helped since the cloth on the coffee table. This month she took on her first job with a machine.

The handing up came first. She sat in the basket herself, in the warm, and passed the load to Mama one piece at a time. The small freight of a sock. The whole-body wrestle of a towel. A pillowcase, worn briefly, as a cape.

The fleece crackled when it came apart, tik, tik, and one sock rose up on its own and stuck to her hair, and stayed there, and she did not know.

Then the sorting. "Find the socks." One found. Held up.

"You found it," said Mama. "Up it goes."

And then the delivery, which was the whole point of the job. Her own drawer, the lowest one, the one that shoves shut. The sock carried across the nursery two-fisted, put in, and the drawer shoved with both hands and the shoulder, and leaned on, in case.

It took twenty minutes to do a six-minute job. Mama did not look at the clock, and that is the trick.

The sock in her hair rode along all morning. Nobody told her.
