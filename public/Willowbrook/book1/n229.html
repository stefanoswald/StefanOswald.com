Night 229

The Dancing Era

Month 8: The Velcro Weeks and the Leaves

Music had been on in the house since the singing rule of the second month, and the kitchen radio had run all summer. The eighth month brought the body into it.

It began at the couch. The music started, and the knees answered: down-up, down-up, deep, roughly on the beat and entirely on purpose, her face wearing the inward look of a body finding out that it is an instrument too. Rhythm is the third thing the year teaches, under the words and under the gestures. The baby who once settled to the swaying walk had gone from being danced to dancing.

Mama and Papa obliged, nightly and badly, in the lamplight behind her. Papa whistled along with the radio. The radio did not deserve it. Mama did the dance she had done at weddings since she was nineteen, which has one move. Olivia held the couch and bounced through all of it, judge and orchestra both.

The set list closed each night with the same slow song, the one Papa sang with his own words in it. The bounce eased to a sway. The dancer was collected off the couch mid-nod and carried toward the sound of the bath running.

Down-up, went the knees, on the way down the hall, out of habit. Down-up. Down.
