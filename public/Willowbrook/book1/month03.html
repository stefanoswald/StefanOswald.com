Month 3 · Rhythm

For the grown-up

Nights 60–90

This is the month the days get a shape. Not a schedule, just a rough order that mostly repeats. You will notice it the first evening you get to the end of a sentence.

She is growing hands. Somewhere in here the waving turns into aiming, and she swats the thing above her on purpose, then holds it, then puts it in her mouth, where everything goes now. She is growing a laugh, if it has not come already, a neck that turns toward a sound and holds, and maybe a roll.

The stories go outside now that May allows it, to the park bench and the other tired grown-ups on it, and to the first practice goodbye. Some of those pages are about you.

Read them the way you would hum. She is listening to the tune of your voice, not the words, so the same page for a week is fine. Do not ask her anything yet. Make the sounds the story makes and let her answer with whatever she has, which might be a laugh.

If you try one thing, make it one feed a day with the phone face down and your eyes available. That is the whole exercise.

The thing not to worry about is the baby on the internet who rolled both ways at nine weeks. Milestones are windows, not deadlines. Good enough is a real standard, the one she was built for.
