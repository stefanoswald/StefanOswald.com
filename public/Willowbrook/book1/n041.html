Night 41

Bathtime Changes Its Mind

Month 2: Smiles and Sleep

The belly button had healed and the sponge days were over. Olivia lay back in her little sloped tub in two inches of real water. Two inches was plenty. Elbow-warm, tested twice. A wrist works, an elbow works better, and a bath thermometer works for people who like numbers: comfortably warm, never hot. Towel, washcloth, diaper and pajamas laid out in reach before the water went in, because nothing gets fetched once the baby is in the tub.

Three baths running, she had been against it.

Tonight, mid-complaint, her own hand hit the water. Splat. She stopped. She did it again. Splat. SPLAT. A loud sea from the waist down, and it was hers.

Mama laughed, and kept one hand under the small back the whole time. That is the one bath rule with no exceptions. A hand on the baby, always, and never a step away, not for the phone, not for the doorbell, not for three seconds. If something must be fetched after all, the baby comes out first, wrapped in the towel, and comes too. Baths are lovely and water is serious, and both are true at once.

The washing took ninety seconds. The splashing took a good deal longer, by request. Then the warm towel, hood up, and out came a small pink person with two wet fists, blinking, dripping on Mama's shoulder.
