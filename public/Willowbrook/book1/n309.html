Night 309

The Runner

Month 11: Mittens and the Early Dark

Cruising had been careful all autumn: two hands, a sideways shuffle, a look down at the feet. This month it stopped being careful.

One hand now, the other out for balance or for pointing at the lamp, which at this age is the same job. The length of the couch went by in four steps and a skid. Then the gap to the coffee table, crossed by leaning and hoping. She was not always right about it.

Standing alone was old news. She could stand in the middle of the rug for as long as a song, arms out. The new thing was what she did with it.

Papa knelt at the far end with his arms open. "Come on. Come on, Olivia."

She let go of the couch. Stood. A wobble that went all the way up, and then two whole steps into open air before her bottom found the floor with a thump.

"You let go!" said Papa. "Two steps, and you went."

She sat where she had landed and looked at her feet as if they had done it without her. Then she crawled the rest of the way, fast, because the point was Papa, not the walking.

The coffee-table corners kept their foam. The falls were let happen, from a height of one baby.

Papa's knees gave out before her interest did.
