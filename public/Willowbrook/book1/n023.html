Night 23

The First Walk Outside

Month 1: Arrival

In the third week, the front door opened outward for a change, and the family went out to meet the weather, which was March, and undecided. Not far. To the corner and back, the stroller wheels ticking over the sidewalk seams like a slow clock, and around the puddles. The bag under the stroller held four diapers, two outfits, a blanket, a hat, a second hat, and a bottle of water, for a trip of two hundred yards. Papa had packed it. Papa would have packed a flare.

Cold air on new cheeks. Real daylight, which tunes a baby's clock better than any lamp, and lifts a shut-in parent like bread rising. The rule for dressing her rode along too: one more layer than you would wear yourself, a hat for the wind, and now and then a hand slipped down the back of her collar. A warm chest means all is well, never mind cool little hands.

From the corner they could hear the brook, running high with the melt. Neighbors waved from their porches and stayed on them. Willowbrook knows how to greet a new baby: gently, and from a respectful distance. Mrs. Henderson called "Oh, look at her" from across the street and did not cross it.

Olivia slept through her whole debut, one hand out of the blanket, testing the air.
