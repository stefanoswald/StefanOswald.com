Night 89

The Dates Inside the Cover

Month 3: Rhythm

Papa had the board book open on his knee, at the inside of the cover, where the pencil dates had piled up since the laugh.

"Tuesday," he read. "Laughed. Belly kiss."

"You tried a goose afterward," Mama said. "She was not interested in the goose."

"Rolled. Swaddle retired. Reached the star. Bit the rabbit. Slept with her arms up like she'd won something."

Olivia slept in the crook of Mama's arm, one fist closed on the sleeve.

Some things had no date. The days had grown a shape, and the naps had names. There was a bench at the park with Rosa on it, Thursdays. One book every night. One feed every dusk with the phone turned over. Crying no longer frightened them, because it was a language now and they could get by in it. The grey day had come and gone, and good enough had been the standard, and the real one.

"Farm," Papa read. "Chickens. Anytime."

Mama added what no pencil had caught. On some hard four in the morning, each of them had seen the other be kinder than the hour required, and had kept it as the thing now certain about the person they had married.

Papa closed the book. Mama stood and carried the whole subject to the crib, where the fist let go of the sleeve in its own time.
