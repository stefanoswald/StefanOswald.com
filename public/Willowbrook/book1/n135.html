Night 135

The Small Hours Return

Month 5: Nearly Sitting, Nearly Everything

Four o'clock, mostly. The nights had been civilized for weeks, and then the small hours opened again.

The cry had no story in it. Just a baby surfaced in the crib at the foot of the bed, finding the surfacing lonely. Reasons enough: sitting practice firing through sleeping muscles, since new skills rehearse at night, the gums, a growth gathering.

They met it with the settled rule. First, the pause. A minute of listening before moving, since a surfacing often sinks on its own, and a baby rescued mid-resettle is a resettle undone. Then, if the cry gathered instead of fading, the low lamp, the hand on her chest, the shush from the doorway. The feed held in reserve, offered when the cry said feed and not when it merely said something.

And the older rule, the one for the parents: no adding up at four a.m. No forecasting the ruin of all future sleep from one loud week.

On Wednesday Papa broke it. "This is what it's going to be now," he told the ceiling at ten past four, and Mama said nothing back, with some force.

At twenty to five he said, "Sorry. I don't know that. Nobody knows that."

"No," she said. "Go to sleep. It's a week."

It was a week. They end. By morning neither of them could have said which visit had been which.
