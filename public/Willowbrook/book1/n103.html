Night 103

Hats and the Summer Sun

Month 4: Rolling and Laughing

June came to Willowbrook in its brightest clothes, and Olivia's first hot season came with a short plan.

Babies under six months belong in the shade, especially through the strong middle hours of the day. So Mama raised the stroller canopy, picked the biggest tree in Dandelion Park for the blanket, and dressed Olivia in thin clothes that covered her arms and legs. A wide-brimmed hat went on to shade her face and neck.

The hat lasted eleven seconds.

On, off, tasted, overboard. Mama put it back without a word, so it would not become a game. Off again. Back on. Mama wore her own hat too, on the theory that a thing everyone wears is not worth a fight. By the fifth round Olivia had decided a hat was bearable if the duck sat on the blanket where she could keep an eye on it.

Shade and clothing did most of the work. Where a small patch of skin could not be covered, the backs of the hands, say, a little baby-safe sunscreen went on that spot and nowhere else. Mama offered extra milk in the heat, more than usual, and watched for flushed skin or a sleepiness that did not look like ordinary sleep.

Under the oak the leaf-shadow moved across the blanket, and Olivia lay watching it go, hat on, duck beside her.
