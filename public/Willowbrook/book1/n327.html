Night 327

The Table for Three

Month 11: Mittens and the Early Dark

An ordinary supper, a Wednesday, the soup on its third night. No occasion, and here it is anyway.

The table is the longest-running thing in this book. It started with a bouncer in the corner in May and it has run every night since: the first sweet potato, the food thrown overboard for science, the nights when the whole meal was one cracker. Now she sits at her own height with what the grown-ups have, cut small, and takes part.

Taking part looks like this. Bread in each fist. A carrot offered to Papa and taken back. "Duh" at the salt shaker, which is not a duck and is not going to be told.

Papa's phone was on the table, and he was on it. Olivia held the carrot out to him twice and got nothing back. The third time she dropped it over the side and looked at the floor.

He noticed the quiet before he noticed the carrot. Phone face down. "Sorry. I went somewhere. I'm back." He picked the carrot up and offered it, and she took it, and offered it back, and this time he ate it.

Being at the table is the point. Three heights, one pot, the day's news in three languages.

The bowls were stacked. The tray went to the sink with its geography.
