Night 119

Once There Was a Girl

Month 4: Rolling and Laughing

On the porch swing, Olivia between them, Papa told her the month in the low voice, the one under the words.

"Once there was a girl who found her feet," he said. "This is how."

Chapter one. A cushion, a father behind it, a laugh with her whole body in it. Four times.

Chapter two. She rolled both ways, and her father crawled after her and found a button under the couch.

Chapter three. Nobody slept. Three weeks, which is long for a chapter. Then everybody did.

Chapter four. She caught her feet and ate part of one.

Chapter five. Potatoes went past her chair. Milk was still all of dinner.

Chapter six. Ba, to the lamp, and her mother said it back until the soup went cold.

Seven, a duck that would not stay down. Eight, an elephant, briefly. Nine, a grandfather checking a car seat strap for a quarter mile. Ten, turnips, in the voice of a cello.

"Chapter eleven," said Mama. "Her parents were shift workers and sweethearts in one fortnight."

The chapter nobody read aloud: she had begun to be somebody. Preferences. A taste in jokes. A way of greeting the duck.

Fireflies came up out of the grass. Olivia reached for every one, missed magnificently, reached again. Nobody went in until the last spark went over the hedge.
