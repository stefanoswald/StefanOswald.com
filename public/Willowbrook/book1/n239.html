Night 239

The Last Picnic

Month 8: The Velcro Weeks and the Leaves

The bench and the blanket, fixtures both since the summer, got one more afternoon out of the year on the last warm Saturday of October. Two families, one big blanket under the oak, the thermoses full of something hot, Rosa's bread, and nowhere else to be.

The babies were installed in the middle of the blanket, and the oak had provided for them. Its leaves lay everywhere, and two mobile researchers with working pincers went to work on them. Olivia's method ran to single leaves: picked up by the stem, held to the light, turned over, and examined by mouth. One was chewed. The face was the green bean face. The leaf was retrieved. Lily's method was to gather a fistful and open it over her own head.

From the blanket's rim, over bread and the last iced anything of the year, the mothers watched the two studies run. Rosa had flour on one cheek. Rosa always has flour on one cheek.

"How's the sleep," somebody asked, the way somebody always asks, and the answer took most of an hour.

The bread ended. The thermoses ended. The oak moved its shade at its usual chess pace and kept the blanket in it until the light went long and gold and then thin.

The strollers rolled home at dusk, freight asleep in both, a leaf in one fist.
