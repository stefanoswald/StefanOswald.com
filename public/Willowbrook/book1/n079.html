Night 79

Dinner With Both Hands

Month 3: Rhythm

A quiet revolution came to dinnertime. Olivia joined the table.

Not to eat. The milk months had months to run. She came as a guest, in her bouncer on the floor at the corner of the table, where she could see faces, and the effect surprised everybody. The six o'clock fussing, the kind that used to summon a standing, swaying parent away from a cooling plate, eased off.

She had not wanted milk at six o'clock. She had wanted in.

Babies are guests like that. What they crave at the loud, warm end of the day is company: faces trading talk, the music of a table, her own name arriving in sentences like a small gift each time. So the family started the habit years early. Eat together, with the baby present, from the start. She takes in the rhythm of conversation months before she has a word of it, and the parents eat warm food with both hands, a luxury they had stopped expecting.

One more table trick rode along, which was narrating the meal. "Papa is having the potatoes," said Papa. "Mama is having seconds of the potatoes," said Mama.

Olivia followed the talk like a very short guest with no language yet, and during a lull contributed one thoughtful "ah-goo," which both parents answered seriously, and at some length.

Then Papa had thirds, and nobody narrated that.
