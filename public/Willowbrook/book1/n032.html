Night 32

The First Real Smile

Month 2: Smiles and Sleep

It happened on a Tuesday in April, over a diaper. Mama leaned in and said the usual morning things, where did you get those toes and so on, and Olivia's whole face opened. Not a wind smile. Not the sliding half smile she made in her sleep. This one had her eyes in it, and it was aimed.

Mama made a noise she had never made before. Papa came running in his socks and nearly went past the door.

Babies do this somewhere around six weeks, give or take. All the faces held close since the first day, eight or ten inches away, feeding distance, as far as new eyes can see, had been building toward it. A smile is her first way of saying something to somebody on purpose, and the answer matters more than the smile. Smile back, big. Say her name. Let her find out that when she does this, the world does it back.

They spent the morning being smiled at. Papa tried for one and got a long stare. "She's saving them," he said. Mama tried again and got two.

Olivia also smiled at the ceiling fan, to be fair. The diaper got finished eventually. Nobody could say by whom.
