Night 86

The Overnight Bag Question

Month 3: Rhythm

Grandpa Joe made the offer at Sunday dinner, plain as bread. "Leave her with me one evening."

That was the whole speech. The silence after it held an entire marriage's worth of conversation, conducted by eyebrow.

Some parents are ready at three months, some at three years, and the baby, honestly, is the flexible one. They said not yet, that Sunday. Two Sundays on, they said yes to the trial size: an evening, not a night.

The bag went out to the farm with the list taped inside the lid. Feeding notes. The bedtime order, bath, quiet, book, song, lights out. The travel crib, and under it, underlined: on her back, nothing else in with her. The doctor's number, which Grandpa Joe accepted solemnly and never glanced at.

He did it all differently, of course. He sang the wrong songs, righter than he knew. He walked her along the fence line in the long light to say goodnight to the chickens, an item on no list anywhere. Then he laid her down exactly as written, and she slept like hay in a barn.

The parents, three hours alone in the someday restaurant, spent the first hour talking about her and the second remembering how to talk. Both hours were needed, in that order.

"Anytime," said Grandpa Joe, and handed her back, warm and boneless and faintly smelling of chickens.
