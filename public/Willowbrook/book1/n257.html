Night 257

Night Walking

Month 9: One Step, One Word

New movement rehearses at night. It is one of the few rules of the first year that never fails. Three weeks in, the two o'clock wakings came back, and the monitor showed a small grey person up at the rail, walking the crib's short coast, drilling.

The pause first. Half the nights she ran the lap to its end and slept where she dropped, folded over her own feet. The other half got the visit: the lay-down, once, boring and level.

On the fourth night, at the third lay-down, Papa said "LIE DOWN" in a voice that surprised them both. She stopped. Her face changed.

He sat on the floor by the crib, forehead against the bars. "Sorry. That was tired talking. Not you." He stayed, one hand through the bars on her back, until the standing gave out.

The crib stayed what it was: flat, bare, the sleep sack instead of any blanket, no hat, on her back to start and rolling where she rolled after, the room cool enough that he wanted a sweater in it.

The storm ran eleven nights. They end. This one was the last big one, because once walking is cheap there is nothing left to drill.

The last night: six strides at fourteen past two, a sit, and sleep where she sat.

Papa stayed in the doorway a minute longer than he needed.
