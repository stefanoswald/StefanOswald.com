Night 57

Eight Weeks of Hands

Month 2: Smiles and Sleep

The hands had been there all along. Waving at the edges of her world like friendly strangers. Swiping at faces. Gripping fingers by old reflex, the tight newborn grip that holds on without asking her.

But this week Olivia found them.

She held one up before her face and studied it, turning it slowly, like an astronomer with a brand-new moon. Then the other. Then, in the discovery of the century, she brought them together, and they took hold of each other, and her whole body went still with the news. These are mine. Both of them. Mine.

Finding the hands is the second month's quiet milestone, the hinge that everything later swings on: the reach, the grasp, the spoon, the crayon, the shoelace. It needs no lessons and no equipment, only stretches of unbundled time, arms free, on the mat or across a lap. The old automatic grip was loosening now, making room for the deliberate kind, where she would see a thing, want it, and send a hand out on purpose. Weeks away. In training tonight.

Mama watched the two small hands fumble at each other, gripping and slipping and gripping again.

"Introduce yourselves properly," she said. "You'll be working together a long time."

One hand held on. The other let go, drifted up, and found her mouth, which it had been looking for all along.
