Night 256

Coming In

Month 9: One Step, One Word

Sunday came round cold, and Grandpa Joe took her from Papa's arms at the porch without a word and walked her down to the barn, because it was the hour the animals come in.

He stood in the open door with her on his arm and did nothing. The field had gone the color of a paper bag. The cows came up out of it one at a time, slow, steaming, and filed past her knee into the warm dark.

Her finger went from cow to cow. Each time it landed he said the word, and only the word. "Cow."

The hens came next, at a run, Henrietta first, the Duchess last and offended, in past his boots to the roost. Somewhere behind the barn Ellie stood at the fence in a blanket, which nobody questioned.

"Everything comes in," he said, which was the whole speech.

Then the lantern, lit with one match, and the dark coming up the field behind the last cow. She leaned out toward the smell and the steam, and he let her lean, and held on.

Papa came down to fetch them and found them still there, the last tail going in.

She was asleep before the gate. Grandpa Joe stood in the door until the taillights turned.

Then he shut the barn.
