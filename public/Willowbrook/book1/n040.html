Night 40

Bicycle, Bicycle

Month 2: Smiles and Sleep

Somewhere in Olivia's middle, a bubble had taken a wrong turn, and everyone in the house had been told about it.

So Papa laid her on the mat for the morning ride. "Bicycle, bicycle," he said, taking one small foot in each hand, and pedaled her legs in slow gentle circles. Round and round. Then both knees up together, folded soft against her belly, held there for a breath, let go. Again. Then a warm palm on her tummy, rubbing slow circles, clockwise, the direction the plumbing runs.

Gas is the second month's most reliable villain. A new gut, a big appetite, swallowed air, and digestion being worked out in public. The remedies are old and belong to everybody: the pedaling, the knee folds, a warm bath, good burps after every feed, and a stretch of upright cuddling while dinner settles.

"Bicycle, bicycle," said Papa. Olivia's face was a fist.

"Bicycle, bi," said Papa, and did not get to the rest, because halfway round the third lap the bubble gave up, out loud, by the southern route, and her whole face opened like a hand.

Papa said nothing, out of respect. Olivia kicked once more on her own, cheerful, and looked at the ceiling as if none of that had been her.
