Night 62

The Shape of a Day

Month 3: Rhythm

In the third month, the days grew bones. Not a schedule. Nothing you could print, nothing you would defend at a party. Just a shape: wake, feed, play, sleep, and around again, like a small water wheel that mostly turns.

The morning nap found a usual hour. Bedtime landed in the same valley most nights. Even the fusses turned half reliable, arriving like the four o'clock bus, tired but on time.

The baby books argue about this stage at the top of their voices. Strict clocks! Total freedom! The path that fits most actual houses runs between them: the same order of events, on a loose clock. The order tells the baby what comes next. The loose clock forgives the day its weather.

For the parents, the bones changed everything. You could plan a walk now. You could warn a visitor off the nap hour. You could sit down at quarter past eight, look at another grown-up, and finish a sentence with a verb in it.

Papa stood at the kitchen calendar, where Mama had drawn a sun, a bottle, a leaf, a moon, and a sun again. "We had a whole Wednesday," he said, with real wonder. "It looked just like Monday."

On the calendar the little moon came around again. Olivia, mid-feed, closed her eyes on time.
