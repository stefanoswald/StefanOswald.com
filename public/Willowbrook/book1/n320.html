Night 320

The Album

Month 11: Mittens and the Early Dark

The far grandparents live on a screen on Sundays, Grandpa Joe lives under a hat, and in the cold half the town lives behind a scarf. So the parents made a book of faces.

Prints from the pharmacy, spread over the table after bedtime. One face to a page, the name under it in whichever handwriting was nearer. Mama. Papa. Grandpa Joe, and here the trouble started.

"She doesn't know him without the hat," said Papa.

"He's not wearing the hat at the table."

"She's never seen him at a table. She's seen him at a fence."

Grandpa Joe went in twice, hat and no hat, facing pages. Rosa went in with flour on her cheek, because that is her face. Lily went in twice as well, smiling and furious. Mrs. Henderson, and her gold dog, who had turned to leave in every picture and was represented by an ear. The far grandparents, cut from the same video call, waving.

Then Papa's page. He wanted the good one. Mama wanted the one from the three a.m. kitchen in April, unshaven, the eyebrow going, because that was the face Olivia had looked at most.

The three a.m. one went in. The good one went in the drawer.

The book was on the low shelf by morning. By lunch every page had been patted and Grandpa Joe's hat was a little eaten.
