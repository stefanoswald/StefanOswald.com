Night 190

The Third Block

Month 7: Explorers and Guardrails

Dinner was over and the tray was clear, and Papa, who had a theory, put one block on it.

Right hand. Taken.

Second block. Left hand, taken, the swap from hand to hand that arrived in July.

Third block.

Here is the puzzle, a real one at six months. Two hands, two blocks, and a third block sitting right there. What do you do?

She looked at the third block. She looked at her hands as if they were somebody else's. Then she opened the right hand, let its block fall off the tray onto the floor, took the new one, and was pleased.

Papa put the fallen block back. Same again. Drop, take, pleased. Every time, for a week of dinners. Then on Friday she did something new. She pressed the right-hand block against her chest with her forearm, to keep it, and reached with the freed hand, and the kept block slid down her front into her lap and was gone, and she looked at the empty forearm, betrayed.

It did not work. It was not going to work for a month. It was the best thing that happened at that table all week, because it was hers, and Papa did not fix it.

"Look at that," he said, to no one.

She had two blocks again. The third one waited.
