Night 107

The Low Voice

Month 4: Rolling and Laughing

Papa cannot whistle. Maple Lane knows this. But he has one instrument nobody else in the house can play, and it lives below the words, in his chest.

Olivia had known that rumble longer than she had known air. Low voices carry through walls and water, and his had reached her for months before she was born, the bass line under Mama's melody. On fussy nights the old frequency still worked like a key. Ear to his chest, his voice low and slow, reading anything, the news, the seed catalog, and all of her came down a gear, and another, her breathing lengthening to match.

The fourth month is when some Papas quietly decide they hold second chair. The milk is elsewhere. The soothing seems to live elsewhere. At the witching hour she cranes past them toward Mama, and they take it personally, at least once.

Second chair is a rumor. A father's kit is different, not lesser. The rumble. The airplane arms. The evening walk on shoulders in no hurry. A baby builds a different room for each parent, and she needs both rooms.

Tonight the seed catalog, read in the register of a cello, had her gone by page four.

"Turnips," Papa rumbled, one last time.

Gone. He sat on with the catalog open and his one listener asleep against him, warm as a cat.
