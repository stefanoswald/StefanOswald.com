Night 248

Geese Over the Lane

Month 9: One Step, One Word

The shoes by the door had been outside-only since October, and had mostly ridden in the stroller. Saturday they went to work.

Indoors she goes barefoot, or in the socks with the rubber dots now that the floors are cold, because bare feet grip and feel and that is how balance gets built. Shoes are for outside, soft ones with bend in the sole, on at the door and off at the door.

The walk was the front path, eleven yards, the longest upright trip of her life, one hand in Papa's. Mrs. Henderson waved from her porch and got a wave back at porch height for the first time. Mateo, two porches down, shouted "BABY!" and was shushed.

Halfway to the gate a sound came over the roofs, high and far and arguing with itself. Papa looked up. Then she looked up, because he had.

Geese, a long wavering line of them, going over the lane toward the brook, honking the whole way.

Her finger went up. She stood dead still, mouth open, until the last one cleared the willow's hill.

"Geese," said Papa. "Going somewhere warmer. Sensible."

She walked the rest of the path with her face up, which is not the best way to walk.

The shoes came off at the door. Under the left one, a red leaf.
