Night 132

The Washcloth in the Icebox

Month 5: Nearly Sitting, Nearly Everything

Something new in the fussing this week, chewier, more gum-forward, and the answer was in the icebox, folded in quarters.

Icebox is Grandpa Joe's word for the refrigerator, and the trick was his too. One clean washcloth, one corner wet, an hour in the cold. Olivia seized it and went to work on the chilled corner.

The fifth month lives in teething's foothills. The first white crowns usually show around six or seven months, some babies sooner, plenty later, all on time by their own calendar. The foothills have their own weather: gums that itch, a need to chew, and evenings when everything is wrong by one degree.

The kit stayed the honest classics. Cold, never frozen, because true ice burns a small mouth. Pressure, a clean knuckle rubbed firm along the ridge, received tonight with closed eyes, like a customer of long standing. And the patience to re-chill and offer again.

One warning rides with the old trick. A true fever is never teething's doing, and that rumor sends real illness to bed unlooked at. The thermometer settles it, and the doctor's line stays open for whatever it says.

The corner warmed. Olivia, unclenched by one degree, let the evening go on. Papa folded the next cloth into quarters and set it in the icebox, to go cold for Thursday.
