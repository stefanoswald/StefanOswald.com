Night 306

The Lantern Walk

Month 11: Mittens and the Early Dark

In the dark weeks after New Year, Maple Lane walks. Every porch sends somebody out with a paper lantern on a stick, and the whole street goes down to the corner and back at dusk, slowly, for no reason anyone can remember. Mrs. Henderson says it started with her mother. Mrs. Henderson's mother, by all accounts, started most things.

Olivia rode high on Papa's arm in the snowsuit, and the first lantern that went past stopped her mid-breath. Her mitten came up. Her whole face followed the light.

"Lantern," said Mama, and held theirs where the mitten could reach. "Warm. Careful."

Papa whistled the lantern song, which has no name and, after Papa's version, no tune either. The street could not pick it out. The street did not ask him to stop. Neighbors are like that.

Down the lane every light got the same treatment: the point, the look back at Papa's face, the look back at the light. Look, said the mitten. I see it, said the faces. It never once got old.

By the corner the mitten was down. By the turn for home she was a warm weight on the shoulder, her breath going slow against Papa's scarf.

The lane walked back without her. The lanterns swung all the way home.
