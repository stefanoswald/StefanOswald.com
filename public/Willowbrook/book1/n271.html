Night 271

Half Past Six

Month 9: One Step, One Word

By the end of the month the dark came at half past four, and the household found out something it had not believed in June: the dark is on your side.

Bedtime had drifted late through the autumn, the walking spending itself in the hall until seven and past. And the nights had got worse, not better. More wakings. A longer fight at the rail. A baby who is kept up past her tiredness does not sleep harder. She sleeps worse.

So bedtime came earlier. Half past six, held. The routine the same as ever, bath, quiet, book, song, lights out, only sooner. The protest at the rail on the first night ran twelve minutes. On the third it ran four. By the end of the week she was yawning at the sound of the bathwater again and slept, most nights, until six.

Papa argued once for keeping her up so he would see her after work. He got the pond on Saturday mornings instead, and the six o'clock wakings, and took the deal.

The blackout curtains had nothing left to do and were drawn anyway, out of respect. The streetlamp showed at the seam.

Mama stood at the door until the breathing went long.

Downstairs, the dishes. Upstairs, nothing at all.
