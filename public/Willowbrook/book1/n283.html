Night 283

The Physics of Overboard

Month 10: First Snow

The dropping game had a long run. The open hand of the spring, the uh-oh of October. In December it became heavy industry.

This was not the drop. This was the throw. The shoulder had joined, the release was timed, and the flight was flat and meant. The cup went, and its splash was studied. The spoon went, and was checked against last night's spoon. The bread disappointed and was tried twice more. Then the peas, which are still being found.

"Enough!"

It came out of Papa louder than he had planned, and she jumped, and her face folded, and the cry came.

He put the cloth down. He stood at the sink and took one slow breath, and another. Then he got down to the tray.

"That was loud. I'm sorry. I think dinner's done."

It was. Throwing is the arm's newest lesson, and the edge of a high chair tray is the cheapest cliff in the house. So the rules that came out of the evening were small. Three things on the tray at a time. The first throw means all done, the tray cleared with no fight, the bedtime snack making up the difference.

The cup went back on the tray the next night, alone. It stayed there, to general surprise.
