Night 226

The Puddle on Maple Lane

Month 8: The Velcro Weeks and the Leaves

It rained for three days in the middle of the month, the steady October kind, and on the fourth morning the lane came out of it changed. The maples had let go of half their leaves in one night. The gutters ran yellow. And at the corner by the Hendersons' walk lay the season's great new waterworks: a puddle the width of the sidewalk, with leaves floating on it like small boats.

The stroller stopped there. The stroller had no choice.

Olivia leaned out under her hat, pointed with her whole arm, and looked back at Mama to make sure the puddle had been seen. It had. "Puddle," said Mama. "Water. Leaves." The point held.

A wet leaf was handed up from the edge. It was studied, turned, and tasted, and given back with the face reserved for green beans.

Under the hedge, where the rain had not reached, a hedgehog with one leaf stuck on his back sat very still and watched the pointing. Olivia looked at him. He looked at her. Neither of them said anything about it.

Down at the end of the lane the brook was up with the rain, loud under the bridge. Mrs. Henderson called "Wet enough for you?" from her porch, and Mama said it was.

Drip, drip, went the maples, all the way home, onto the hood of the stroller.
