Night 289

The Goose Egg

Month 10: First Snow

The safest address in the house: the living room rug, the padded coffee-table corner, and the one bare inch beside the padding, found mid-turn.

Her first goose egg, and the cry came instantly. An instant cry is the reassuring kind.

The check took seconds. Her own height, standing. The surface, that inch. The landing, forehead, glancing. The cry, easing once she was held.

The level face was the harder part. Both kept the alarm off their faces, since a bump takes its size from the faces around it.

Comfort first, as long as it took. Then the cold cloth from the refrigerator, offered, worn a few seconds at a time.

Then the list off the fridge, read aloud. A fall from higher than her own height. A cry that will not settle, or no cry at all. Throwing up, going floppy or strange, hard to wake, a swelling that keeps growing. Any of those meant the doctor. Hard to wake, floppy, a fit, or a fall she never cried at meant the emergency number, not a wait. Under one, the office would rather hear about a bump than not, so Mama called anyway, and the nurse said watch her tonight.

They did, twice, by lamplight. The inch got padded before bed. By morning the bump was the color of a plum, and she had found the coffee table again.
