Night 315

The Winter Table

Month 11: Mittens and the Early Dark

Sundays at the farm moved indoors for the winter, to the long table, with the dark at the windows by four and the woodstove going.

Rosa and Lily came too that week, Rosa with a loaf still warm and flour on one cheek. Two old wooden high chairs came down from the barn loft. The babies were set at the corner beside Grandpa Joe, eleven days apart, and the table talk went where talk about two babies goes.

Lily crawled faster. Olivia stood longer. Lily had two more teeth. Olivia had the molar. Rosa said Lily was pulling up on the oven door, and what do you do about an oven door. Mama said Olivia had let go of the couch. All of it went across the table at speed, over the soup, while the two people in question tore bread into pieces and dropped them on each other.

Grandpa Joe said nothing. He broke a crust off the loaf, soft side, put it on Olivia's tray, and watched her get it to her mouth. Then one for Lily. Then his soup.

"Well now," he said, when the walking dates came round the table a second time.

That was all, and the talk went somewhere else.

At the door, with both babies asleep in their suits, he took his hat off to the two of them. "Bring them Sundays."
