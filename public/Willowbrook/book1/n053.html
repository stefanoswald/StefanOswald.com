Night 53

The Long Stretch

Month 2: Smiles and Sleep

Mama woke with a start, because nothing had woken her.

Light, real morning light, lay across the bed. The clock said half past six. The last feed had ended at midnight. She counted it twice, disbelieving it politely, and leaned over the rail of the bedside crib.

Olivia lay on her back in the starfish position, with the face of someone deeply committed to her current work. Six and a half hours.

Around the second and third month this begins. The night sorts itself into one long anchor stretch, usually the first shift. It is not owed to anyone. Some babies take months longer to find it, and a growth spurt can take it away overnight. But when it comes, it comes like this, unannounced, leaving a parent in morning light feeling smuggled into a new country.

Two notes ride along. The long stretch spends itself in the first hours, so the parent who goes to bed soon after the baby gets the best of it. And the encore is usually shorter. One long night is a preview, not a promise.

Olivia woke at seven, pleased with herself, having done nothing unusual in her own opinion. Mama fed her and had coffee on the porch step, in the thin sun.

Papa came down looking suspicious. "What happened?" he said.

"Nothing," said Mama. "Nothing happened all night."
