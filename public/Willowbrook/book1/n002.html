Night 2

The Language of Crying

Month 1: Arrival

That night, Olivia cried. She had one word, and this was it. Papa walked the floor with her and listened, the way you listen to a song in a language you have only just started.

A short cry with a rhythm to it usually means hungry. A whimpery one, with fists rubbing at the eyes, means tired, probably very. A fussy, squirmy one might be a bubble of air that cannot find the door. Papa did not know yet which was which. No parent is fluent on the first night. Mostly he tried things in order: milk, then diaper, then shoulder, and once, in a low moment, a lullaby with no words in it because he could not think of any.

"I'm here," he said. It was the only sentence he had in her language, so he gave it to every cry. She did not seem to want more than that. Somewhere around the fourth "I'm here" she settled against his shoulder like a small warm loaf of bread, with the whole heaviness of sleep in her.

He was nowhere near fluent. He said it once more anyway, and she fell asleep in the middle of it.
