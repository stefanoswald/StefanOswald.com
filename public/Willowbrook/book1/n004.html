Night 4

Day Moon, Night Sun

Month 1: Arrival

Olivia had her days and nights on backwards, like most new babies. At noon she slept like a stone. At two in the morning she was ready to discuss everything. "You are nocturnal, my love," Mama said. "And I am not."

Mama and Papa began teaching her the first lesson: day is bright, night is boring. Daytime feeds came with the curtains open, the radio on, and Papa telling her about the weather, which was rain, as if she had asked. Nighttime feeds became the dullest party in Willowbrook. Lights low. Voice a murmur. No games, no singing, no smiling more exciting than a nod, and straight back into the crib after.

It takes a week or three. Give a baby bright, noisy days and dim, dull nights, and her inside clock turns itself the right way up.

Not tonight, though. Tonight Olivia lay in the bassinet at ten past two, wide awake, staring at the ceiling with enormous interest, and there was nothing on the ceiling. Mama sat beside her, half in a dream already, doing her best to be boring.
