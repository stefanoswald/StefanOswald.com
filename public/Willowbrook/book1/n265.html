Night 265

The Cold Bench

Month 9: One Step, One Word

By November the Thursday bench held four strollers, and the bench was cold, so the meeting ran in coats, with paper cups, the babies on one blanket with another over the top.

The babies were close in age and wonderfully out of step. Olivia had been walking for three weeks and had one word and still woke before six. Lily was not walking and saw no reason to, and cruised the bench collecting words like acorns. She had four, and one was no. One baby had walked at nine months flat and said nothing at all. Another slept twelve hours a night and had a tower going, four high.

For a few minutes the parents compared. Everyone was ahead somewhere and behind somewhere else.

Then Rosa, flour on one cheek even here, said, "They all get everything. Nobody gets it in the same order."

The parents looked down at the blanket. The walker toured its edge. The talker narrated. The sleeper slept through the meeting. The stacker added a fifth block and knocked the lot over.

Rosa passed round something warm from the oven, and the comparing stopped, the way it does with a mouth full.

The cold came through the blanket and the babies began crying together. At last, a milestone they could share.

Four strollers went off across the leaves in four directions.
