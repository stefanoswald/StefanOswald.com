Night 318

The Mitten Problem

Month 11: Mittens and the Early Dark

The cold came in for good, and with it the oldest argument in the winter closet. The mitten problem.

Getting a mitten onto a baby is thumb work. The thumb goes in, the thumb comes out, the thumb goes in with the hand fisted around it, and the mitten rides the fist like a hat on a doorknob. Two mittens, two doorknobs, and the stroller not yet off the step.

Then the flinging. A mitten flung from a stroller used to be gone, somewhere back down the lane, the rest of the errand spent with one bare hand inside Mama's glove. So out came the old craft. A string, run sleeve to sleeve through the coat, a mitten at each end. Now a flung mitten travels as far as the yarn allows and dangles there, defeated.

A spare pair lives in the diaper bag all winter.

And the two-yes rule held in the doorway. That mittens go on in this cold is not a question. Which pair, striped or red, is hers every time, and she chose by grabbing.

"Red. Red today."

On the walk the flung one swung against the stroller wheel the length of the lane, tock, tock, and every time it swung she looked down for the noise.

Cheeks were checked at the corner, and the walk was kept short. Home the near way.
