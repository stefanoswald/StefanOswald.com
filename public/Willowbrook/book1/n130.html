Night 130

Swapping Tuesdays

Month 5: Nearly Sitting, Nearly Everything

"Take both of them next Tuesday morning," Rosa said over the strollers, flour still on one cheek from six a.m. "And I'll take both the Tuesday after. Two babies is barely worse than one, and a morning off is a morning off."

It was the oldest arrangement in the world, two people taking turns, and it had taken the Tuesday walk a month to think of it.

So the next Tuesday came, and Lily came to Olivia's house, two babies laid side by side on one mat, each the best weather the other had ever seen, one grown-up conducting. The Tuesday after, the flow reversed.

Every other week, each mother got a morning belonging to nobody. A haircut. Coffee finished hot in a shop. Or the choice taken more often than admitted, which was nothing at all.

House rules traveled with the babies. The nap steps written out and followed in both houses. The same bare crib, the same back to sleep, the same doctor's number taped up in both kitchens, under Rosa's croissant magnet and Mama's lemon.

By the third trade each baby had left something behind on purpose. Lily's second bib lived at Olivia's house, and Olivia's at Lily's.

"Same trade next time?" Rosa asked at the handoff.

Mama already had the empty stroller halfway down the walk. "I'm not turning around."
