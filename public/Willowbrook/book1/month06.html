Month 6 · The Feast Begins

For the grown-up

Nights 152–181

This is the month the high chair earns its keep. Somewhere around now your baby sits without your hands, watches your fork like a cat at a window, and gets her first taste of something that is not milk. The stories follow that: the readiness signs, the first spoonful, the gag that is not a choke, peanut and egg early and often, a thumb of water in an open cup, and the mess, which is the lesson and not the price of it.

You will also see her sounds turn into strings, ba-ba-da-da at full volume in rooms with good echoes, and you will see her lean over the tray and look for the thing she dropped. That look is new. A dropped ring used to stop existing. Now it is on the floor, and she knows it, and she will want you to know it too.

Read these aloud as you would hum. She is here for your voice and the closeness, not the plot, so no questions yet. Let her hold the book. Let her turn the wrong page, and read that one.

One thing to try: put your fingertips together and say "more" at every refill, on faith, for weeks. Nothing will happen, and then one day it will.

One thing not to worry about: she is not crawling. She rocks, she launches, she goes backward and roars about it. That is the crawl, arriving.
