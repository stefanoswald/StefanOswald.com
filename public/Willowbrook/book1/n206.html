Night 206

Apple Sunday

Month 7: Explorers and Guardrails

The rain stopped, the farm road was open again, and it was apple week at Honeywell, a week Grandpa Joe treats like a wedding.

Bushel baskets stood along the porch and the kitchen smelled of it. Henrietta walked the rail as if she had picked them.

"Best apples in the county," said Grandpa Joe, holding one up to Olivia's nose. "Don't tell the county."

She breathed it in with her whole face and tried to eat it through the skin, and was not allowed, a whole apple being for later and a raw slice for later still. What she got was apple cooked soft in his kitchen, without sugar, because Grandpa Joe has a rule about sugar and babies and a longer one about sugar and himself.

The face at the first spoon was thought. The face at the second was the whole orchard.

She ate six spoons and then put both hands in the bowl.

Behind the barn something large and grey moved along the fence and stopped to look at the porch, with a red balloon on the end of it. Papa looked, and looked away. Nobody at Honeywell questions it.

Grandpa Joe filled a basket for the car and put one apple in Olivia's lap, too big for her hands, to hold.

She held it to the second turn, asleep, and did not let go.
