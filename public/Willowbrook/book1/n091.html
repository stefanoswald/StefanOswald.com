Night 91

The Comedy Season Opens

Month 4: Rolling and Laughing

Papa went down behind the arm of the couch, and the room waited. Olivia waited. Mama, on the rug, waited with her hands already halfway to a clap.

"Boo."

Up he came, and Olivia laughed with her whole body, the kind of laugh that starts in the belly and has to go somewhere. Mama clapped. Papa bowed. He went down again.

Laughing had been rare in the third month, one coin found on the path. Now there was a pocketful. The cushion pop. The kiss on the belly that buzzed. The tickle spider that walked up her ribs so slowly it had time to announce itself the whole way, the announcing being most of the joke.

Every one of them works the same way. Something ought to be there and is not, and then it is. A baby laughs at the surprises she already trusts. The face goes away, and she cannot be sure it is coming back, and then it does, and her body finds out one more time that the world can be startling and safe in the same second.

Tonight the cushion pop ran four times. On the fourth, Papa's knees cracked on the way up.

"We'll be here all year," he told the audience.

The audience yawned halfway through his bow, and Mama picked her up.
