Night 363

The Party

Month 12: The Brook Comes Unstuck

They came in a rolling hour, coats on the bed, until the living room was full and the coats were three deep.

Grandpa Joe took the seat beside the small starred chair and stayed there. Rosa came in backward with the cake box. Lily arrived at speed on all fours, eleven days from her own birthday, and took Olivia's duck. Mateo brought sticks, one each, and gave them out, and took them back. The far grandparents were propped on the bookshelf on the screen, waving with both hands. The plant-house woman sat on the floor and got two children in her lap that were not hers.

Papa was mouths. He stood by the low table with a plate of things cut to the size of a fingernail, no grapes whole, no nuts, no popcorn, and said "small bites" eleven times.

Olivia stood in the middle of it in the star sweater and did not do anything.

Then Papa found the envelope on the bear's shelf, and read it out.

Dear Olivia. Happy birthday. Tonight I am allowed in, and I have been practicing being soft. Next year: words, your own spoon, and a friend who takes your hat. Bobby Bear.

"He writes every year," said Papa. "It's a thing he does."

Rosa came to the kitchen door with the matches.

"Gather in. First candles wait for nobody."
