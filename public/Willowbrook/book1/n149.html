Night 149

The Collection of Sounds

Month 5: Nearly Sitting, Nearly Everything

She woke talking now.

Alone, unprompted, to the ceiling and to nobody, from the crib at the foot of the bed, the whole collection, at length and with feeling. Ba-ba-ba, the first consonant she ever owned, now in chains of six and eight. Da-da-da, once the lamp's name, now applied to everything. And the new arrivals: a guttural g from the back country, the squeal discovered in the grocery aisle and used ever since, and the growl, invented for the rocking, brought out now for all serious work.

Mama and Papa lay in the bed six feet away with their eyes shut, not moving, because the speech ends the moment it gets an audience. This is practice in its pure state, the mouth and the ear running drills, a sound tried, heard, adjusted, tried again. The wiring of speech gets laid down alone, in a crib, before breakfast.

Then the answer. Every sound met and matched and handed back. Her chain, then a turn, then hers. The first conversation of the day, in the true mother tongue, which is turns.

"Ba-da-ba. GRRR. Ma-ma-ba," Olivia concluded, sighting Mama's one open eye.

"Big news," said Mama, sitting up. "Let's take it to breakfast."

Down the hall they went, in full voice, the speech carrying on without a pause for the change of room.
