Night 236

The Harvest Festival

Month 8: The Velcro Weeks and the Leaves

Willowbrook keeps one festival when the harvest is in. The square goes into bunting, the brass band sets up on the library steps, the stalls double, and the whole town turns out under the flags at once. The household brought its newest citizen to her first.

They went by the veteran rules: arrive at the opening hour, when the crowd is gentlest, and know the way out before you know the way in. A festival is the richest thing the town serves, and it is dosed.

The band was the dancing era's first live orchestra. The knees bounced on Papa's shoulders through the big drums, and the trombone was followed slide by slide with the devotion normally kept for the tractor. At the bakery stall, Miss Rosa's, the corner of one apple bun was gummed per tradition. Lily was met mid-square in her own carrier, and the two of them held, above the crowd, a meeting of pointed balloons.

The fuse announced itself at the ninety-minute mark. They left waving, the band's last march following them down Maple Lane.

The balloon rode home on her wrist, was pointed at through the whole bath, and spent the night tied to the dresser, level with the bear.

By morning it had sunk to the floor. It was pointed at anyway.
