Night 75

The Rolling Commute

Month 3: Rhythm

The first roll had been an accident. The tenth was a hobby. By this week it was a commute.

Olivia had connected the dots: roll, and the world moves. The knitted rabbit at the edge of the mat, formerly scenery, was now a destination.

Back to side, a mighty heave. Side to tummy. Pause, regroup, sphinx up, look around. Then the return trip, tummy to back, which she had picked up in the other order, like most babies, and performed now with a showman's flop. Two rolls and she had traveled her own length. Three, and the mat had no more borders.

This is the first draft of getting around, and it rewrites the house rules on the spot. The mat needs open runway now, floor time in cleared country, the best gift there is for a new roller. And the changing table enters its dangerous age. One hand on the baby was always the rule. Now it is the rule twice over, and on squirmy days the floor is the wiser place to change her. Sofas and beds, never alone, not for a moment, since the moment is what rollers are made of.

She reached the rabbit in four rolls, seized it, and bit it in triumph.

The trip home could wait. Her yawn said the traffic was terrible anyway.
