Night 143

The Ocean in a Cup

Month 5: Nearly Sitting, Nearly Everything

One plastic cup. The bath acquired it on Monday, and physics acquired its most devoted student.

Mama poured, slowly, from a hand's height, and Olivia met the falling water with both hands, and the lesson began. Water fell. It fell every time. It fell through her fingers no matter how the fingers conspired, and arrived in the tub with a sound and a sparkle and a splash she tracked like weather.

Twenty minutes of it, undiminished. The cup is the finest toy of the summer and it costs nothing. Gravity, flow, cause and effect once a second all evening. And the tub is the right classroom, wet without consequence.

The one print that never changes: Mama's hand stayed on her the whole time, since a baby in water is never left, not for the length of a towel, and the bath ends at the first shiver or the first glassy stare, before the student does.

Esquire the duck bobbed alongside, wearing his one expression, and got poured on, and took it well.

Someday the cup teaches the harder half, filling instead of spilling, and someday after that a real child pours real milk mostly into a real bowl. It began here, in two inches of water.

The student was lifted out, pruned and protesting, into the hooded towel. The cup stayed behind, bobbing beside the duck.
