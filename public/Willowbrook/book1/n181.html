Night 181

Oliver Owl Asks the Half-Year Question

Month 6: The Feast Begins

On the Great Willow's highest branch, where the tree keeps its oldest wood, Oliver Owl took the late August air, which had an edge in it tonight for the first time.

Stella came down through it with everything she had seen that month, and the Sleepy Moon leaned in over the hill to hum himself present, and the willow let down its faint gold in the dark.

"The Maple Lane one," said Stella, stars being brief. "Half a year tonight. One tooth. Sits by herself. Rolls to wherever she is going. Said mmm to a sweet potato and meant it."

"Mm," hummed the Moon.

Oliver Owl speaks only in questions. It is how the wisest listening is done.

"And do they know," he asked the branch, the star, the humming dark, "that this is the one mark they can stand on and look both ways from?"

Stella considered the small dark window on Maple Lane, far below, where two people had lit one candle for the number and looked, she had watched them, both ways.

"They know," she said. "Tonight they know."

"Then what," asked Oliver Owl, settling his feathers, "could an old bird add?"

Nothing, said the willow's hush. Nothing, hummed the Moon.

Stella rose back to her place over the lane. The town settled under her, porch by porch, the last fan clicking off.

Goodnight, Willowbrook.
