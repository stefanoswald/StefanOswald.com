Night 141

Under the Coffee Table

Month 5: Nearly Sitting, Nearly Everything

Somebody had left the gate open.

The ring was on the rug by the sofa, a fact Olivia took in from her back and acted on. She rolled. Once, and the ring got no closer. Twice, and it got farther. Three times, going the way rolling goes, which is sideways to whatever you want, and she arrived, stately as a rowboat blown offshore, under the coffee table.

The face that looked out from under it held the most complicated expression of her life so far: triumph and outrage, about the same event.

"She went somewhere," said Mama.

"Not on purpose," said Papa.

"Somewhere is somewhere."

Rolling with range is how a baby gets about at this age, and it has no steering. She wants the ring and arrives at the table. For weeks it works like this, a small determined tide going out from every desire, fetched back from under the furniture, gone again.

The temptation is to turn her around. Aim her. Fix it. Mostly, resist. A body sorts out its directions with mileage, not instruction. The better help is the floor itself: clear at rug level, the gate latched, more floor time now that the floor leads somewhere.

Papa slid her out by the ankles, gently, like a drawer. She glared at the ring, which had not moved, and rolled straight back under the table.
