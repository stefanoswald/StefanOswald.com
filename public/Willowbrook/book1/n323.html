Night 323

The Sniffle Season

Month 11: Mittens and the Early Dark

Colds go round a town in winter, and this one came home from the library Thursday and was in Olivia's nose by Saturday.

The kit was known by now. Saline drops, then the bulb, a brief indignity that works. The humidifier making its sea noise all night. Fluids, milk and the cup and the cup again. Upright cuddles by day, on a parent who was awake, so the nose could drain. At night the crib, flat, on her back, the mattress not propped.

The list on the fridge was read anyway, as always: the fever numbers, the breathing signs, the doctor's number under the lemon magnet. Nothing on it applied. She was miserable and ordinary.

The new part: she could ask. A stuffed-up baby cannot say what is wrong, but she can rub her whole face on your shoulder and hold up her arms, and she did, half the night. She was held, sitting up in the chair, lamp on, until the crying stopped, then laid back in the crib on her back. She asked again. She was held again, and laid back again. It is still the whole treatment for the part saline cannot reach.

By the third morning the nose ran clear and the arms went up for the fruit bowl instead.

Papa took her temperature that morning to be sure. Normal. He kissed the spot anyway.
