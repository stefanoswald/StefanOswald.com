Night 128

The Texture Tour

Month 5: Nearly Sitting, Nearly Everything

Bark first. The old oak's hide, ridged and patient, Olivia's palm pressed flat to it under Mama's hand, and her face going through the whole review: surprise, then study, then approval.

The tour had no tickets and it ran whenever the July morning was cool enough. After the bark, grass, ten thousand cool blades at once against the back of a hand. A smooth stone from the path's edge, already warm. The bench's plank. Mama's sleeve. The stroller's cool frame.

Skin is this year's schoolbook. Rough, smooth, warm, cool: she will know every one of those words by hand years before she says them.

The rules were the standing ones. Nothing sharp. Nothing small enough to go in a mouth, because everything goes in the mouth. Hands washed after the outdoor stops, since the oak was home to several kinds of ant. And one rule worth saying out loud: say it out loud. "Rough. That's rough bark." The word goes on at the moment of touching, when words stick best.

"Warm," said Mama at the stone. "Warm stone." Olivia licked it before anyone could rule on that.

The last stop was at home, Bobby Bear's fur, appraised and found superior to all bark. She yawned in the middle of the appraisal and was carried up with the fur still shut in her fist.
