Night 68

Choosing the Village

Month 3: Rhythm

The calendar said what calendars say. The leave was ending, the world wanted its grown-ups back, and somebody kind would need to hold Olivia for some hours of some days.

So they sat at the kitchen table with the papers, after she was down. They had toured the sunny center in town, where the babies napped in a row like loaves. They had met the woman who kept four small children in a house full of plants and patience. They had done the sums on shifted hours and grandparents. Every choice cost something, and only some of the cost was money.

The list that mattered turned out to be short. Warm hands, and few babies for each pair of them. Safe sleep done right, backs, bare cribs, no exceptions, no negotiation. People who talk to babies, not near them. And the feeling in the doorway, the yes or the no that arrives in the chest before the checklist is finished.

The guilt came too, uninvited, as it does. Mama said it out loud, which helped. Papa said the thing they both needed said: "More people loving her is not less love."

They chose the plant house. Two weeks of practice mornings would come first.

Papa turned the calendar to the new month. Mama turned off the lamp. In the next room, the person under discussion did not stir.
