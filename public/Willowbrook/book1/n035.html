Night 35

Drowsy but Awake

Month 2: Smiles and Sleep

The advice sounded like a riddle. Put her down drowsy but awake. Asleep enough to go, awake enough to notice she went.

The idea under it is kind. A baby who drifts off in her own crib, even now and then, is starting on the biggest bedtime skill there is: falling asleep in the place where you wake up, so the small surprises of the night do not need a search party.

Some nights it worked. Eyes at half mast, down onto the mattress, a squirm, a sigh, gone. Mama stood in the dark like a person who has just landed a plane. Many nights it did not. Down drowsy, up furious, rocked all the way to sleep, and lowered into the crib like something that might go off.

That is not failing. That is a baby being eight weeks old. Rocking her to sleep, feeding her to sleep, walking the hall with her, none of it is a bad habit yet. It is the job. Drowsy but awake is a ticket worth buying most nights, and never a test to pass.

Tonight the plane came in. Mama backed out on her socks and did not breathe until the hall. Olivia lay on her back with one arm flung up over her head, and stayed that way.
