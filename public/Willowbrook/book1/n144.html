Night 144

The Long Distraction

Month 5: Nearly Sitting, Nearly Everything

Mid-feed, the world kept happening.

A door. A dog two streets off. And Olivia would come off like a fire chief hearing the bell, swivel toward the event. A feed that had run twenty calm minutes now straggled across forty.

On Tuesday, at the fourth bell in ten minutes, Mama said "Oh, for heaven's sake," in a voice she did not like, put her down on the mat, and went and stood at the window.

Then she came back and sat on the floor. "That was me," she said. "Not you. Let's go again." And they went again.

The distracted feeder is right on time. She is a nervous system succeeding. The wiring that tracked the bear across the room now finds the whole house worth attending to, and the feed has competition. The fixes are the room, not the rules. Feeds moved to the dim bedroom, door shut, world muffled, appetite winning by default. A cloth to grip, for the hand that would otherwise point. And a reset of the parent's patience, because shorter, more frequent feeds are this season's shape and not a crisis of supply.

By the year's turn babies become quick, businesslike feeders, and the forty-minute wanderings get remembered fondly.

Tonight's dinner ran in the dim room, nobody interrupted. The world, unattended for a whole feed, managed itself.
