Night 148

The Season Pass Falters

Month 5: Nearly Sitting, Nearly Everything

Same bath, same book, same wrong note in the song, and at the crib, revolt.

Silk for a month, and this week, gravel. The arch. The wail. The whole-body objection from a baby who had gone down easy since May.

The suspects were lined up. The rocking, first, since a body rehearsing a new move resents all horizontal proposals. The gums, always eligible. The July light, still up at eight, arguing with the blind. And the plainest suspect, the day's last wake window grown too long as a nap shifted, delivering an overtired baby to the rail.

On the third night Mama, holding a rigid and furious person over the rail, felt her own jaw set. She put her against her shoulder instead and took one slow breath at the window, and another, until her hands went soft. Then she started the song again from the top, wrong note and all.

The routine stayed the routine, because the gravel is weather and the road is the road. The wake window got checked and trimmed, fifteen minutes earlier. A darker blind went up against the sun. Nobody decided that all progress had been a trick.

Twelve days the gravel ran. Then the nap settled and bedtime went back to silk. The next patch would come. The same bath, the same book, the same wrong note would be waiting for it.
