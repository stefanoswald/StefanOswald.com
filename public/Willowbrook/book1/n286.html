Night 286

The Molar Weather

Month 10: First Snow

The front teeth were in. Now Olivia's gums seemed sore farther back, where molars would eventually appear.

She drooled through two bibs, chewed at new angles, and pressed a fist against one cheek. She chewed the arm of the couch, the strap of the high chair, and Papa's watch, which he had not offered. Mama offered a clean chilled washcloth and a firm rubber teether from the refrigerator, never frozen hard enough to hurt tender gums. A clean finger rubbed the sore ridge. Teething necklaces and numbing gels stayed out of the kit.

The thermometer mattered, too. Teething can bring sore gums, drool, and restless sleep, but it does not explain a true fever or a baby who seems genuinely ill. Persistent ear pain, trouble drinking, unusual sleepiness, breathing trouble, or a fever meant calling the pediatrician instead of saying, "just teething."

Olivia had no fever. She was simply uncomfortable and furious about it. The dark came at five, as it does now, and the evening ran long. Papa held her through the gravelly part of it while Mama rotated the washcloths.

The molar itself was in no hurry. It kept its own calendar, and no one in the house could read it. What worked was the cold cloth, and a lap, and the song, and by the second verse the fist had come down from her cheek.
