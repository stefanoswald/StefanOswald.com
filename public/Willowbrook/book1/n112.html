Night 112

The Pillow Court

Month 4: Rolling and Laughing

A throne went up on the nursery rug: pillows in a horseshoe, the firm one at the back. Olivia was set upon it, Mama kneeling an arm's length off, and the pillow court held its first sitting.

Sitting at four months is an argument with gravity. Tall for three proud seconds. Then the slow lean. The correction. The over-correction. The sideways settle into a cushion.

And then the thing the court had gathered to see. Both hands went down, planted like two small posts, and held the realm upright. The tripod sit. Most babies get there in the fifth or sixth month, and this is the rehearsal, all wobble.

The rules of court were three. Sessions stayed short and ended before the wobble turned to woe. The floor stayed honest, cushions for the fall and not instead of the practice, since falling is how catching gets practiced. And no propping her to perfection: a baby wedged bolt upright gets good at pillows, and a baby allowed to wobble gets good at balance, which is the part she keeps.

Today's session ran four minutes. Two topples. One stretch of nine seconds on her own, during which the court held its breath.

Ended by yawn. Carried off still faintly regal.

The pillows kept the shape of her all afternoon, and nobody wanted to be the one to put them away.
