Night 211

The House at Ten Inches

Month 7: Explorers and Guardrails

On the month's last night they crawled the house again, at her height, by lamplight.

Papa stopped at the bookcase brackets a beat too long. "Not misty," he said.

In the kitchen, the cat's dish on the washing machine, the cat still looking at Mama about it. The bowl a block went into once. The tube on the counter, the coin and bead in the jar above.

By the door, a leaf that came in on a sock, under the sweater that went on over a scream. The hedgehog, Mama thought, and the dog, and the look back after both. "She looks back at me now," she said.

The farm apple in the fruit bowl, held asleep to the second turn, the rest cooked, beside where the tablet leans, palm to flannel, forty miles off.

In the stroller tray, a fossilized puff, a gift. On the couch arm, the peekaboo cloth, hers now.

They met under the lamp she says ma-ma to, cord tied twice, fern on the fridge. "I said no too loud," Mama said. "And sorry."

The stair gate, rattled six days, opened Sundays for two steps, a hand on her back.

Down the hall, the crib that left their room, an eleven-day strike, a six-day cold, a new tooth in a sleeping mouth.

Under the couch, one sock. First morning. Rug to hall, four seconds.
