Night 61

The Swaddle Retires

Month 3: Rhythm

The end of the swaddle came during a diaper change. A heave, a twist, and Olivia was looking at the other wall. She had rolled, for the first time ever, with no witness but Mama, who gasped as if the changing table had done it.

That was that. The rule is plain and unbendable: the first sign of rolling, even attempting, and the arms come out for good. A roller needs her hands free to push, prop, and protect herself. The swaddle, fold-fold-tuck of a hundred nights, was finished as of that afternoon.

Mama expected a riot at bedtime. The riot lasted about the length of one song. Then the sleep sack took over, sleeveless and zippered, a blanket she could wear, warm without wrapping and safe without a single loose end. Olivia slept her new sleep with both arms flung overhead like a tiny weightlifter mid-cheer. She seemed to have been waiting for this.

Later, in the quiet, Mama washed the swaddle blanket, folded it, and put it in the keepsake drawer. Out of pure habit she folded it once more on the way in. Fold, fold, tuck, around nothing at all.

Across the room, both arms up, Olivia slept through the retirement.
