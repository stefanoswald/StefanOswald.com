Night 249

The Clocks Go Back

Month 9: One Step, One Word

The clocks went back on Saturday night, and everyone in town got an hour, except the people with babies, who got a five o'clock.

Mama had done the clever thing. For four nights she nudged bedtime later, a quarter hour a night, so her clock and the kitchen's would meet Sunday. Olivia woke at ten to five. The clever thing had bought half an hour, Papa figured, at the black window. Half an hour is not nothing.

So, the dark shift. The kitchen lamp only, and the nursery kept dark, because a dark room at five says night to a body and light says morning, and her body should hear night for another week. He named what was out there: the fence, the maple, Mrs. Henderson's porch light.

At seven, which was six, grey light came up behind the fence, and he took her out on the step into it, hat on. Morning light moves a body's clock more than any nudging.

Naps drifted all week. The five o'clocks became half past fives, then sixes, and by the next Sunday the clock and the baby agreed again, more or less, which is all they ever do.

Mama came down at half past seven, which was half past six, and found Olivia asleep in the crib and Papa asleep in the kitchen chair, the kettle cold, the hat on the table.
