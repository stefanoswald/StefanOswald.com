Night 34

The Sleepy Signs

Month 2: Smiles and Sleep

Mama got everything wrong that day. Olivia fussed, and Mama offered milk, and Olivia did not want milk. Mama changed her. She was dry. Mama tried the bouncy walk, the window, the song, and the song made it worse. By four o'clock they were both crying, one of them quietly.

Here is a thing worth saying plainly. Parents and babies are in step about one time in three. The other two, somebody misses, and the whole art is noticing the miss and coming back. Nobody reads a baby right all day. Nobody is meant to.

What Mama had been missing was the tired. Olivia had been sending the signs since lunch. A long stare at nothing, done with faces for now. A fist at the ear, then the eye, like a small cloth polishing a window. Then, when those went unanswered, the arched back and red-faced fury of a baby fighting the very sleep she needs. At this age the happy awake time is an hour or so, sometimes less, and then the signs begin.

At six, in the amber light, Mama caught one. Fist to eye. She did not try anything clever. Curtains. Lamp down. The crib.

"There you are," she said. "I missed it. I've got it now."

Olivia yawned, enormous and forgiving, and went down inside the yawn.
