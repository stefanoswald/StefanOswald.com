Month 4 · Rolling and Laughing

For the grown-up

Nights 91–120

This is the month the laughs come. Last month there was one, found by luck. Now there is a pocketful, most of them at the same joke: a face goes away and comes back. She laughs at the surprises she already trusts. You will hear it all month, the cushion pop, the cloth over your face, the spoon over the side of the high chair.

She also gets the floor. Rolling both ways means she can go somewhere, so expect an afternoon on your knees finding out what is under the couch. Her first consonant may turn up, a "ba" out of nowhere, and everything in reach goes in the mouth. Around now the nights may come apart for a few weeks. That is a rebuilding, not a step backward, and not your fault.

Read these the way you have been: for the voice, not the plot. She follows the tune of you up its hills and down. The same page for a week is fine. No questions yet. At this age the sounds are the questions, and a "boo" is a whole conversation.

One thing to try: when she says "ba," say it back, then stop, hands still, and wait. The wait is the part that matters.

One thing not to worry about: whether she is sitting yet. Most babies get there next month or the one after. The wobble is the practice.
