Night 272

The First Haircut

Month 9: One Step, One Word

The curls had been town business since the festival, and by month's end they crossed a practical line. The forelock was in her eyes every time she settled on the rug, pushed back hourly with a whole-hand sweep.

They did it at home. At this age a stranger plus sitting still is a poor trade against a kitchen she knows by heart. The chair was the high chair, strapped in properly, the tray stocked for distraction: Esquire on dry-land duty, one banana for the opening act, cereal in reserve. Mama was the barber, and Papa took distraction and photographs. The cape was one towel and a clothespin, an outrage survivable only under banana.

The cut took ninety seconds. The forelock lifted between two fingers, the click of the scissors listened to with interest and no complaint, one snip, then a second to even it. And the curl caught as it fell into the waiting envelope. Dated, sealed, and put with the keepsakes beside the swaddle and the hospital band. That hair had been on her since the hospital, the last of the newborn still riding along, and ninety seconds took it off.

"Uh-oh," Olivia said at the falling curl, the way she says it at everything that falls.

Both parents initialed the envelope. The forelock stayed clear three weeks.

The curl is still in there.
