Night 228

The Big Tub Graduation

Month 8: The Velcro Weeks and the Leaves

The little sloped tub had been shipping water over its rails for weeks. The harbor was outgrown from the inside.

Graduation was held on a Tuesday evening. The real tub, the big white ocean, three inches of water tested at the elbow, and the graduate set down at the center of it, dwarfed, delighted, and instantly at work on the new physics.

The rules came with her, and the new address bought no exceptions. The non-slip mat was laid before the first voyage, since porcelain and a wet sitting baby are a pair that takes no chances. Water to the hips, no higher. The cold tap finished last, so the spout ran cool. The hot handle was beyond her reach and guarded, and the spout wore its padding. And the oldest bathing law in this book, unchanged since the sponge days: within the hand's reach, the whole time, no exception the width of a ringing phone.

The old crew transferred. Esquire the duck bobbed out onto open water with his expression unchanged. The pouring cup resumed its lectures from deeper reserves.

The washing took ninety seconds. The session ran twenty minutes, by filibuster and charm. The little tub went to the basement beside the bassinet.

Steam settled. The hooded towel received its small pink prophet, as ever.
