Night 8

Enough Visitors for Today

Month 1: Arrival

Everyone in Willowbrook wanted to meet Olivia. The doorbell had opinions all day long. Visitors brought casseroles and kindness, and also noise, and germs on hands that had not been washed, and one aunt who woke the baby on purpose "just to see her eyes." By four o'clock Mama had said "yes, she's very alert" eleven times and had not sat down once.

Then Grandpa Joe came up the walk with a basket of eggs. He left his muddy boots on the step and washed his hands at the kitchen sink without being asked. He took Olivia, nodded Mama toward the shower, and sat in the armchair with the baby on his chest for forty minutes, saying nothing to anyone. When Mama came back with wet hair, he handed Olivia over, said "Well," and went home.

The doorbell rang once more. Papa opened the door six inches and became a gate. A polite gate, but a gate. "Thank you for coming," he said. "We're resting now." Visitors who wash their hands, hold the baby so you can shower, and leave before you wilt are medicine. The other kind can wait a month. Or three.

The door closed. The house went quiet. Mama sat in the warm armchair with her hair still wet, and Olivia slept against her, smelling faintly of flannel and the farm.
