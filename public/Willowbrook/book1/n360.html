Night 360

The Year in One Walk

Month 12: The Brook Comes Unstuck

On the last Sunday before the birthday they took the year for a walk. Past the places.

The porch first. Mrs. Henderson's porch across the street, where the first thing anybody ever called to her was "Teething!" The corner, where the plow had waved. "Maybe," said Mama at the corner.

The park, the bucket swing pushed once on cold chains. The bench, where Mama and Rosa had sat all summer and stopped counting other people's babies. The pond, and two ducks back on it already, and Olivia pointing with her whole arm.

The library steps, where the card had been signed with a fist. The bottom of the hill, and the willow at the top with its bare gold branches, and they did not climb, because the hill was mud and January's walk had been enough.

Then the bridge.

The brook had been stuck since December. It was not stuck now. It ran under the bridge loud and brown and fast, with the ice broken along the edges in plates, and the noise of it came up through the boards into their feet.

"That's the sound you were born to," said Papa. "It came unstuck the day you came."

Olivia walked the last stretch of the lane herself, in the mud, holding nobody, and was carried the rest.

From the porch you could still hear it going.
