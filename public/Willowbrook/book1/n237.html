Night 237

Dark by Bath Time

Month 8: The Velcro Weeks and the Leaves

In the third week the dark moved in early. Dinner, which had run in daylight since the tastings began, now ran under the lamp, and the window behind the high chair went from blue to black between the soup and the bread. The clock said six. Every part of the room said later.

It was a crowded week besides. Papa worked late two nights. Mama had a cold that put her voice down half an octave, which Olivia found suspicious. The festival had been Saturday, the sitter was Thursday.

Through all of it, the five things happened in the same order. Bath. Quiet. Book. Song. Lights out.

They happened when Papa was there and when he was not. They happened in Mama's cold voice and in the sitter's careful one. The book was the bear book four nights out of seven. The song was the slow one with the wrong words in it.

Olivia yawned at the sound of the bathwater every night that week, as she had since April. The dark at the window was new. The order was not.

"Same as ever," said Mama, hoarse, on the last night, lifting her out of the water.

Lights out. The window was black. The hall light was on. A thin gold line under the door.
