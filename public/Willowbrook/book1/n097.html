Night 97

A Chair at Headquarters

Month 4: Rolling and Laughing

The high chair came in a box with forty steps and one picture, and Papa built it on the kitchen floor after bedtime with three screws left over. Nobody mentioned the screws.

It was not for food. Milk was still all of dinner and would be for months, and the tray would stay bare a while. But her back had firmed and her head rode steady, and propped snug in the chair's recline, strapped in, a grown-up at her elbow, she reached the place she had been angling for since the bouncer. Table height.

The bouncer had made her an onlooker down by the ankles. The chair made her one of them. Faces across, not above. The talk ran along at her ear, her own name sailing past at close range.

She slapped the tray once, flat-handed, like a person who has arrived somewhere.

A high chair earns its floor space before the first bite: five or ten minutes at family meals, always strapped, always with someone right there, the recline kept until she sits solid on her own. The tray stays empty until around six months, when she sits steady, reaches well, and watches food travel with plain intent.

She was already watching the potatoes with plain intent, two months early. They carried her off to the bath still craning back at the table.
