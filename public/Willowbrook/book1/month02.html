Month 2 · Smiles and Sleep

For the grown-up

Nights 32–59

Somewhere around six weeks, give or take, she smiles at you on purpose, and most parents make a noise they have never made before. Underneath it, she is finding her hands, following a slow bear with both eyes, and learning that when she makes a sound and somebody answers, she has said something. Answer, then wait, eyebrows up. The gap is where the conversation is.

There is a harder part. Crying peaks about now, some for a reason and some for none. She sends tired signs, and you will miss some. The stories this month have a mother who missed every sign until the yawn and a father who said something too loud and went out to the porch. Both came back.

Read these the way you would hum. She is listening to the tune of you, not the words, and the same page seven nights running is a lullaby, not a rut. Do not ask her anything. When the last line goes quiet, leave it quiet.

If you try one thing, make it the order. Bath, quiet, book, song, lights out, every night, not most nights. She cannot tell time yet, but she can tell what comes next.

And do not worry about rocking her all the way to sleep. It is not a habit yet. It is the job. April is outside the window, and you will see about ten minutes of it.
