Night 133

The Bib Drawer

Month 5: Nearly Sitting, Nearly Everything

Three bibs, in the beginning. Gifts. Decorative. The drool era did the rest.

The terry workhorses, bought by the eight. The bandana kind that made her look like a very small train robber. The plastic-backed heavy-weather gear. The two fancy ones, kept for photographs and ruined at the first photograph. It was the lowest drawer in the kitchen.

The bib is the least glamorous invention of the year and the most useful. One snap of cloth turns four soaked outfits into one dry one, and a red, sore chin and chest into clear skin, since a wet chin left wet all day rubs itself raw. Cotton against the skin. A snap that survives a one-handed change.

On Wednesday Mama sat Olivia in front of the open drawer to see what would happen. What happened was a sorting. The train robber, out, into the mouth, passed to the other hand, dropped. The strawberry one, out, chewed, passed across, dropped. Passing a thing from one hand to the other was new this month and she did it with every bib in the drawer until the floor was a tide of them.

"Those were the clean ones," Mama said.

Olivia got the red bandana onto her own head by accident and sat under it, pleased with nothing in particular.

The drawer stood open behind her, eleven deep, and rising.
