Night 214

The Kiss, the Wave, the Window

Month 8: The Velcro Weeks and the Leaves

Every door in the house needed a goodbye that month, so the household built one. It had three beats, and it did not change.

Beat one: the kiss, given at floor level by the parent who was leaving, with the same words riding on it every time. "Papa goes to work. Papa comes back. Always comes back."

Beat two: the wave at the door. Her hand went side to side like a windshield wiper against his glove. Two seconds, no lingering. Lingering feeds the storm. And nobody ever slipped out while she was looking the other way. A sneaked goodbye buys one quiet minute and sells every minute after it, because then any turned back might be a leaving.

Beat three: the carry to the front window, fast, arriving as the car door shut. The second wave. The horn, two taps. Beep, beep. The watching until the corner took him.

The first week, the wail after the door lasted four minutes. The second week, under two. By the end of the month it had turned inside out. When Papa picked up his keys, Olivia crawled ahead of him to the window, hauled herself up by the sill, and waited there for her horn.

Beep, beep. The corner took him. She turned back into the day.
