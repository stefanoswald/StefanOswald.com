Night 139

The Kitchen Dance

Month 5: Nearly Sitting, Nearly Everything

No date on it. Just a Thursday when the dishes got ignored and the kitchen got danced in.

It had been five months and change since the door on Maple Lane opened for three, and here is how the two of them were doing, honestly, since a marriage is equipment too, and equipment wears out unattended.

Tireder than in any year before. Kinder in it. The four a.m. handoffs had built a reflex of mercy that was outlasting the four a.m.s. They spoke a new dialect, half logistics, "she's fed, bath's run, your window's now," and half code: the eyebrow, the two-finger tap on the table that meant sit down, I've got this. The old dialect, about books and plans and nonsense, had thinned, and they caught it in time.

So there was a twenty-minute no-baby-talk rule at dinner, broken nightly at minute six. A walk together, alone, on a traded Tuesday. The rule for couples is that small and often beats grand and never. The six-second kiss by the sink. The thank-you said out loud for the invisible shift. The argument had early and small, before it sets.

And the dance, to no music. The monitor stayed dark the whole song.

"We should do this weekly," Papa said, turning her past the dishwasher.

"It is weekly," said Mama, cheek on his shoulder. "You keep forgetting."
