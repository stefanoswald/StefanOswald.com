Night 147

The Five-Month Stare

Month 5: Nearly Sitting, Nearly Everything

It happened at the tail of the dusk feed, in the dim room, no bells ringing. Dinner done, drowse not yet arrived, Olivia lay in the crook of Mama's arm and looked up, and stayed.

Not the scanning look. The long look, eyes holding eyes, unhurried, both ways, her one free hand rising to rest on Mama's cheek like a small warm signature.

A minute. More. Neither of them broke it.

July deepens this. The gaze gets duration. The tuning of the early weeks thickens into something the two of them can feel in the sternum. In the held look two nervous systems settle toward each other, the small one borrowing calm from the large one, the large one borrowing something back that has no name.

These stares cannot be scheduled or begun on purpose. They come at the margins, a feed's end, a wake's edge, and they ask exactly one skill of a parent, which is not being the one who looks away first. The phone goes face down. The minute is allowed to be a minute.

They will thin with the years, crowded out by walking and words and doors closed politely.

The hand patted the cheek once, twice, and stilled. The eyes went to half-mast.

Mama stayed until the signature slid off her cheek, and a good while after.
