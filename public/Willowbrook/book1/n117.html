Night 117

Sleeping Through the Heat

Month 4: Rolling and Laughing

The first hot week of the year leaned on Willowbrook, and the room where the crib stood needed diplomacy.

Babies sleep best at the same temperature grown-ups do, high sixties to about seventy. Summer does not take requests. So the house worked its levers. Curtains drawn through the fierce hours, the room hoarding its morning cool. The fan in the corner turning the air without aiming at the crib, a breeze for the room, not the baby.

Bedtime clothes stripped to summer kit: a diaper and the thinnest cotton sleep sack, or no sack on the nights that refused to cool, since a baby's night outfit should lose a layer before her parents' would. Nothing else in the crib. The only thermometer that mattered was a parent's hand at the late look-in, on the chest and the back of the neck. Warm and dry, carry on. Damp with sweat at the neck, take off a layer, no debate. Cool hands and feet, proving nothing.

Extra milk stood ready for the thirst heat runs up. The cat lay flat on the kitchen tiles, poured there.

Olivia slept spread to her full span, working across the mattress all night toward whichever part of the sheet was still cool.

Inside, the room held at sixty-nine, and the fan went on turning its slow half circle, over and back, until morning.
