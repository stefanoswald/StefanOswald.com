Night 298

The Call at the Holiday

Month 10: First Snow

The holiday morning ran on her clock, and her clock said ten to six.

One parcel from the far aunts, containing a toy she ignored and a ribbon she did not. One box lid, worn as a hat. One felt star, retrieved from the couch and put back on the couch.

Then the call.

Mama's mother and father, two time zones west, in a kitchen still dark. Two faces on the screen. It is the one screen she gets, and will be for a long while yet: no shows, no games, nothing that plays at her. A face that waits for her is a different thing from a picture that moves.

They waited beautifully. Grandma sang the song about the boat, and Olivia pulled up on the coffee table and bounced at the knees, holding on, dancing.

Then she went and got the duck.

Esquire arrived at the screen at speed and was pressed to Grandma's face, twice, and left there while everyone wept a little at both ends.

"That's your grandma," said Mama, twice, since the first time got the duck.

Mama's father asked whether she could see him, as he does every call. No one has ever answered that.

When the screen went dark she patted it, once, as she pats the bear, and went to find the box lid.
