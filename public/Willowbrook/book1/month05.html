Month 5 · Nearly Sitting, Nearly Everything

For the grown-up

Nights 121–151

This month she sits up. Not for long, and not without her hands, but from the top of herself the room looks different, and so does she. She turns to her name. She keeps her smiles for people she knows now and gives strangers the long look instead. She talks to the ceiling before breakfast in chains of sounds that are practice, not noise. And the bedtime steps you have kept since April start paying you back, most nights, in a baby who goes down awake and finds sleep on her own.

You will see yourself in here too. The suitcase packed for one night away. The ten o'clock hallway where the guilt keeps its hour. The Tuesday morning that belongs to nobody, traded with another tired parent. A few of these stories show a parent getting it wrong and coming back.

Read these the way you have been reading, for the voice, the tune, the closeness. She will grab the page. Let her. Say her name when it comes up and watch her head turn. No questions yet, only sounds she can answer, and the one goodnight line said the same way every night.

One thing to try: a clean washcloth, one corner wet, an hour in the refrigerator, before the gums get loud.

One thing not to worry about: the week bedtime falls apart. It is weather. Keep to the road and it passes.
