Night 60

The First Laugh

Month 3: Rhythm

Tuesday morning, an ordinary one, which is how milestones like to arrive. Papa was doing the belly kiss, the one that had been getting a delighted cough for two weeks. He kissed. He waited. And Olivia laughed.

Not the cough. A real laugh, rolled out and bubbling, up from somewhere deeper than she was tall. The room went still. Mama stopped in the doorway with a towel in her hands.

Papa kissed the tummy again. She laughed again, harder, and then Mama was laughing, and then Papa, and for a minute the whole house ran on one baby's joy.

Here is the thing about a first laugh, which lands around three or four months. She was not laughing at the kiss. She was laughing with him. It was her first joke, and she had handed it to somebody on purpose.

They spent the day making fools of themselves for an encore. Papa tried the kiss eleven more times. He tried a sneeze, a sock puppet, a noise like a goose. Olivia watched him with polite interest. A laugh, it turns out, cannot be ordered. It comes when it comes.

That night Papa wrote the date inside the cover of her board book, in pencil, his hand not quite steady. Then he sat a while longer with the book open on his knee.
