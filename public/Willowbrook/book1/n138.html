Night 138

The Rainy Day Orchestra

Month 5: Nearly Sitting, Nearly Everything

Thunder at nine. The storm canceled the park, the walk, and the Tuesday trade, and by ten the house had the caged energy of a small zoo.

So Mama opened the low cupboard, and the orchestra was founded.

Three pots, turned over, were the drums. One wooden spoon went to the percussionist, who tested it first on the floor, bonk, then on the pot, BONG, then, discovery of discoveries, on the second pot, which went BANG, a different animal entirely. The metal bowl rang bright. The plastic bowl went thud. The whisk, held by the conductor, was mostly ceremonial.

The rain kept time on the windows. The thunder came in on the offbeat and was not invited back.

Twenty-two minutes it ran. At this age that is an opera.

The only work was the choosing. Nothing sharp, nothing glass, nothing with small parts, and lids matched to their pots for the coming age of putting things into things.

"Louder," said Mama, and it was.

The finale was all pots at once, then the spoon thrown, then the spoon retrieved and applied to the conductor's knee. The reviews were fair. Then a sudden slump, mid-clang, and Mama carried her up to the crib.

The rain played the second half alone.
