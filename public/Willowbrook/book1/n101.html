Night 101

The Kick Factory

Month 4: Rolling and Laughing

Kick. Jingle.

Kick, kick. Jingle, jingle.

On the mat under the play gym, Olivia had worked out weeks ago that the world above her could be made to ring, and she had been ringing it ever since. Both legs went like pistons. The felt star and the wooden ring swung on their strings like rigging in a storm, and she watched them go with the face of somebody whose plan is working.

The bath got the evening shift. Two inches of water, Mama's hand on her back, and the whole tub turned into a fountain. Spray on the wall. Mama's shirt soaked to the elbow, as it was every night now, the splash zone widening by the week like a small wet empire.

None of it is wasted. That alternating kick, one leg and then the other, is the same pattern crawling will use later, and walking after that. The strengthening runs all the way down the chain, hips, thighs, the small stubborn middle. And the best conditions cost nothing: floor time, bare legs, room to fire, something worth kicking hung in range. Bouncers and seats hold still the legs that the floor would put to work, so they get short turns, and then it is back to the mat.

Last shift of the day: the crib, lights low, two slow kicks under the sleep sack, then one, then none.
