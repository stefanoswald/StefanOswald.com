Night 314

The Snack Cup

Month 11: Mittens and the Early Dark

The snack cup has a lid with soft slits, so a hand can go in and the oat circles cannot come out on their own. Then one morning it became the most important thing at the counter.

Mama's rule was not to help. She filled it, set it down, and sat on her hands. Olivia turned it over first, because that is how you find out what a thing is. Nothing fell out. She looked at it, offended.

Then in went the hand, up to the wrist, and out came a fist with two circles and a third on its way to the floor, where the cat took delivery.

She fed herself for ten minutes without anyone touching the cup. The whole story of the snack cup, and bigger than it sounds. A hand that can go into a cup and come out with breakfast belongs to a person, not a passenger.

Halfway through she pointed past Mama at the fruit bowl, finger steady. The look said the rest.

"Banana? You want the banana."

The sign for more went with it, both hands patted together, the one she has used since November, done at the bowl as if the bowl might argue.

Banana was served, in pieces. One went into the cup for safekeeping. One went to Mama's mouth, held out with great ceremony, and eaten.
