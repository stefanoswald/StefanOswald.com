Night 111

The Dropping Game

Month 4: Rolling and Laughing

The spoon went over the side of the high chair, and Papa picked it up. The spoon went over the side, and Papa picked it up. The spoon went over the side, and you can see the shape of the evening.

By the ninth retrieval Papa had worked out his position. He was in the fourth month's great experiment, played in every high chair on earth, staffed by one small scientist and one large unpaid assistant.

It looks like mischief. It is physics, taken seriously. There is letting go, the deliberate opening of the hand, a skill newer than the grip. There is falling, always down, never up, confirmed across all known spoons. There is the sound, different for every object, a percussion section commanded from a chair with a strap on it.

And there is the part she tests most. The thing goes, and the assistant brings it back. Gone, then here. Peekaboo, in cutlery.

The assistant is allowed his limits. Three retrievals, then the toy on the tether takes over, or the business moves down to the floor, where the fall is shorter for everybody.

Drop ten ended the evening. She peered over the edge at the spoon, then at Papa, unbothered.

He picked it up. Of course he did. He set it on the tray and waited, and she considered the edge again, her hand already opening.
