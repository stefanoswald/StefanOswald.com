Night 55

The Almost Laugh

Month 2: Smiles and Sleep

It lived somewhere south of a coo and north of a squeal, and it came out when the world was exactly funny enough. The almost-laugh. A cough of joy. A hiccup with an opinion.

Papa got the first one with the puffed-cheek face, and reorganized his whole life around getting another.

The kisses on the belly: almost-laugh. The slow loom in, nose landing on nose: almost-laugh, with kicking. The sock puppet's tragic speech about being a lonely sock: nothing. A tough room. The eyebrow thing, where one eyebrow went up and the other stayed home: almost-laugh, almost-laugh, and a squeak that might have been new.

"She nearly laughed at the eyebrow thing," Papa said at dinner, a man describing a miracle.

"You've been doing the eyebrow thing at me for nine years," said Mama.

"And?"

"And I nearly laughed once."

The sock puppet was retired. The eyebrow thing went into the permanent collection, between the cheeks and the loom. Papa practiced it in the bathroom mirror, which Mama saw, and did not mention, and told Rosa about on the phone.

On the blanket, in the last of the light, Olivia looked up at the eyebrow going up and made the sound again, the small cough of it, and then lay there with her mouth open, waiting for the next one.
