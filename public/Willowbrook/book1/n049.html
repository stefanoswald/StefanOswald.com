Night 49

All-Weather Baby

Month 2: Smiles and Sleep

The sky was doing something between mist and rain, and the old Mama would have stayed in. The new Mama looked at the ceiling she had memorized, and dressed the baby.

There is a saying from the cold countries, where babies famously nap outdoors in every season: no bad weather, only bad clothes. It is truer for parents than for babies. A house can shrink around a person by the second month. Same walls, same laundry, same loop of thoughts going round. Weather is the cure. Daylight, even the gray kind, sets the baby's clock and the parent's mood, and somehow the gray kind does it best.

So: one more layer on Olivia than Mama wore herself, rain cover clicked over the stroller, umbrella up, and out.

The lane smelled like wet earth and new grass. The brook was running loud and important. Rain came down on the umbrella in the exact rhythm of every shush Mama had performed at two in the morning. Nature had invented white noise first. Mrs. Henderson waved from her porch across the street, with the look people give a parent pushing a stroller into rain, which is admiration with a question in it.

Twenty minutes, and home, damp at the edges and entirely repaired.

Olivia stared out from her dry cave the whole way, at the ceiling being sky.
