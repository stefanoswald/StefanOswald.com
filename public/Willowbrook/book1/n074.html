Night 74

Strong Enough to Look Around

Month 3: Rhythm

The wobble was leaving the tower. Three months of tummy-time press-ups had built something you could see now: a head that rose and stayed risen, and a neck that turned it, slow and sure, like a lighthouse.

Held sitting on Papa's lap, Olivia rode tall. The kettle clicked, and she found it. The screen door creaked, and she found that. Mama's voice came from the hall, and her head swung toward it, sound found, turned to, and taken in.

Underneath all of it is head control, the plain foundation stone. Nobody frames a photo of it, but everything to come sits on it. Sitting itself, a month or three away. The start of solid food, months off yet, which waits for exactly this steadiness. Every gymnastic from rolling to running.

It changed the days in practical ways. Carrying could go upright and world-facing now for stretches, her back against his chest, the town scrolling by like the world's slowest and best television. The play gym gained a sitting wing, with pillows on duty.

And tummy time, the old gym, went advanced: chest high, forearms planted, a small sphinx surveying her rug.

"Any news up there?" Papa asked the lighthouse.

She turned, found his face, and looked at him with such steady interest that he was the one who had to look away.
