Night 329

What They Told the Willow

Month 11: Mittens and the Early Dark

Before they went down, Mama did one more unplanned thing. She put a hand on the bark and told it the month.

"She let go of the couch," she told the willow. "Two steps, and down on her bottom, and she looked at her feet like they'd surprised her."

"The stairs. She holds the rail now. Three up, and down backward on her tummy, and she hates the turning around."

"She kept a sock for a week," Papa said. "One sock."

"And the bear moved to the shelf by the crib. Not in. By."

Olivia patted the bark and looked up into the branches.

"She fed herself from the snack cup and I sat on my hands. She did the spoon. Most of the yogurt is still in this hat."

"The dark at four o'clock," said Papa.

"I was cross at the dark, and she got some of it," Mama said. "I came and found her at the window."

"The molar. The cup with the mustache. The night she just wanted holding."

"Rosa in a scarf. She thought Rosa was a stranger till it came off."

Then the month was told, and the tree had all of it.

The willow let its gold down a little further, and the three of them turned for home in their own boot prints.
