Night 184

The Crib Down the Hall

Month 7: Explorers and Guardrails

The crib had stood at the foot of their bed since the night she came home. At least the first six months, the doctor had said, and longer was fine, and they had passed six months the week before.

The argument was whispered, as all their arguments were now. Papa snored. Olivia rustled. Mama woke at every rustle and Papa woke at nothing, and neither of them had slept a whole night in that room for half a year.

"She's ready," said Papa.

"You're ready," said Mama, which was true, and also not the point.

They carried the crib down the hall on Saturday, one at each end, Olivia on the rug watching a piece of furniture leave. The rules went with it and did not change. On her back to sleep, every time. The mattress flat and bare, no blanket, no bumper, no pillow, nothing soft in with her, the sleep sack instead. Bobby Bear on the dresser across the room, where he can see her and she cannot reach him, until she is one.

The monitor went on the nightstand, its small green light the only new thing in their bedroom.

She slept. Mama did not, much. Twice she walked the eleven steps down the hall to look at a baby who was fine.

The second time, Papa was already there.
