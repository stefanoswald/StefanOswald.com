Night 21

The Burp Symphony

Month 1: Arrival

Somewhere inside Olivia, after every meal, a bubble of air went looking for the exit and could not find the map. Papa became a musician. Up over the shoulder, cloth in place, and then the patting, firm as a friendly drum, gentle as everything else. Thump. Thump. Thump.

Some nights it was the sitting version instead: Olivia perched on his knee, chin cradled in his hand like a thoughtful old man, his other hand making slow circles on her back. Some nights the burp came quick and enormous, a sound so large from someone so small that both parents applauded. Some nights it never came at all, and that was fine. Not every meal traps a bubble, and a comfortable baby does not need to be patted forever.

The habit stayed, though. Meal, then a few quiet minutes upright, then bed. It kept the milk down and the spit-up off the third shirt of the day. Mostly.

This one arrived in the key of B flat, Papa decided, and he decided it out loud. "Bravo," he whispered. He bowed to the dark room. The audience was already asleep.
