Month 10 · First Snow

For the grown-up

Nights 274–304

This is the month the talking starts to show, mostly in the direction you cannot hear. She has a word, maybe two, and she follows forty. Say bath and watch her head for the hall. What she says is the small list. What she takes in is the real one.

The rest of her arrives with the words. A head shake at the spoon. A hat pulled off five times. Two fists on one block at the library. None of it is aimed at you. She is sorting out where she stops and the world starts, and no is her first way of being a separate person. Meet it as news, not mutiny, and it stays friendly for years.

Read these for the voice and the lap, not the plot. Make the animal sounds and let her supply one. When she hands the book back closed, read it again. That is how a book gets inside a person.

One thing to try: when she echoes the end of your sentence, answer as if she said a real thing. She did.

One thing not to worry about: how few words there are. Two at ten months is plenty, and none yet is inside the range. The fever and cold pages are back this month because December is when you need them. Read them once in daylight, so the first time is not three in the morning.
