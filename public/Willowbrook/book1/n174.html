Night 174

The Page Turner

Month 6: The Feast Begins

The nightly book got a co-operator this month, and the operation changed hands mid-story.

By week's end she took the book itself, hauled it from the basket, hoisted it across her knees, right side up by luck, and worked the pages solo in long sessions of what her parents could only call reading. The gaze traveled the spreads, the commentary was babbled at the pictures, one corner was sampled by mouth, and the whole volume was finally worn as a hat.

Book handling is reading's true first chapter. Before letters, before words, the object itself comes first: books open, pages turn, turning changes the picture, and the pictures hold still while the world does not. The chewed corner and the backward reading are the course itself. That is why board books are built like armor, and why the book is not rescued from its student.

The nightly reading adapted. Papa read whatever spread her turning landed on, the plot of the bear book experienced from now on in shuffle mode. Her own shelf, the lowest one, took its first three residents that week. She hauled them nightly.

"The bear went to the shop," read Papa, from the last page, since that was the page she had chosen. "The end. Also the beginning."

The hat slid down over one eye. He read on.
