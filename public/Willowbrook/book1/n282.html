Night 282

The Showing Point

Month 10: First Snow

Pointing had been an order slip all autumn. That cup. That duck. That cracker, now. The arm goes out, the grown-up fetches, and the world works.

At the park it turned out to have another use.

The oak was bare and the pond had a rind of ice along its edge, and the hedge, where a hedgehog was asleep somewhere and would not say where, had become a white shelf. Then, on the lowest branch of the oak, a cardinal. Red on white. The only red thing for a mile.

The arm came up, full lock.

And then she looked back. That was the signature on it, because she asked for nothing. She did not want the bird. She could not have had the bird. She had something in her head and she wanted it in Mama's head too, for no reason except the having of it together.

What a parent does with that is simple. Take the shared thing as shared. Look at the bird for as long as she is looking at it. Do not fetch anything.

"Bird," said Mama. "A red one."

They looked. The cardinal stayed exactly long enough, as cardinals do, and went.

The two of them went on looking at the empty branch a while, since that was where it had been.
