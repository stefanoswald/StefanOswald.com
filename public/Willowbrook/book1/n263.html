Night 263

All Done

Month 9: One Step, One Word

The bowl had been going on the floor for a month. Thrown in conclusion, never in anger. The meal was over, and the floor was how she said so.

So Mama had been saying all done, with the sign for it, both hands open and turned over, every time she cleared the tray. Weeks of it. More, the fists together, had come in the summer. All done, nothing. Mama began to suspect the sign was for her own benefit.

Tuesday supper. The window already black at half past five. Oatmeal, mostly gone, three peas moved around like chess. Olivia looked at the bowl. Looked at Mama. Opened both hands wide and turned them over, and made a face that meant, plainly: well?

The bowl stayed on the tray.

"All done!" said Mama, and her voice did something. "You told me. You're all done." She cleared the tray. Nothing hit the floor.

After that the signs came in a run. Milk arrived at bedtime, one fist squeezing. More got a sound with it, "muh," at the cheese, which is how words arrive, riding in on a hand. All done spread to the bath, the book, and the walk, where it was ignored.

Papa, told about it at the door, tried the sign himself and was handed a pea.

The floor under the high chair went a whole week as a floor.
