Night 170

The Two-Nap Treaty

Month 6: The Feast Begins

The catnap died quietly, the way small habits do: skipped once, unmissed, skipped twice, unmourned.

The late-afternoon third nap, the little bridge that had carried her to bedtime since June, had kept shrinking, then taken more than it gave. Taken, it shoved bedtime late into gravel. Refused, the evening somehow held anyway. The two remaining naps stretched and steadied meanwhile.

The two naps took over on a Tuesday, and the day ran like this. The morning nap moved to midmorning. The afternoon anchor sat in the early afternoon, the long keel of the day. Wake windows stretched to match, two and a half hours, sometimes three.

Bedtime came a half hour earlier all through the changeover, the usual bridge, since a baby dropping a nap runs short on sleep until the new shape settles. Some days needed the old third nap and some days refused it, and both were allowed without argument. Follow the baby, trim the edges, hold the anchor.

The nursery chalkboard was redrawn, two blocks where three had been. Nothing in ink. The chalk stayed in the tray beneath it, where the eraser lived.

The old catnap hour became the second walk, before the heat came up. Ninety minutes at the anchor now, in the fan's hum.

She slept through the lawnmower.
