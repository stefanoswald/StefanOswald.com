Night 38

The Breathing Check

Month 2: Smiles and Sleep

Every parent does it, and hardly anyone says so.

Papa woke at twenty to four for no reason, listened, heard nothing, and was up and across the room before he had decided to be. Hand on the crib rail. Eyes finding the dark.

There. Rise, and fall. Rise, and fall.

New babies breathe like jazz, not like marching. A quick run of breaths, then a rest, a pause of five seconds or even ten, then quick again. It has a name, periodic breathing, and it is normal, and it goes away on its own over the coming months. What earns a call to the doctor is different: a pause that runs well past that, at any hour, is a call, not a wait. Blue or gray at the lips or face, or a baby working hard to breathe, is the emergency number, that minute, not a call. The list on the refrigerator said so, and the list on the refrigerator was a kind of peace.

He stood a minute longer than the check needed. That part was not worry. That part was just looking at her, which has no medical name. Her mouth was open a little. One hand lay open on the mattress beside her head.

Rise, and fall. He went back to bed on the tide of it, and by the third rise he was asleep too.
