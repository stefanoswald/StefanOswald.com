Night 362

The Birthday Morning

Month 12: The Brook Comes Unstuck

She woke at six, and stood at the rail and told the room about it.

Both parents came in together. That was the only new thing, and she noticed it. "Happy birthday," they said, not quite in time, and she was lifted in four arms, and looked from one to the other for an explanation.

It was raining. The last of the snow was leaving down the gutters in a hurry, the yard was mud to the fence, and on the kitchen sill, in the three pots Mama had planted in the dark part of January, something green had come up overnight.

Pancakes, the same as every Saturday. The aunts, under orders, stayed upstairs till nine. Papa whistled the birthday song at the stove. It could have been any song. Mama did not ask.

Then the box on the floor, and inside it a wooden duck on wheels with a string. She pulled the string. The duck came. She pulled it again, walking backward, and the duck followed her down the hall and into the wall, and she said "duh," and pulled it out, and went again.

The star sweater went on over the pancake shirt, too big by a year.

"One," said Mama, to nobody, at the sink. "She's one."

Down the hall the wheels went on, and the wall, and the wheels.
