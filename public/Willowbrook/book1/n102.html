Night 102

Rubber Duck, Esquire

Month 4: Rolling and Laughing

The duck had arrived in the fruit bowl weeks before, a gift from Grandpa Joe, who said every baby was owed one duck and would not say by whom. Tonight, with Olivia's grip gone professional and Mama's hand at her back in the sloped tub, the duck was launched.

She got it. Both hands. Straight to the mouth for the usual test: rubber, wet, faintly squeaky under pressure. Approved.

Then came the discovery that would carry a month of baths. She leaned on it, and it went under, and came back. She pushed it under. Back it came, punctual, bobbing.

Olivia stared. Then she pushed it under again. Science.

The tub is the best room in the house for finding out how things behave. Water that pours. Splashes that answer kicks. A thing that floats, when everything else she has let go of has dropped. Mama's hand never left her, since a baby in water is never left, not for the length of a towel. The only housekeeping is the duck itself: toys that trap water inside grow dark secrets, so squeeze them dry after, or buy the sealed kind.

The washing took its usual ninety seconds, around the research.

Esquire, Papa called it, drying her off, and nobody ever called it anything else. The duck bobbed on in the emptying tub, wearing its one expression.
