Night 24

When the Crying Wins

Month 1: Arrival

There came a night when nothing worked. Fed, dry, burped, swaddled, shushed, swayed, and Olivia cried on, and on, and the walls leaned in, and Papa felt something hot and helpless rise in his chest.

The rule was for exactly this. A baby is never hurt by being laid down safe. She can be hurt by arms that have run out of calm. So he laid Olivia in her bare crib, on her back, crying and completely safe, and he walked out to the hallway and pulled the door most of the way shut and stood there breathing slowly for five minutes by the clock.

Never, ever shake a baby, no matter the hour, no matter the noise. Step away instead. Call someone. A partner, a neighbor, a grandmother, a friend, the nurse line on the fridge. Papa called down the stairs to Mama, asleep on the couch on her hours off, and she came up, and they stood in the hallway together listening to their daughter be furious in the next room.

When he went back in, he was Papa again, and not a storm wearing his shirt. He picked her up. He swayed. Babies always land eventually, and eventually she did.

"Sorry, love," he said into her hair. She slept, a warm weight against a shirt that was only a shirt again.
