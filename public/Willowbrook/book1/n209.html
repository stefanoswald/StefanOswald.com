Night 209

The Sick Week

Month 7: Explorers and Guardrails

The first cold of the fall came home in the third week, fever and cough and misery in full kit, and ran the house for six days.

The list under the lemon magnet was read every night and never crossed, though twice, at two in the morning, the breathing was watched by lamplight as it had been in March, the chest rising and falling, the count made. The nurse was called on the second day and the fourth, and the medicine was the one she named, in the amount she named, not a drop more.

Fluids in sips. The humidifier. The shifts, kept.

On the second night the spare mattress went down on the nursery floor beside the crib, because the monitor was not enough, and a parent lay there in the lamp's small circle, awake mostly, a foot from the bars. When she cried she was lifted and held sitting up in the chair, lamp on, until the crying stopped, then put back in the crib, on her back, to sleep. Every time. A tired parent holding a sick baby lies down alone.

The sixth morning came as it does. The walk down the hall braced for anything. And a baby at the bars, on her knees, thin and bright-eyed, yelling for breakfast.

A tooth had come through during it, unannounced.

The mattress went back on the spare bed.
