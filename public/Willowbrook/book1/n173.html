Night 173

The Sign for More

Month 6: The Feast Begins

The hands had been shown the sign for weeks. Mama's fingertips bunched and tapped together at every refill. "More? More."

This Tuesday, over sweet potato, the silence answered.

The bowl stood empty. Olivia regarded it, regarded Mama, and then, unmistakably, brought her own two hands together in front of her chest, patted them once, twice, and looked up with the face of somebody who has said a thing and expects the world to act on it.

More.

The world acted. The bowl came back full, and with it the great discovery of the season: I can put a thought in another head, and the head will move.

The hands run months ahead of the mouth. The wanting sits fully formed behind lips that cannot make a word yet, and that gap is where the season's shouting lives. Signing babies talk on time and often sooner, the signs helping the words along instead of standing in for them. The method costs nothing: make the sign as you say the word, daily, on faith, until the silence answers.

All done arrived within the fortnight, flung open-palmed like a small umpire. Mama cried a little at the swing, over a sign, and said it was the sun.

"More," said the hands at breakfast, at the swing, at the tenth reading of the bear book.

And the bowl came back full.
