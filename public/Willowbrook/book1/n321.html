Night 321

The Scarf Problem

Month 11: Mittens and the Early Dark

Everybody Olivia loves is wrapped to the eyes this month, and that turned out to be a problem.

It started on the errand walk. She was in the carrier on Mama's front, hat down to her eyes, looking out at a town of hats. At the library desk the librarian, who has been waved at every Thursday since the summer, leaned over in a wool hat and a scarf, and Olivia's face crumpled and went into Mama's coat.

"She's shy today," said the librarian, kindly, and pulled the scarf down, and Olivia looked back out, and stopped crying, and waved.

Not shy. A face is a whole face at this age, and a face with a hat and a scarf on it is somebody else. Take the scarf off and the somebody else turns into a friend right in front of her, and that is either alarming or a very good trick.

At the bakery it happened again. Rosa, in a hat. Rosa, unwrapping. Rosa. The wave, the flour cheek, the crust passed over the counter.

By the corner Mama had worked it out and was announcing everyone in advance. "That's Mrs. Henderson under there. Watch. She'll come out."

Mrs. Henderson came out of her hood like a tortoise and said hello.

Olivia looked at her for a long moment before the wave.
