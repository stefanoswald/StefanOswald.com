Night 348

The Thaw

Month 12: The Brook Comes Unstuck

The porch dripped. That was the first thing. Papa stood under the eaves with his coffee and got a drop on his glasses, and then another, and moved.

On the lawn the snow had gone to lace, with the grass coming through it brown, and along the walk it had shrunk back from the edges like a rug that had been washed wrong.

"Thaw!" called Mrs. Henderson from across the street, in the voice she uses for teething and frost. Her gold dog came out behind her and lay down in the one dry patch of her porch.

The party was two weeks off and indoors, so the weather could only get at the edges of it: the aunts' drive down from the north, the farm truck, the walk in from the cars. Papa wanted to end the day with a walk down the lane, everybody, as the three of them did most evenings, and Mama said they'd see.

"The brook's still stuck," he said. "I looked on the way to the bins. Ice at the bridge."

"Give it a week."

"It'll snow again before it's done. It always does one more."

They stood a while longer. Drip. Drip. Inside, on the monitor, Olivia said something to the ceiling, and they finished the coffee first.
