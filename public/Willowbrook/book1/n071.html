Night 71

Date Night, Population Three

Month 3: Rhythm

The reservation was for eight o'clock. The table was their own kitchen table, and the third guest was asleep in her crib in their room, the monitor standing in for her.

Somewhere out in Willowbrook there were restaurants, and someday they would sit in one again. But tonight the sitter question had no easy answer, the evening stretch was newly reliable, and the two of them were tired in a way restaurants cannot fix.

So they made the cheap magic instead. Real cooking, too many pans. Candles, because candles cost nothing and change everything. Phones exiled to the counter, face down. And one rule, made by Mama and kept imperfectly: for the first twenty minutes, no baby talk.

Not because Olivia was a poor subject. They had spent three months being somebody's parents and needed reminding that they were also this.

It creaked at first, the old conversation. Papa began a story from before and lost the thread of it. Mama found the thread and kept it. Then it caught, the way it always catches: some plan from after, laughing with their actual laughs and not their tired ones.

The monitor glowed between the candles and stayed quiet the whole evening.

They ate the good dessert slowly, all the way to the end, and neither of them stood up once.
