Month 7 · Explorers and Guardrails

For the grown-up

Nights 182–212

This is the month she leaves the rug. Not far, but by the end of it she can get to the hallway on her own, and the house you have lived in for years becomes a new house, seen from the floor.

So these stories are about two things at once: a baby learning to go, and two parents learning to let her, inside rooms they have made safe first. You will see them on their hands and knees checking outlets and cords, dropping small things through a cardboard tube, screwing a gate to the wall, and moving the cat's dinner. You will also see the harder half of going. She now knows you exist behind a closed door, and she minds. That is not a step back. It is how much world she has.

Read these as you have been reading, for the voice and not the plot. She will chew the corner of the book, and that counts. Keep the sounds, the pot lid, the clink, and skip anything that sounds like a question. Under one there are no questions, only company.

One thing to try: once this month, get down at her height and cross a room as she does. You will find one thing you missed.

One thing not to worry about: she cries when you leave the room now, and she stops when you come back. The coming back is the part she is learning.
