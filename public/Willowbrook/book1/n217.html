Night 217

Five A.M., Newly Permanent

Month 8: The Velcro Weeks and the Leaves

The night sleep, rebuilt since the fourth month's storm, grew a new feature in the eighth, and the feature was five in the morning.

It was not a wake-up in the old sense. There was no cry, no case to answer. It was a start time. The monitor delivered it daily: a rustle, the haul up the crib rail to standing, and the morning address beginning in the dark, like a rooster who had done graduate work.

The rail-haul was new, so the mattress went down to its lowest setting that same day, before the rail could become a step.

Bedtime was checked and held true, since too early a bedtime banks a full tank by five. And then came the rule the bench veterans deliver with their flattest smiles: some of it is the age, and some of it is simply her. Under the wave, every baby carries a factory setting. Hers, all evidence suggested, was dawn.

So five a.m. became a shift. One parent down with the rooster and the coffee, one banking sleep until seven.

"Lark," Papa told her at a quarter past five, over blocks, having said something less kind to the monitor ten minutes earlier and apologized to it.

"Ba!" said the lark, and flattened a tower.

The light came up on the two of them, eventually. It was pointed at.
