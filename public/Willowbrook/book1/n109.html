Night 109

Milk Is Still Dinner

Month 4: Rolling and Laughing

With the high chair at the table and an audience in it, a reasonable question knocked. Shouldn't she be eating something?

The aunts thought so, loudly. A little cereal in the bottle, for the sleeping. A taste of banana, for the fun. In their day babies ate at three months, and look how everyone turned out.

Mama looked at Papa. Papa looked at the potatoes.

But milk, breast or bottle, either or both, is not the appetizer of the first six months. It is the menu, all of it, and it is enough. Every calorie and every drop, made to her size and changed week by week. The early spoon takes more than it gives. It crowds out milk, asks a gut and a swallow that are not ready, and buys not one extra minute of night sleep. And cereal in the bottle is retired for good, choking among the reasons.

The six-month feast would come, with its signs. Sitting steady. The tongue no longer pushing everything back out. That look at the potatoes. It would be glorious and messy.

Until then, watching was the lesson and milk was dinner. Olivia finished hers at dusk, drunk as a lord.

The aunts, telephoned later, were told she had slept beautifully, and drew their own conclusions. By then she was asleep on Mama's shoulder, one hand still open on her shirt.
