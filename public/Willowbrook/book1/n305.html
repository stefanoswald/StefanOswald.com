Night 305

The Snowsuit

Month 11: Mittens and the Early Dark

The snowsuit came from the aunts, who plan ahead, and the first hard cold brought its first real fitting.

Getting a baby into a snowsuit takes two people and a rule: name every limb as it goes. "This arm. Now this arm. Where did the foot go?" Mama did the naming and Olivia did the going, mostly. One leg went into the wrong hole and came out again, unbothered.

Zipped, she stood in front of the mirror like a starfish that had been asked to stand. Both arms out. Both arms stuck out. She turned to look at the mirror baby, who was wearing one too, and the two of them considered each other.

Then the hat. The hat is a separate treaty. It went on, came off, went on, and stayed, because the mitten strings were more interesting to pull.

A baby in a snowsuit does not fall so much as tip, slowly, and lie there looking up at whoever did this to her.

At the car the whole suit came off again. That rule came with the puffy coat in August: straps snug over thin clothes, never over a puffy suit, and the suit laid over the top of her like a blanket.

She was asleep before the corner. Two arms out, still.
