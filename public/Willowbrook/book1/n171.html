Night 171

Her Own Cupboard

Month 6: The Feast Begins

The kitchen at supper hour produced a problem. She sat where she was put now, and where she was put was the floor by the stove, and the floor by the stove is where every cabinet door in the world lives. Mama cooked one-handed for a week.

The house solved it the old way. Give one cupboard away entirely. The low one by the sink, emptied of anything sharp or glass, refilled with pots, lids, wooden spoons, a funnel, three steel bowls. Hers, new again every evening.

Each supper hour she was set down in front of it, and she hauled the door wide and got to work. She banged the lids. She nested the bowls wrongly with total conviction and wore the funnel briefly as a hat. Pots above and pots below rang in rough ensemble, and supper got cooked with both hands for the first time since July.

Babies do not want the toys. They want the verbs. A shelf of hers in every room the family truly lives in beats any toy corner exiled from the action.

The cat left the kitchen at the first lid and did not return until dessert. The cupboard was repacked every night, wrongly, according to a sitter with opinions.

The funnel never recovered its dignity.
