The Velcro Weeks

Month 8: The Velcro Weeks and the Leaves

The eighth month arrived with hands, and the hands were closed on Mama's leg.

Olivia clung now. To the leg at the stove. To the shoulder at the bench. To the collar at every put-down. The cupboard was visited only in company. Some evenings even Papa would not do. Only the leg, and the leg had dinner to make.

None of this is spoiling, and none of it is a comment on the traded Tuesdays. It is love, newly aware of what it could lose, holding on with both fists.

On Wednesday, with the onions catching and the leg pulled at, Mama said "Not now, Olivia," in a voice she did not like. She let one long breath out through her nose, and then she sat down on the kitchen floor beside the wailing and said, "Sorry. Here I am. You'd rather come along, I know."

The carrier came out of the closet after that. The barnacle became a passenger. Both hands went back to the dinner, and every trip across the kitchen was announced. "Mama's going to the sink. Watch me go. Here I am."

The onions were started again. The soup was made one-legged and eaten the same way, with a small warm weight against the shin under the table.
