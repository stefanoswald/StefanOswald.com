Night 92

Both Directions Now

Month 4: Rolling and Laughing

Tummy to back, back to tummy, and again. Olivia had put the two halves of rolling together, and now she could go somewhere. Across the stripes of sun on the rug like a small pleased log, all the way to the low shelf, where she pulled down a magazine and chewed it.

The cat on the couch watched without moving anything but its ears.

Rolling both ways changes the house. The floor is hers now, all of it. So Papa got down on his knees and crawled the living room, seeing what she saw. A lamp cord hanging within reach. A tote bag with a long strap by the chair. And under the couch, a button off a coat, small, round, just the size of a mouth that tastes everything. It went into his pocket, and he sat a moment, holding it.

Two rules came in that week and stayed. Nobody's hand leaves her on the changing table, not for one second, and on the wild days the diaper happens on the floor. And the play yard, which had looked like a cage in the box, turned out to be a safe place to set her down while the kettle boiled or the door was answered.

She rolled to Papa's feet and took his slipper in both hands.

"Welcome to the mainland," he said. She was already rolling away.
