Night 232

The October Sun

Month 8: The Velcro Weeks and the Leaves

The days had gone cold and bright, with the sun low enough to come straight in the window at breakfast, and the going-out routine grew two layers and kept one.

The hat came first, wool now instead of straw. The jacket came second, zipped.

Then the sunscreen, because a low sun is still the sun, and October is not a permission slip. Olivia was older than six months now. Mama chose a broad-spectrum, water-resistant sunscreen and followed the label. A mineral formula with zinc oxide or titanium dioxide was gentle on her skin. She covered the places clothing did not: cheeks, ears, nose, and the backs of two busy hands.

The application took eleven minutes because Olivia wanted to eat the process. By next summer, Olivia would hold out her own arms.

Sunscreen was only one layer of protection. Shade still came first. So did the hat, long sleeves, and avoiding the fiercest midday sun. Mama reapplied about every two hours and after any swimming or heavy sweating, following the bottle's directions rather than trusting a single morning coat to last all day.

At the park, Olivia sat under the oak with the hat slightly crooked. The sun warmed her cheeks. It did not get to burn them. That was the arrangement.
