Night 42

The Black and White Gallery

Month 2: Smiles and Sleep

The gallery opened on the living room rug, and every piece in it was black and white. Stripes. A checkerboard. A spiral that meant business. Mama propped the cards eight or ten inches from Olivia's face, and Olivia went still, arms paused mid-wave, staring with her whole body.

New eyes are hungry for edges. Color is still warming up in the second month, so the loudest thing you can show a baby is contrast, black on white. Every hard edge she studies is work for the seeing parts of her brain, and she does it gladly for about four minutes, and then she is done, and done means done.

Mama lay on her stomach beside her and looked at the spiral too. It was not a bad spiral. Papa came through, said it was giving him a headache, and was ignored by both of them.

The gallery had a second wing, better than any card: faces. The dark of eyes, the line of a hairline, the mouth that moves and makes the sounds. Faces are a baby's favorite pattern in the world. So the show ended as the best ones do: cards down, Mama's face in close, and the brand-new smile turned on it.

Olivia held the look as long as she could. Then her eyes slid shut, and Mama stayed where she was on the rug.
