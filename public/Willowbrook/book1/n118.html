Night 118

Three Toys at a Time

Month 4: Rolling and Laughing

The toys had multiplied. Gifts, hand-me-downs, rattles bought in weak moments, until the play mat looked like a prosperous market, and Olivia, set down in the middle, played with none of it. She batted one thing, dropped it for the next, skimmed six toys in four minutes, and fussed, a shopper lost in her own shop.

So Mama tried the old trick. Three toys on the mat. The wooden ring, the crinkle block, the standing mirror. Everything else went into the basket, the basket onto the shelf, the shelf out of sight.

It worked the same morning. With the market shrunk to a gallery, Olivia settled on the ring and worked it. Turned it. Mouthed it. Passed it from one hand to the other, dropped it, found it, and began again. Eleven minutes on one thing, and at four months eleven minutes is an era.

Fewer choices bought deeper attention. Grandmothers have always known it. Swap the three every few days, and the old toys come back as new ones. The crinkle block, returned after a week in the basket, was received like a ship home from sea.

Novelty, made out of nothing, free.

The rest stayed on the shelf, getting more interesting by the day. Olivia stayed on her mat with the wooden ring, turning it and turning it, and did not look up once.
