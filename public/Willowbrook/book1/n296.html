Night 296

The Fever Rules, Again

Month 10: First Snow

The cold had one more thing to say, at three in the morning.

Hot against Mama's shoulder. The thermometer said so, and the thermometer is the one that gets believed, not a hand on a forehead.

"She's warm, she's fine, come back to bed," said Papa from the door.

"Don't."

It came out sharp, and the room went still.

"Sorry. I'm frightened. Get the list."

He got the list.

Any rectal temperature of 100.4 degrees Fahrenheit, 38 Celsius, or higher in a baby three months old or younger requires an immediate call. Older babies have different thresholds, and the number for her age was on the list in the doctor's handwriting, and no one guesses it at three in the morning. The number was over it. They called.

Hard breathing, a rash that does not fade when pressed, unusual limpness or difficulty waking, repeated vomiting, refusing feeds, too few wet diapers, or hours of inconsolable crying all mean calling promptly, and so does a parent's sense that something is seriously wrong. She had none of those.

The nurse said light clothes, frequent fluids, and the correct weight-based dose of medicine only if she was miserable. No cold bath. No heavy blankets. Call back for any of the rest.

By morning it was falling. The list went back under the lemon magnet. Neither of them went back to bed.
