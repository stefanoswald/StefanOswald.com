Night 105

Grandparents' Day

Month 4: Rolling and Laughing

Grandpa Joe held Olivia with the terrible ease of a man who has raised two babies and forty springs of lambs, and the farmhouse table wore more food than three grown-ups could get through, since farm kitchens cook in proportion to love.

He did not say much. He never does. He held her so Mama could eat with two hands, hummed her something with no words, and let her gum the brim of his hat.

The rules bent, the way they are meant to at a farm. Her perfections narrated to the chickens, at length, by Papa.

What did not bend was the safety floor, and nobody had to say so. When she dissolved into yawns after lunch, Papa carried in the travel crib, and it was Grandpa Joe who set it up in the cool back room, nothing in it but the fitted sheet, laid her down on her back, and stood a moment, one hand on the rail. When they drove the quarter mile to see the neighbor's new foal, the car seat got buckled for the quarter mile, and he checked the strap himself.

Three of them drank coffee in the kitchen while the fourth slept. Not much was said.

"Sundays," he said at the gate. It had already been decided by everyone, including the chickens.

He touched Olivia's foot once, and let them go.
