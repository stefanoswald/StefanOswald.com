Night 197

Soup for the Strikers

Month 7: Explorers and Guardrails

The eleventh day of the strike was the worst, so it is the one the book keeps.

No morning nap. The afternoon one ran forty minutes and was ended by the recycling truck. Mama had been the whole show since six, and at dusk she was at the window doing the bounce with a baby who had turned down milk, the ring, the spoons, and comfort itself.

Papa read it from the doorway. The stillness, the bounce gone ragged. He crossed the room and did it in one move, lifted Olivia out of her arms mid-bounce, kissed the cheek that was left, and said the only sentence that has ever helped at that hour.

"I've got her. Soup's on in ten."

He did not ask how the day was. Asking, at that hour, makes the tired person talk. Lifting is help. He said nothing about the day either. The day was the striker's doing, and the one who held it has nothing to answer for.

Mama was nowhere for an hour. A bath with the door shut, the September dark coming early at the small window, the house's noise muffled and somebody else's. She came down to soup and a fed baby and Papa on the floor with the wooden spoons, losing.

"True number?" he said.

"Four," said Mama, which was up from two, and ate.
