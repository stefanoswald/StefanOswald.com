Night 292

The Winter Cold

Month 10: First Snow

The cold came home from the library rug, as colds do, and by Saturday the whole house was breathing through its mouth.

The kit was the May kit. Saline drops and the bulb before feeds and before sleep, which she fought hard. Extra feeds, in sips if sips were all she would take. Upright cuddles by day, on a parent who was awake. A clean cool-mist humidifier on the dresser. Nothing off a shelf, no cough or cold medicine at this age, only what the nurse named, in the amount she named. Honey would wait until after her first birthday.

At night the crib, on her back, flat, the mattress not propped, because propped is not allowed no matter the nose.

The list stayed on the fridge and got read. Hard or fast breathing, bluish lips, unusual sleepiness, trouble waking, repeated vomiting, poor feeding, or too few wet diapers. Any of those meant calling promptly, and the blue lips meant the emergency number. So did the quiet inner alarm that says something is not right. She had none of it. She had a nose, and an opinion about the bulb.

"True number?" Papa asked Mama at midnight, in the hall.

"Three."

"Go to bed."

By the fourth day she was stealing the bulb and offering it to the bear.
