Night 33

The Conversation

Month 2: Smiles and Sleep

"Ah-goo," said Olivia.

"You don't say," said Papa. And then he waited.

The waiting was the trick, and he had only found it that week. Answer, then stop. Eyebrows up, floor open. Olivia gathered her whole small self, kicked twice, and produced another sound, longer, with a bend in the middle of it.

"Go on," said Papa.

That is all a conversation is, at the start. She makes a sound, somebody answers, then leaves a gap for her to fill. A baby's brain grows on turns the way a garden grows on rain, not on words poured over it but on being answered. Every returned coo tells her the same thing: you said something, and somebody heard.

So the house filled with talk that made no sense from the hall. Papa held long vowel arguments with her about the laundry. Mama discussed the rain with someone who had never been out in any. Both sides were treated with the greatest respect.

"Ah-goooo," Olivia finished, with a small fist for emphasis.

"You win," said Papa, and carried the argument upstairs, where it went on above the crib in the dark, quieter and quieter. Papa gave out first.
