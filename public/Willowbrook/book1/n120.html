Night 120

What the Fireflies Told the Moon

Month 4: Rolling and Laughing

On June nights the fireflies rise late, after the porches have gone quiet, and they carry the day up with them.

Up they went from the lawns of Willowbrook, slow sparks, past the hedges, past the roof of the little house on Maple Lane, where one lamp had just gone out. They rose past Stella, who had dipped low over that porch the way she does, and they told her what they had seen. A baby on a swing, reaching, and reaching again. Nothing caught, and laughing anyway.

Stella did not say anything. She twinkled once, and settled lower, and kept the porch.

Higher up, the Sleepy Moon lay on its back with its lashes closed, the way the babies of Willowbrook are laid down, and the fireflies blinked their small news at it in the old code.

And the Sleepy Moon hummed.

Below, the town tucked itself in, street by street. A fan turned in one dark room. The fireflies sank back toward the grass.

Goodnight, Willowbrook.
