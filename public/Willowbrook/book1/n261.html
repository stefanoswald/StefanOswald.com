Night 261

The Wind

Month 9: One Step, One Word

The wind came up at dusk and took the rest of the leaves in an hour. The walk was called off. Wind that brings branches down is not walking weather, and the porch chairs came in.

The first big gust frightened her, the house making a noise she had never heard it make. So Papa carried her to the window and they watched it together, his calm running under her like the floor. He gave it the interested voice: "Wind. Big wind. Look at the leaves go. Whoosh."

The startle at the first bang of the gate got ridden out inside two steady arms. She looked up to read him, and read: big. Not bad. By the third gust she was pointing at the leaves past the streetlamp like breaking news, and by the fourth she had a sound for it, a long breathy hoo that was either wind or owl.

Papa tried to whistle along with it. The wind won on the first note. This has been noted before.

The routine ran straight through the weather. Bath, drummed by rain. Book, against the roar. Song, the wrong notes holding their own against the sky.

In the crib she said hoo at the ceiling, sleepily, and the wind said it back.

By morning the maple was bare and the lane was all leaves.
