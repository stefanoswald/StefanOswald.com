Night 225

The Brush and the Banana Phone

Month 8: The Velcro Weeks and the Leaves

The copying took up two new subjects this month.

The brush came first. The hairbrush, liberated from the dresser in a routine raid, went not to the mouth, for once, but to the head. Her own head. Brushed, after a fashion: bristles backward half the time, the stroke more salute than grooming, the intent unmistakable. This is what the tall ones do with this object, and I am of the tall ones' tribe.

The brushing spread within the week. To Mama's head, gently. To the bear's head, thoroughly, the flopped ear getting the full salon. To Papa's beard, a professional challenge, met.

The telephone came second, and it was the month's finest theater. Anything of roughly the sacred size, the toy phone, the remote, one wooden block, a banana at breakfast, was pressed to the ear, and the call began. Long chains of babble in the exact tune of telephone talk. Pauses held for the other party. The head nodding. And the sign-off: the object looked at, then dropped, as every call she had ever witnessed had ended.

Everything done in front of her is a lesson. The parents' whole art is to do the world slowly, at watchable range.

The dinner call, taken on the banana, ran two full minutes. Whoever was on the line got the whole story.
