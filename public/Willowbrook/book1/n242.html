Night 242

Papa Tells It Backward

Month 8: The Velcro Weeks and the Leaves

The kitchen notebook lay open between them, the kettle ticking as it cooled. Papa offered to tell the month back. Mama let him, which was her mistake.

"It started," said Papa, "with the picnic."

"It ended with the picnic."

"And before that she stood up by herself for a minute."

"Thirteen seconds."

He went on. The no that stopped her hand three inches from the cord, then four. The ten minutes on the floor with the rings. The festival from his shoulders, and the balloon that slept beside the bear. The dark coming early, the five things in order through it. The tractor named every night. Where's Papa's nose. The banana phone. The table wiped twice. The shoes by the door, for outside only, worn mostly off.

"And the balloon at the farm," said Mama, "which you keep leaving out."

"She kept it four months. Grandpa Joe said 'Well now.'"

The big tub, the same hand within reach. The wagon, the couch, the way down backward. Uh-oh before the spoon. The oat circle between one thumb and one finger. The sweet potato refused and the plate left alone. Three meals. Five in the morning. The sitter's notes. The other lap.

"And the door," Papa said, in order at last. "Kiss, wave, window."

"Where she waits for you now."

He closed the notebook. The kettle had stopped ticking.
