Night 252

One Word for Everything

Month 9: One Step, One Word

The week after the pond, the house ran on one word, and the word did every job.

"Duck," at the bathtub, pointing. An order: bring Esquire, the bath is not complete.

"Duck," at the bear book, all business: the mallard page, you know the one.

"Duck!" at the window, both arms, breaking news: a bird, any bird, a crow on the fence.

"Duck?" at the empty pond on Thursday, palms up, rising. A question, an absence noticed, a whole sentence of where did they go.

One word and the body's whole grammar. The job on the other end is building it out: "Duck! Yes, the ducks went into the reeds. It's cold. Where did the ducks go?" She banks the long version, months before she can say it.

Under it the babble kept widening, ba and da and ga, a new m, a wet b that sprayed. Papa answered all of it as if it were news.

He tried to teach her ball, on the grounds that he had a ball. She looked at the ball and said duck. He accepted this.

The house had understood her for months, so the word carried no news. It carried company: the world named aloud, together, both ends talking at last.

"Duck," Olivia offered at lights-out, at the ceiling, in her settling voice.

"Duck," Mama agreed, and turned the lamp.
