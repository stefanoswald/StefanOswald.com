Night 46

Grandpa Joe Comes to Town

Month 2: Smiles and Sleep

Grandpa Joe drove in from Honeywell Farm with two dozen eggs, a rocking chair he had fixed for the occasion, and his own way of visiting, mostly silence with a hat on it.

Muddy boots on the step. Hands at the sink, without being asked. Forty years of livestock teaches a man what hands carry. Then he held out his arms, took Olivia, and settled into the armchair with his hat on his knee.

"Sleep," he said. "Both of you."

Mama opened her mouth to say she would do the dishes first. Grandpa Joe looked at her. She did not do the dishes.

The best visitors hold the baby so the parents can be people for an hour. The other kind hold the baby so the parents can make them coffee. Families are allowed to tell the difference out loud, and to ask the first kind back.

Mama meant to go up to bed. She got as far as the couch. Papa meant to shower. He got as far as the other end of the couch. Grandpa Joe sat with Olivia asleep against his flannel and looked at the ceiling for two hours, and did not fidget, and did not once check the time.

When she stirred, she found his enormous finger and held it.

"Well now," said Grandpa Joe, very quietly, so as not to wake anyone.
