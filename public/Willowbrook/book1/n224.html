Night 224

The Push Wagon Debut

Month 8: The Velcro Weeks and the Leaves

The cruising along the couch, new and coast-hugging, took delivery this month of its first ship for open water. The push wagon. Wooden, the handle at collarbone height, weighted with a bag of rice in the hold.

It was launched on the living room's open middle, that unclaimed floor the couch and the coffee table had eyed all month from their shores. She pulled up at the handle. She gripped. She leaned. And the wagon, traitor and liberator at once, moved.

One step chased it. Then another. The face at the far wall, when the voyage docked there, was lit at a wattage nobody tried to estimate.

The wagon was tuned for the trade: weighted against running away, socks with grips or feet bare, the rug edges flattened along the shipping lane, and the turn at the wall taught early, since the turn is what makes a crossing into a round trip.

By the end of the month it ran daily. Cargo was loaded and hauled between rooms: blocks, the bear, one sock in permanent transit. The wagon lent only its handle. The legs did the rest, and were allowed to believe the wagon was doing the carrying.

Rumble, rumble, rumble, went the wheels across the boards. Bump, went the wall. Turn. Rumble, rumble, rumble.
