Night 137

What Rosa Knows

Month 5: Nearly Sitting, Nearly Everything

Porch, shade, two strollers, both babies asleep, and the coffee gone honest. Some afternoons are what the village is for.

Rosa had been at this eleven days longer, and she handed over what she knew unranked, unpushed, like plates at a potluck.

That the sleep collapse she had dreaded for Lily came and went in twelve days, and the dreading cost more than the collapse. That her own mother's advice ran thirty percent gold and seventy percent nineteen-eighty-something, and the sorting got easy once she stopped resenting it: safety from the doctor, soothing from the grandmothers, sanity from the bench.

That freezer dinners made in one Sunday hour had saved more evenings than any philosophy. That the guilt kept an hour at her house too, hers in the shower with the water running, and that saying it out loud to another mother cut it in half.

"Nobody's doing it right," Rosa said. "Everybody's doing it over. The ones who look finished are just farther from their last bad week."

Mama carried that one home and turned it over for days, like a river stone.

The babies slept through every word, as they slept through every Tuesday.

The strollers stirred at five. Both mothers stood, and the talk came down the porch steps with them and went on half the length of Maple Lane.
