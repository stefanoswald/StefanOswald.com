Night 180

In Two Hands

Month 6: The Feast Begins

Month's end, the window open on the August dark, a candle lit for no occasion, Olivia asleep on Papa's shoulder, the notebook down from beside the kettle.

Mama read it straight. The lines were short. The month had left no room for long ones.

Cereal. Four spoonfuls. Fifth in bib.

Gag. Fine. Grinned.

Peanut, on cheek.

Broccoli. One ear.

Water. Down chin.

TOOTH, boxed.

Twice her birth weight. Halfway.

Sat, hands free. Fell. Sat.

Ring over the edge. Looked.

Rolled to the ring. Backward under the chair. Roared.

Hall chase. Papa lost.

Puff, pinched, mostly.

Library. Echo.

More, with her hands.

Fever. Gone by breakfast.

Papa's capitals for the tooth and the chase, Mama's slant for the rest, the fever line tilted, one-handed, two in the morning.

"It hasn't got the faces," Papa said. Green beans, betrayed. Peach, needing a moment alone.

"Or the gag." Four seconds, Mama's hands in her lap, doing nothing, the hardest thing.

"Or the fever night." The turns. The one not holding her pretending to sleep.

And the line nobody wrote. Three eaters, one painting. Two people who had stood here at three in the morning not speaking Baby were a family that mostly knew what it was doing.

Papa blew the candle out for her, since she was asleep. Mama added a line, the faces, ask us, and left it to dry.
