Night 262

The First Frost

Month 9: One Step, One Word

The morning after the wind, the fence was white.

Mama saw it from the kitchen. The hat, the mittens, one more layer than she wore herself, and out.

A baby loses heat faster than a grown-up, so the outing was short, ten minutes and in, and her hands and cheeks were checked twice on the way. The mittens, which were an outrage, stayed on because they were clipped.

The grass crunched. That was the first news. The puddle by the gate had a skin of ice, and Mama tapped it with her boot and it cracked into plates, and the plates were the second news. Then the rail. Mama held her up to it and she laid the mitten flat on the white and pulled it back and looked at it, and laid it on again, and the white went to water.

Across the street Mrs. Henderson called "First frost!" the way she had once called "Teething!", and both of them looked at the porch mums, gone black overnight.

Her breath came out in a cloud and she tried to catch it. Papa's, when he came out with the coffee, was bigger, and she tried to catch that too.

Inside, the mittens came off and the hands were warmed in Mama's, one at a time.

By nine the rail was wet and plain.
