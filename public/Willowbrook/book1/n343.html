Night 343

The Chair

Month 12: The Brook Comes Unstuck

The yard at Honeywell Farm had turned to mud, and the boots stayed by the workshop door. Inside, under a sheet, there was something the size of a dog.

Grandpa Joe took the sheet off. He had rehearsed it. You could tell.

A chair. Small oak, low to the ground, the seat the width of one Olivia and no more, and on the back rail, cut with a knife and sanded until it shone, one star.

"Well now," he said.

Olivia put a hand on the seat. She looked at him. He looked at the chair.

"Sit," he said.

She did not sit. She pushed. The chair went across the workshop floor on its four small feet with Olivia behind it, walking, and stopped against the leg of the bench, and she pushed it back.

Grandpa Joe watched a chair he had made over forty winter evenings become a wagon in under a minute. He did not seem surprised. He moved a can of nails out of its road.

Papa, at the door, started to say something about growing into it. Grandpa Joe held up one finger, and Papa stopped.

The chair went round the workshop three more times. Then Grandpa Joe opened the door, and the chair and Olivia went out into the yard, slowly, toward the mud, and he went after them with the sheet.
