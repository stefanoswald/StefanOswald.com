Night 200

Two Hundred Nights

Month 7: Explorers and Guardrails

Papa drew the two-hundredth pencil star on the kitchen calendar after the book, and did not make a speech about it, which for Papa is a kind of speech.

The hundredth had marked a habit becoming a place. This one marked the place being lived in. Somewhere in the second hundred the routine had stopped being done to her and started being done with her.

At the word bath she crawled toward the bathroom, the routine's first responder. The sleep sack, once an outrage, was entered arms first like a coat she owned. The book was held by both parties and the pages turned by whoever got there first. And under the song, the wrong-noted one Papa has sung since April, there was a new thing this month, a low tuneless sound riding along, which the kitchen notebook called humming and Mama called singing and Papa called harmony.

Bath, quiet, book, song, lights out. The same short order, every night and not most nights, since the thirty-sixth. Through a strike, a rainy week, a fort, and the moving of the crib, it had held. Moved earlier, sung through gravel, but held. The power of the thing was never one night. It was the two-hundredth night knowing the shape of the one before it.

Night two hundred ran eleven minutes and was entirely ordinary.

She hummed. The lamp went off.
