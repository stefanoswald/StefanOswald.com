Night 353

The First Maybe

Month 12: The Brook Comes Unstuck

It snowed in the night, one more time. In the morning, at breakfast, the open cup of milk went over, the whole cup, across the table and into Olivia's lap.

"Uh-oh," said Olivia, and then her face went, and she cried.

"Oh, for-" said Mama, in the voice she does not like, and heard it, and stopped. She took one slow breath and got down to the chair.

"That was my cross voice. The milk isn't your fault. Uh-oh is right, it went everywhere."

They mopped. They changed the trousers. Because they mopped they were late, and because they were late they were at the corner when the snowplow came down Maple Lane, the big one with the yellow light, throwing snow off its blade in a long white wing.

The driver lifted a hand and tapped the horn twice.

Olivia waved back with her whole arm and said "duh," which was the wrong word and the right amount of it.

At the lamp that night Mama told her the day back, the cup and the mop and the plow.

"So was the milk bad?"

"Maybe," said Mama. She had never said it like that before. "Maybe it was. We don't know the whole story yet."

"Maybe?" said Papa, from the hall.

"Maybe."

She said it once more on the stairs, to herself, trying it.
