Night 355

The Aunts Return

Month 12: The Brook Comes Unstuck

The aunts came down from the north ten days early, four of them in one car, over roads that had been white on Thursday and were mud by the weekend, and the guest room got its people.

They came in talking. They stopped talking at the door, all four, because they had read the same book Mama had, and they knelt on the mat and waited.

Olivia stood against Mama's legs with a handful of cardigan and looked at them. She had seen them in August and on a screen, which is not four of them in a hall in coats.

"Give her the boots," said Mama. "She goes for laces."

The youngest aunt undid a bag instead and held out a sweater. Small, grey, knitted in the north through the dark part of the winter, and on the chest one gold star.

"One a year," said the aunt. "Until she tells us to stop."

"She won't."

"Then we'll run out of chest."

Olivia let go of the cardigan. She did not go to the sweater. She went to the boots and worked at a lace, and by supper she was in the oldest aunt's lap with the star sweater on over her pajamas, too big by a year on purpose.

At the rail she said "nigh-nigh" to the room. In the guest room the needles had already started.
