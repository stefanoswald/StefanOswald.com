Night 212

The Oak Tells the Willow

Month 7: Explorers and Guardrails

News moves through Willowbrook by its own weather. In June the fireflies carried it. In September it goes by leaf.

The big oak on Maple Lane let go of one more leaf that night, a yellow one, and the wind took it up over the roofs and up the hill, turning, and laid it at the foot of the Great Willow, whose branches were just gold at the tips, as they are after dusk.

The willow does not read. It listens. It heard: a hallway crossed in four seconds. A door, cried at, and opened. A crib carried down a hall. Two steps up, a hand on a back. A hedgehog with a leaf, going the other way. A palm on a glass, forty miles from another palm. Two hundred nights of the same shape.

Stella came low to hear the rest. The Sleepy Moon leaned in over the rooftops, lashes shut, and hummed, once.

Below, on Maple Lane, the porch lights went off one by one. In one window the small right darkness held: the crib against the wall, the bear on the dresser across the room, a mouth open with a new tooth in it, and a sock, somewhere, under the couch.

Under the fence a hedgehog turned round twice in the leaf pile and settled.

Stella twinkled once, for yes.
