Night 126

The Circus Sleeps Anyway

Month 5: Nearly Sitting, Nearly Everything

The wedding was lovely. Olivia was danced with by four aunts in turn, applauded for existing, and asleep on Papa's shoulder by the cake.

Then the guest room. The travel crib stood in a strange corner, nothing in it but the sheet, and the room smelled of elsewhere. The white-noise sea shushed loyally, but Olivia, laid down at the usual hour after the usual steps, surfaced at once and objected to all of it, item by item, at volume.

It took ninety minutes. Three encores of the song. One unplanned feed. A hallway walk in wedding clothes, Mama in one shoe. This is how first nights away go, and it says nothing about the sleeper or the parents. Elsewhere is loud, in a pitch only babies hear.

"Whose idea was a wedding," Papa said to the hallway, at two.

"Your cousin's."

And then she slept. In the wrong corner, in the wrong room, in the portable sea, she slept, on her back, one fist up by her ear. The steps had traveled after all. The second night away, whenever it came, would run at half the circus.

Morning came in sunny. Olivia woke delighted with the new ceiling. The aunts had breakfast on. They drove home at noon with the suitcase refusing to shut and everyone in the car asleep but the driver.
