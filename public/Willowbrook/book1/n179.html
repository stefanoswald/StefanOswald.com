Night 179

Chasing the Light

Month 6: The Feast Begins

All month the walks had chased the light to its edges: early, before the heat came up, and late, after it let go. The middle of an August day was for the fan and the floor. So the evening walk became the day's second outing, after supper, the stroller's passenger tracking every porch.

Daylight is the dose. The small clock in her winds on it, and the night's sleep organizes itself around days that contain actual day. And the weather inside the parent lifts with every hour spent outdoors. The vitamin drops from the doctor stayed on the counter regardless, sun or no sun. The hat went on. The shade went up.

Then, on the last Thursday of the month, the air had an edge in it.

Nothing much. A thin coolness under the warm, the cicadas quieter, the light going a little sooner than it had the week before. Mama felt it on her arms at the corner and stopped, and reached into the basket under the stroller, and the light blanket went over Olivia's legs for the first time since June.

The streetlights woke one by one down the lane. Home through the blue, the last lamp theirs.

Olivia had one hand out from under the blanket, feeling the new air.

Mama tucked it in. It came back out.
