Night 196

The Nap Strike

Month 7: Explorers and Guardrails

The morning nap, which had worked since June, stopped working on a Monday.

Dim room, sack, song, on her back in the crib, hand on her chest, the leaving. Before Mama reached the door there was a roll, a push, and a baby up on her hands and knees at the bars, hollering, a one-baby picket line.

Some days she folded mid-holler and slept where she landed. She goes down on her back every time, and what she does after is her own business now that she can roll both ways. Other days there was no nap and the afternoon paid for it.

On the fourth day Mama said "Fine. Don't," in a voice she did not like, and shut the door, and stood in the hall with her hand on the frame and let one long breath out. Then she went back in.

"That wasn't fair. You're not tired the way I'm tired."

The strike is the new skill, hands and knees, practiced at the moment she is asked to put it away. It is also a person finding out she can want things, including not-this. The routine did not change. Mama went in every few minutes, a hand on the back, the same six words, out again.

It ran eleven days.

The naps came back at ten instead of nine, and neither side mentioned it.
