Night 341

Across the Kitchen

Month 12: The Brook Comes Unstuck

Since November it had been two steps and a sit. Two steps, a sit, a look around to see who had noticed, and then crawling, because crawling was faster.

The doctor had said in November what doctors say. Anywhere from nine months to eighteen is on time, every baby inside that range, and the only thing to do is wait and keep the floor clear. Past eighteen months, you ask the doctor. Papa had written the range down and still counted the steps.

This morning the cat walked across the kitchen. Olivia, standing at the cupboard, watched it go, and let go, and went after it.

Two steps. Three. She did not sit. Four, with her arms up like a person carrying something invisible and heavy. Five, and the cat was at the door, and six, and she was in the middle of the floor with nothing to hold, and still up.

"Keep coming," said Papa, very quietly, with his hands open.

Seven. She fell at eight, forward, onto her hands, and looked surprised at the floor for being there.

She got up. Not to Papa. Toward the door, where the cat had been.

The cat had not agreed to any of this and was gone. Olivia stood in the doorway and looked down the hall after it, on her own two feet, for a long time.
