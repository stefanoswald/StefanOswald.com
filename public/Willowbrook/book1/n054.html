Night 54

Two Shots and a Lollipop for Later

Month 2: Smiles and Sleep

The two-month checkup had a scale, a tape measure, a doctor with warm hands, and at the end the part parents dread more than babies do.

Two shots. A quick sting in the little thigh, and the betrayed wail. Sixty seconds of heartbreak, and Mama's eyes went too, though she had promised herself they would not.

And then it was over. Nursing started, and the wail wound down through gears of complaint, into snuffles, into none. By the parking lot Olivia was asleep. By dinner she had forgotten the whole morning.

What she kept was invisible and permanent. Her immune system now held wanted posters for some of the oldest enemies of babies, the coughs and fevers that once went through towns like Willowbrook and took children with them. Grandpa Joe could name them from his mother's stories. He called vaccine day a harvest day, with no joke in his voice.

The evening asked only for gentleness. Extra feeds, extra holding, some fussing, maybe a warm little leg where the medicine went in, maybe a slight fever practicing. The doctor's sheet said exactly when to call. It went on the refrigerator next to the other list, and stayed quiet all night.

"Braver than me," Mama told her. Olivia slept the deep sleep of the immunized, one small bandage on her thigh like a medal she had not asked for.
