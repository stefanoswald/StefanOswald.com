Night 245

Bonks

Month 9: One Step, One Word

With the step came its companion, and every walking house knows it: the fall.

They came all day. The lurch that outran the feet. The sit-down, dozens of times. She was built for it, in the finest padding ever made, a diaper.

The hard part was the face.

Every fall ended with a look up: how bad was that? Whatever she read on Mama's face became the size of the fall. A gasp made it an emergency. A level face and "oops, up you get" made it a comma. Mama chose the comma and held it through her flinches. Papa needed one breath first, then got there.

Most bonks want nothing but a lap and a minute. A bonk to the head wants a look. Hard to wake, a fit, or going limp is the emergency number, not a call. Throwing up more than once, crying that will not stop, a bump bigger than a thumb, or a fall from higher than she is tall means she needs to be seen now. Under one, any real knock to the head earns a call to the doctor, and at night the nurse line rather than waiting for morning. And if you are simply worried, call. The number is under the lemon magnet.

By month's end she sat down hard in the kitchen and told the room: "Uh-oh."

Up she got.
