Night 82

One Book Every Day

Month 3: Rhythm

The habit was one book long, and it began before it made any sense.

Every evening, in the slot between bath and song, Papa read Olivia one board book. She could not follow a plot. She occasionally tried to eat the main character. By any strict measure the audience was unqualified, and the reading went on anyway, because the measure is wrong.

What a three-month-old takes from a lap and a book is nearly everything except the story. The voice, pitched into its reading music, going up the hills and down. The rhythm, the same shape of sound each night, steady as a rocking chair. The closeness, her back against his chest, a page turning in front of them.

Underneath it, laid brick by nightly brick, a sum that will hold for the rest of her life: book equals lap, book equals voice, book equals loved.

So read anything. Read the seed packets in the reading voice, some tired night, and she will take it in just the same way. The habit is the point, and the habit is short.

Tonight's book was about a duck, and it was consumed twice, once by ear and once, briefly, by mouth.

By the last page her hand lay flat and still on the picture, and Papa read the ending to nobody.
