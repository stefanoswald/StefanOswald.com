Night 161

The First Tooth

Month 6: The Feast Begins

It was found, as first teeth are, by accident: a click against the spoon at breakfast, a hard little edge where no edge had been. Mama's finger went in to check. There it was, bottom front, right of center by a whisker, one serrated pearl through the gum.

The house was informed at volume. Papa examined it twice and declared it the finest incisor in the county. The kitchen notebook took the date, boxed. The bottom front two come first for most babies, somewhere between six and ten months, though teeth keep their own calendar: some show at four months, some hold off past the first birthday, all on time by their own reckoning.

With the tooth came the chores. The wiping, a soft cloth or the tiny brush, twice a day now, with a rice-grain of fluoride paste, the habit installed while the teeth are one. The rule, no bottles taken to bed, since milk pooling on new enamel is the one true villain of the age. And a pardon issued backward for the fortnight's gravel nights and the gnawed crib rail, explained at last.

The teethers went back in the refrigerator door, cold and never frozen. The washcloth went back on cold shifts, the next gum already swelling.

Olivia bit the spoon again, on purpose. Click. Applause. Click.

The clicking went on well into the washing up.
