Night 325

The Keeping Shelf

Month 11: Mittens and the Early Dark

She had begun to keep things. That was the month's quiet news.

A wooden spoon went everywhere for a week. Then one blue sock, wept for when it went in the wash. And every dusk the arms went up toward the bear, and the b sound came out, and Bobby Bear was brought down and held for the story and the song, then set back on the dresser across the room. Out of the crib. The rule until the birthday. Nothing soft in the crib before twelve months, not one night before.

But across the room had started to feel far. So on a Tuesday Mama cleared the low shelf across from the foot of the crib and moved him there. Not in. Not within reach of the bars either, since a standing baby pulls in whatever she can reach. Close enough that a face turned sideways could see one flopped ear against the lamp.

That is what a keeping thing is for. A parent cannot be held all night. A bear on a shelf across from her is a piece of the day that stays where she can see it, and the seeing is enough.

She looked at him through the bars long after the song. He looked back, as bears do.

"Buh," she said, once, softly, and turned over.

Mama went down. The bear kept the shelf.
