Night 280

The Bundled Walk

Month 10: First Snow

Twenty-two minutes to get out the door. Nine minutes of walk. Four to get back out of it all. That is the winter ratio, and no family has improved on it.

Mama said the bundling rules out loud while she did them, since saying them is how she remembers them. One layer more than the grown-up is wearing. A hat that covers the ears. The face open to the air, nothing over it, ever. The blanket tucked over the buckled straps, never under them. And the check is not the hands, which are always cold and mean nothing but December. The check is under the collar, at the back of the neck, warm and dry.

The lane was bright enough to squint at. Two porches down, Mateo stood on his step in a suit so full he could not lower his arms, and greeted them with the whole of himself.

At the Hendersons' shoveled stretch Olivia wanted down, and got down, and walked four steps holding two fingers, and sat. The snow took her weight with a soft sound. She considered it from there, both mittens flat in it, and then held her arms up, decision made.

Her cheeks were two apples the whole way home. The print she had left was still there when they came back, the shape of a small sitting person.
