Night 123

Safe Harbor

Month 5: Nearly Sitting, Nearly Everything

Mama's friend from before was back in town, with a gift in tissue paper and a face Olivia had never seen. Olivia studied her from Papa's arms the way she studied the vacuum cleaner, from a distance, with her whole face.

The easy smiles of spring, handed out to anyone with a face, were over. There was the long look. Then the check back over her shoulder at Papa, to read what his face said about this person, her newest trick. Then the slow not-yet of her expression. Known faces went in one drawer now and new ones in another, and the new drawer opened slowly.

The friend, briefed on the porch, did the right things. No swooping. She sat, talked to the grown-ups, and let herself be watched. Nobody passed the baby across the room like a parcel. Nobody said "she's just shy" in that voice, because the slowness was doing real work, sorting the world into people she knew and people she did not know yet.

The lemonade warmed up faster than Olivia did.

Late in the visit, from the harbor of Papa's arms, she put out one starfish hand toward the friend's bracelet. The friend held still and turned her wrist so the light moved along it. Olivia leaned out after the light and forgot to be careful.
