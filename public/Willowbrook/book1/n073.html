Night 73

The Drool Era Dawns

Month 3: Rhythm

The springs turned on in the third month and did not turn off. Drool, sudden and shining, in quantities. Fists went into the mouth and stayed there like tenants. The laundry gained a whole new load: bibs, worn as daily fashion now and changed like tides.

"Teething!" called Mrs. Henderson from her tulips across the street, thrilled.

Possibly. But the calmer truth is that the drool era usually opens months before any tooth shows. Around now the saliva simply comes online at full pressure, and swallowing has not caught up. The fists-in-mouth business is as much curiosity as sore gums. Actual first teeth keep their own calendar, six months, eight, sometimes a birthday-late surprise, and they arrive when they arrive, regardless of forecasts.

Meanwhile, the era asks a little housekeeping. Soft bibs, to spare the chest from constant damp. The chin patted dry, not rubbed, and a whisper of balm if it chaps, drool rash being the era's one real nuisance. Cold things to gum when the gums seem to itch: the chilled (never frozen) teether, the cold corner of a washcloth, all of it checked first against the toilet-tube rule.

Olivia took her fists out to smile, a bright, streaming, four-fountain smile.

"Very becoming," said Mama, and handed over bib number six.

Olivia accepted it, and put the corner of it straight into her mouth.
