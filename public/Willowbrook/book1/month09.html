Month 9 · One Step, One Word

For the grown-up

Nights 244–273

This month holds the two things everybody asks about, a first step and a first word, and in these stories both come early. In your house they may already have come, or be months off. Nine to eighteen months for walking, about as wide for talking, and every point inside those ranges is on time. What is not in doubt is how much she understands, far ahead of what she can say. Watch where she points. Give it the word. That is most of the method.

What you will see: the falls, and how your face decides the size of them. The crawl coming back for the long routes, which is not backward. Practice done in private and stopped the second she is watched. The clocks going back, the dark by half past four, and a bedtime that moves earlier, because a tired baby sleeps worse, not better.

Read these as sounds and rhythm, not questions. Let her hold the book upside down. Honk with the geese, say her word back to her, and stop when she stops.

One thing to try: at the high chair, say "all done" with your hands open every time you clear the tray. In a few weeks she will hand it back to you instead of the bowl.

One thing not to worry about: the baby at the park who did it first. Yours will do something else first.
