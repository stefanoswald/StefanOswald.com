Night 1

Welcome Home, Little One

Month 1: Arrival

The door of the little house on Maple Lane opened, and Mama carried Olivia inside, out of a thin March rain, for the very first time. It had taken eleven minutes to get her out of the car seat. Papa set the bag down in the hall and lost it there, somehow, in a hall four feet wide.

Nobody minded. The house did not know what to do with itself either. The kettle stood very still. The lamp glowed a shade softer than usual, as lamps do when someone important comes in. Mama did not tidy anything. Papa did not fix anything. They sat on the couch, the three of them, and did nothing at all.

That was the whole plan for the day, and it was plenty. The dishes could wait. The phone could wait. The casserole on the front step could sit there in the cold and think about what it had done. The first days are only for landing, the way a leaf comes down on a pond, and a leaf is not expected to do the dishes.

Olivia slept in the crook of an arm that was already learning her shape. Her fist opened and closed around nothing. Outside, the first star found the window.
