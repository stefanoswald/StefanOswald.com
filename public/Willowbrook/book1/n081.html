Night 81

The Phone Goes Face Down

Month 3: Rhythm

Feeds were long, and the phone was always right there, and thumbs get hungry too.

Nobody could fault it. The two a.m. scroll was survival some nights, company in the loneliest chair in the house. But somewhere mid-month Mama noticed a pattern. Olivia's eyes, during feeds, searched upward. They found the underside of a phone. They waited there.

Mama remembered the eight inches. Feeding distance is the exact focal length of new eyes, the first classroom, where faces are studied. And she had been missing class.

"Sorry," she said, to the top of a small head. "I was somewhere else. I'm back."

So the house adopted the gentlest rule it could think of: one feed a day, at least, phone face down, eyes available. The dusk feed became the chosen one. No screen, just the old two-way stare that parents measure in the chest. Her gaze catches yours. Yours answers. Hers deepens. Serve and return, with the eyes alone. It is the first conversation, older than words, and all it asks is that somebody turn up for it.

Some feeds stayed scroll feeds. Parents are human, and two a.m. is two a.m. But the dusk feed belonged to the stare now, and both of them were there for it.

Hello, said Mama's eyes.

Hello hello hello, said Olivia's, which had no volume control yet.
