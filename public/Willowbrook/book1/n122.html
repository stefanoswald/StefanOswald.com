Night 122

Her Own Name

Month 5: Nearly Sitting, Nearly Everything

"Olivia," Mama said across the kitchen, to nobody, halfway through a sentence about the electric bill. The small head on the play mat came around like a compass finding north.

Mama stopped. She said it again, softer. "Olivia." The head turned again, and the face that came with it was asking what happens now.

Somewhere around now the name, said a thousand times a day since the first night, stops being weather and starts being hers. She does not know it means her yet. She knows the sound comes right before good things, a face, a lift, a spoon. It has been sorted out of the river of talk and kept. It is the first word she will ever own, and she is getting it the only way anyone gets a word, by hearing it wrapped in a face, over and over.

The house, having found the trick, overused it, as houses do. Olivia from the doorway. Olivia at the changing table. Olivia sung by Papa in three keys from the hall, to test the acoustics.

At bath time he tried it from behind her. The whole wet head swiveled, found him, and split into a grin. He said it again, to see if it would happen again. It did, and twice more after that.

The water went cool and nobody noticed.
