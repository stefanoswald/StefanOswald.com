Night 78

The Comparison Trap

Month 3: Rhythm

The phone said a baby in another town could roll both ways at nine weeks. The phone showed a nursery with color-matched bins and a mother with finished hair. The phone announced that some babies Olivia's age slept twelve hours, ate kale, and possibly did their own taxes.

Mama put the phone face down.

Every parent wanders onto that racetrack some tired night, and the babies are not racing. Milestones are windows, not deadlines. Rolling spans months of normal, first words span a year, walking wider still, and the late walker turns into the same eight-year-old as the early one. Nobody posts the four o'clock crying, theirs or the baby's. The only chart that matters is the doctor's, where Olivia's own curve climbed her own hill at her own speed.

And the standard was never perfect. Good enough is a real standard, the one babies are built for. A parent who gets it right most of the time, notices when she has not, and comes back, is doing the whole job. Nobody with finished hair is doing more than that.

The real baby was on the rug, gnawing the wooden ring with visible genius.

Mama got down on the floor beside her. "Hello, you," she said. "What have you got there?"

Olivia held up the ring. The phone stayed face down, out of the conversation.
