Night 156

Six Handfuls of Broccoli

Month 6: The Feast Begins

In the feast's second week she was allowed to feed herself, and the kitchen was never clean again. This page will defend the mess to its last line.

Beside the spoon courses came the things a fist can hold: broccoli steamed soft, the stem a handle made for exactly this customer. Banana in its half peel. Avocado spears rolled in crumbs for grip.

Olivia took her first floret like a scepter and ruled with it. She gnawed the crown, wore the crown, and painted with the crown, a green mural from tray to eyebrows. Some fraction arrived inside her. The tarp under the chair, the wisest furniture of the year, took the rest.

Here is the defense. The mess is the meal. Feeding herself runs the whole lesson at once, grip and aim and pressure and texture, the hand and the eye learning each other with lunch as the fee, and a smeared face is a face running experiments. The spoon-only, wiped-clean road is tidier tonight and poorer all year. She got through perhaps a sixth of the floret and the whole of the point.

She required a full bath afterward and glowed through it, Esquire the duck in attendance. Carrots tomorrow, said the list on the fridge. The tarp braced itself.

Green in one ear, still, at bedtime.
