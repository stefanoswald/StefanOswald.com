Night 65

The Reach

Month 3: Rhythm

Above the play mat hung a small felt star, and under it lay Olivia, plotting.

For weeks her arms had waved at the world on their own, close but random, more weather than wanting. Now something new gathered in her face. She looked at the star. She looked at her hand. Back at the star. And slowly, wobbling, the hand rose, steered, and swatted it.

The star swung wildly. Olivia shrieked at it.

This is the reach, and it is the third month's big event. For the first time, seeing and wanting and doing lined up in one arrow. It looks like swatting a felt star. It is the opening move of every skill she will ever own, the spoon, the pencil, the doorknob, the garden gate.

Helping is easy, and mostly means not helping. Hang things just barely in range. Let the misses happen, because the misses are where the aiming gets built. Cheer the swats, quietly, from the couch.

She hit it four more times, each swing wilder with confidence. Then she held her own hand up in front of her face and studied it for a long while, this new tool, suddenly obedient.

The star swung slower and slower. Olivia gloated at it, out loud, for some time.
