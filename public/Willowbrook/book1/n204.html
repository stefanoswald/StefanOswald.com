Night 204

The Corner of the Bear Book

Month 7: Explorers and Guardrails

The bear book was read every night, which is not to say it was listened to.

Papa opened it on his lap. Olivia took the far edge in both hands and put the corner in her mouth, and Papa read the first page around her, to the top of her head.

This is reading to a six-month-old. A voice going up and down in the shapes of a story, a warm lap, a book that is also a thing to chew, and a page turned when she bats at it, sometimes backward. The bear went to the woods twice that night and never came home, because she had the last page in her mouth.

It counts, exactly as much as if she sat still, which she will not do for a year. The words go in through the ear while the book goes in through the mouth, and both are reading, and the second ends around two, and a parent will miss it.

Papa did the voices. The bear's voice was low. The bird's was not a success and never has been, and Olivia looked up at it, as she looks up at anything that goes wrong, and went back to the corner.

The corner is soft now, gummed to velvet, the first corner she has worn out.

He closed the book. The corner was still wet.
