Night 235

Naming the Pictures

Month 8: The Velcro Weeks and the Leaves

The nightly book had a new job this month. The pictures got names, and the finger that asked for them was hers.

Jab, down on the dog. Face up, expectant.

"Dog," said the lap.

Jab, the same dog, asking again.

"Dog. Big brown dog."

Jab, the duck, a new one.

"Duck."

Jab, jab, jab, the whole spread named item by item at the pace she set, and some pages asked for nightly for weeks. The barnyard's tractor was requested more than anything else that month.

Underneath the game, the month's largest business was going on quietly. The words she understood were running months ahead of any word she could say, like windows lighting one by one at dusk. Where's the duck? The finger came down on the duck, four pages deep. Where's Papa's shoe? The crawl turned, and the shoe was fetched and mouthed. Bring the bear, and the bear arrived, hauled by one ear.

Understanding is the iceberg. Talking is the tip. The finger on the duck holds the whole word, kept and aimed, and only the mouth is behind.

The reading closed when the lamp went down. Moon, at the window, jab. Star, jab. Both supplied in the lowest reading voice, and the finger came down one more time, on the dark glass, for the last one.
