Night 219

Three Meals

Month 8: The Velcro Weeks and the Leaves

Without anybody deciding it, the scattered tastings settled into a shape that month. Three meals, at the hours the family already kept.

Breakfast came after the dawn shift: the oat circles, a soft scramble. Lunch came before the big nap. Dinner stayed at the table with everybody, the family's food handed down to the tray, salt held back, pieces cut small and soft.

Milk still did the main work of feeding her at this age. It had just moved into a shape too: four feeds, at waking, mid-morning, after the nap, and bedtime, fitted between the meals like mortar between stones.

The shape changed more than the menu. Hunger, given hours, became readable. The strange mid-afternoon fusses of the seventh month turned out to have been hunger with nowhere to land.

The jobs were divided the old way. Mama and Papa decided what was offered and when. Olivia decided whether, and how much. No pushing the spoon, no bargaining with dessert, the plate simply there. The full feeling was hers to notice, and they meant to leave it that way.

Dinner that night: soup for the third time that week, bread gummed at the crust, one green bean accepted without comment. Mama and Papa did not look at each other. Looking, they had found, jinxes beans.
