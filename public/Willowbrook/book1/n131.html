Night 131

The Guilt Hour

Month 5: Nearly Sitting, Nearly Everything

Ten o'clock, the hallway, hand on the doorframe after the last look-in. The guilt kept the same hour every night, and it kept it here.

It counted up. The morning she had wished, for one hot second, to be anywhere else. The email answered during floor time. The Tuesday off, enjoyed, while her own child laughed in somebody else's kitchen. The song hurried on Thursday because she was simply, humanly, done.

What the hallway has to say back is short.

The guilt is not evidence. It is love pointed the wrong way, the same attention that reads the yawns and packs the bag, aimed at herself. And its whole case falls apart against one honest check: fed, held, answered, delighted in. The real list, ticked every day in this house since the first night home.

The wished-for elsewhere, the good Tuesday, the coffee finished hot, those are not leaks in the love. They are how it gets refilled. A mother is a person keeping a person, and both of them need keeping.

So Mama ran the check. Fed. Held. Answered. Delighted in, twice, at breakfast and on the rug. It would be back tomorrow. It comes back nightly for a while, for everyone.

The moon had laid its path down the floorboards. She stood in the middle of it a minute before she went in.
