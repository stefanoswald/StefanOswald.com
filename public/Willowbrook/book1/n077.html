Night 77

The Bench of Parents

Month 3: Rhythm

There is a bench at every park, and on it sits the rest of the village, waiting to be met.

For three months Mama and Miss Rosa had been friends by telephone. Two couches, two babies born eleven days apart, two women never once awake and dressed and out of the house at the same hour. On Thursday they managed it. Rosa came up the path past the lilacs with the stroller and flour on one cheek, and sat down like somebody arriving home.

What followed was forty-five minutes of the conversation new parents are starving for, the one where nothing has to be explained. The witching hour. The laundry mountain. The three a.m. breathing check.

"You too?"

"Everyone," said Rosa. "Everyone, forever."

A stranger with a stroller paused at the end of the bench and said the password: "How's the sleep?" Room was made. New parenthood is famously the loneliest crowded time there is, never alone and never accompanied, and the cure is not advice. It is one other grown-up in the same boat, laughing at the same waves.

A walking date was set. Thursdays.

In the strollers, Lily slept with her chin tucked and one sock off. Olivia slept with her mouth open. Rosa put the sock back on without looking. It came off again.
