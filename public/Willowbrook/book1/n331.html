Night 331

The Snow That Stuck

Month 11: Mittens and the Early Dark

There had been snow all month, coming and going. This one came down all night at the end of January and was still there at breakfast, at lunch, and three weeks after. The snow that stuck.

The yard was blank and blazing by the time the suit was on. Boots went into snow for the first time. She stood in it to the shins, holding Papa's hand, and looked down at what her feet were doing, long and seriously. Then she sat, suddenly, because snow makes you sit, and the look that came up was outrage.

"Uh-oh," said Papa. "Up?"

Up. And down again, on purpose, to see if it did the same thing. It did.

Then the sled from the aunts, waiting in the garage for this. She was set in it, propped, the rope in Papa's hand and Mama's hand flat on the back, and it moved. Hiss, went the runners. The whole white yard slid past her, and the smallest speed there is went across her face like sunrise.

One circuit of the yard. She looked back at Papa with her mouth open. He went round again. And again, until the pullers begged.

Back inside, the suit steaming on the radiator, she fell asleep in the high chair mid-toast. Mama took the toast, lifted her out, and carried her up to the crib, crumbs and all.
