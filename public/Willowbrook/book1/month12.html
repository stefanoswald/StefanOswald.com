Month 12 · The Brook Comes Unstuck

For the grown-up

Nights 335–365

This is the month the steps turn into walking, for the babies who have steps, and the month of waiting for the babies who do not. Anywhere from nine months to a year and a half is on time. You will also see a finger pointing at what she wants you to look at, a word or two you can count, and a stranger's face still needing a few minutes before it counts as a face.

The stories this month remember the year out loud: four nights of telling her where she came from, a walk past the places it happened, two letters that go in a drawer, one candle. She will not follow any of it. She will follow your voice, as she has all year.

Read it the way you have been reading: sounds, not questions. Let her make the plow noise and say uh-oh at the milk. Let her hold the album and turn the pages the wrong way.

One thing to try: at the lamp, tell her the day back, the good bit and the hard bit. It becomes the story of her life, and later she will tell it to you.

One thing not to worry about: the party. One-year-olds want the box, a lap, and a nap. The rest is for you, and you have earned it. You did the whole year. Tomorrow night, a shorter page and a louder child.
