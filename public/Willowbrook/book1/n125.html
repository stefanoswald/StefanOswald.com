Night 125

Packing for the First Journey

Month 5: Nearly Sitting, Nearly Everything

The invitation had sat on the counter for a month. A cousin's wedding, two towns over, one night away. In July they said yes to it.

The packing began three days early. That was the first lesson. Travel with a baby is nine parts packing and one part travel. Diapers went in first, twice as many as anyone would guess. The medicine kit with the thermometer. The sleep sack that smelled right. The white-noise machine, that portable sea. Bobby Bear came, but he rode in the suitcase, since a crib away from home keeps the same bare rules as the crib at home: on her back, on a flat mattress, nothing in it but the fitted sheet.

Clothes were worked out at outfits per day, times two, plus one, a formula written in spit-up long ago.

Then the part first-time packers forget, the grown-up provisions. A spare shirt for each of them. Snacks. The phone chargers. A plan for the morning coffee.

"We're taking the routine," Mama said, reading the list. "Bath, quiet, book, song, lights out. Wherever the crib is."

"The routine takes up no room," said Papa.

"The routine is most of the suitcase."

The suitcase shut on the second try. Down the hall, the traveler slept her ordinary hours, packed for by everyone, her whole country coming with her.
