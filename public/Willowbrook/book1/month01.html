Month 1 · Arrival

For the grown-up

Nights 1–31

It is March outside, cold rain and mud, the brook running loud, and none of it is your baby's business yet. Her business this month is finding out that when she calls, somebody comes. That is the whole job of the first weeks, done in the dark, one cry at a time, by people who have not slept. She is also borrowing your calm, having none of her own. When you settle, she settles, a beat behind you.

These stories are about what is probably happening at your house: days and nights on backwards, the hungry week around day fourteen, the dinnertime crying nobody can explain, the third shirt before noon, the whispered argument in the hallway at three. Nobody in them is doing it perfectly. That is on purpose.

She will not follow a word of this. Read it anyway, for the voice. The same page five nights running is fine. Keep it low and slow, make the sound words, and stop when she is asleep, even mid-sentence. No questions yet. She has no answers and does not mind.

One thing to try: give every cry the same short sentence in the same low voice. "I'm here" is plenty. It is most of her language so far.

One thing not to worry about: not knowing which cry is which. Nobody arrives fluent. A month in, most parents mostly can, and mostly counts.
