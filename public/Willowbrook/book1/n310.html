Night 310

The Stairs, Upright

Month 11: Mittens and the Early Dark

The Sunday stairs had been a hands-and-knees lesson since autumn. Now that she stood at everything, she stood at the stairs, one hand on the rail through the bars, and looked up them like someone who had heard there was a party on the landing.

Papa opened the bottom gate for the lesson and sat on the step behind her. "Rail. Hold the rail." She held it. He held her other hand. Up came one foot, and, after a long think, the other foot to meet it. One step, both feet, the way stairs are climbed at ten months and, on bad days, at forty.

Three steps up was the whole of it. Then the important half. Coming down is what keeps a climber whole, so it was taught first: turn around, tummy on the step, feet first, backward, slow, Papa's hand on her back the whole way. She did not like the turning around. She liked the bottom.

Stairs are a lesson this year, never a place. The gates, screwed into the wall, stay latched top and bottom the rest of the week, and she is never on the steps without a grown-up one step behind her.

"Down," said Papa, at the bottom. Click went the gate.

She rattled it once, for form, and went to see what the kitchen was doing.
