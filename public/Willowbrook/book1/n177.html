Night 177

The First Fever

Month 6: The Feast Begins

The fever announced itself at the two a.m. feed. Olivia felt hot against Mama's shoulder, and the thermometer confirmed it.

They did not guess and they did not rely on a hand against her forehead. They used the thermometer, checked the age-specific instructions from their pediatrician, and watched Olivia closely. Any rectal temperature of 100.4 degrees Fahrenheit, 38 Celsius, or higher in a baby three months old or younger requires an immediate call. Older babies have different thresholds, and a playful child with a fever still needs the guidance appropriate for that age.

The number was only part of the picture. Hard breathing, a rash that did not fade when pressed, unusual limpness or difficulty waking, repeated vomiting, refusing feeds, too few wet diapers, or hours of inconsolable crying all meant calling promptly. So did a parent's sense that something was seriously wrong.

Olivia was six months old, alert between naps, breathing comfortably, and drinking milk. The nurse advised light clothes, frequent fluids, and the correct weight-based dose of medicine only if discomfort required it. No cold bath. No heavy blankets. Mama and Papa took turns holding her, and the one not holding her pretended to sleep.

By morning, the fever was falling. Olivia woke hungry and offended by the thermometer. The list went back under the lemon magnet, ready for another night but quiet for this one.
