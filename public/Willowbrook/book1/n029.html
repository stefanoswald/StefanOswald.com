Night 29

The Six-Diaper Salute

Month 1: Arrival

In the first month, love is measured in strange units, and one of them is diapers. Six or more wet ones a day. That was the golden number taped inside the changing-table drawer, the daily proof that milk was going in and the small machinery was running. Papa saluted each one. "Number four. Carry on."

The changing table had its own rules, and Papa said them out loud while he worked, because saying them kept them. One hand on the baby, always. Everything within reach before you begin. Never a step away, not for a second, because babies choose exactly that second to learn rolling. Wipe front to back. Let the little bottom air out when it reddens. Cream standing by.

Somewhere along the way, changings stopped being chores. Eye to eye, Papa narrated the socks in a voice that went up and down like a song, and Olivia watched his mouth as if the socks were news. "Now the LEFT sock, madam." She kicked. He waited. She kicked again, and he counted that as her side of the conversation and went on. These were some of her first conversations, and her favorites.

Diaper number six arrived at bedtime, punctual as a train. Papa saluted. Olivia kicked him gently in the wrist, which he took as a salute returned.
