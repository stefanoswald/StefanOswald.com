Night 337

The Remember Game, Part Two

Month 12: The Brook Comes Unstuck

Part two was Papa's, because the second night the album fell open at the photograph Mama took of him at three in the morning in the kitchen, in April, holding Olivia and a spoon and looking like a man who had been lost at sea and found again by the wrong ship.

"That's me," he said. "That was a hard night."

"There were some hard nights," said Mama.

"There was one evening you cried from six to nine and we tried everything twice. There was the week the sleep left and we didn't know where it went. There was the fever, in August, with the list on the fridge and the phone in my hand."

Olivia put her thumb in her mouth and watched his face.

"And there was the night your mother and I argued in whispers about whose turn it was, and it was both of ours, and we knew it."

"We got things wrong," said Mama. "About once a day."

"And came back about once a day."

"That's the bit I'd keep."

Olivia yawned at that, the big square yawn she gives the bathwater. Papa closed the album on his own tired face. The hard nights were in there, and he did not mind leaving them shut.
