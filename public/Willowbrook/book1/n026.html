Night 26

Hiccups and Other Small Weather

Month 1: Arrival

Hic. The whole of Olivia jumped, once, as if a sneeze had knocked at the wrong door. Hic. Again. Mama used to worry about the hiccups. Now she watched the show.

Newborns are full of small weather. Hiccups after meals, and she had practiced those before she was even born, little rhythmic taps from the inside. Sneezes that clear dust, not colds. Startles where both arms fling wide, as if applauding a dream. A trembly chin. A milk-drunk smile. Breathing that hurries and rests and hurries again while she sleeps. All of it ordinary. All of it, the first time you see it, alarming at two in the morning.

Which is why the doctor's number lived on the refrigerator, under the magnet shaped like a lemon, and why no question was ever too small for the list. The hiccups themselves needed nothing. No startling, no tricks, just time, or a little feed. They leave like weather.

Hic. Papa looked up from the paper. Outside, the real weather was sleet. "That one had a bit of a question in it," he said.

Hic. And then no hic. The front passed. Olivia looked mildly betrayed, then deeply asleep, in that order.
