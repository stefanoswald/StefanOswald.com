Night 205

The Boy from Two Porches Down

Month 7: Explorers and Guardrails

Mateo came on Saturday. He lives two porches down, is a year and a half old, has a run like a small tractor, and came with his mother and a bag of blocks and never sat down.

Olivia watched from the blanket like a person at a lecture in a language she nearly speaks.

Everything he did was aimed. He stacked two blocks, knocked them down, said "Boom," and she jumped, and he did it again. He went round the coffee table on his feet, on his feet, and she followed him with her head. He ran to the window and back. He drank from a cup, spilled it, and did not care.

A just-older child pulls, close enough to copy, far enough ahead to reach for. She could not do one thing he did, and she tried the blocks the moment he left, one on top of the other, and it fell, and she made a sound at it that was nearly boom.

The mothers had coffee over it and watched two children want the same block, and Mateo's mother said "Wait, buddy, her turn," twice, calmly, and he waited once.

Papa said nothing when they had gone. He raised the eyebrow.

Mama did not answer it. She wiped the table.

The tower stood at two by bedtime, fell at two, and was built again at two.
