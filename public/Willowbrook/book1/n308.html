Night 308

The Molar

Month 11: Mittens and the Early Dark

The teeth had come in at the front all autumn, in the usual pairs. Then one came in far back, out of sight. A molar, early, and the big ones make themselves known: three nights of drool, a fist jammed into the cheek, and a "mamamama" that meant something hurt.

Mama found it with a spoon. Tink. A small white edge at the back of the gum, and Olivia's eyebrows went up at the sound as if the spoon had said something interesting.

A molar has a top. News for the brush. Twice a day has been the rule since the first tooth, and now the brush has more ground to cover. A soft brush, a smear of toothpaste no bigger than a grain of rice, and Mama's finger finding the back tooth while Olivia chewed the bristles as hard as she could.

Then the swap. Olivia got the brush. Mama got a wooden spoon and brushed her own teeth with it, seriously, in the mirror, and Olivia laughed so hard the toothpaste went sideways.

The other rules stayed on the fridge. No bottle in bed, ever. The first dentist visit is written on the calendar for the birthday month.

"All done," said Mama. Olivia kept the brush.
