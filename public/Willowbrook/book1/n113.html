Night 113

Toes in the Brook

Month 4: Rolling and Laughing

The hot afternoon walked them down to the brook, where Willowbrook keeps its cool. At the pebbled edge, where the water runs two inches deep and has never hurried, Papa crouched with Olivia against his chest, both hands around her, and lowered her feet.

Toes, dipped.

The first dip got the full gasp. Eyes enormous, arms flung wide, everything stopped to work out what this was. Cool. Moving. Alive, somehow, tugging at her toes with a thousand small fingers.

The second dip got kicks. The third got the pumping legs of a professional, and the brook took the splashing kindly, having raised generations of Willowbrook toes.

Water outside the bath is a new subject. Temperature, current, sparkle. And the sound, so like the shush of the white-noise machine that Olivia twice looked around for her bedroom.

The safety part is small and permanent. Hands on the baby, always, in any water, of any depth, however shallow it looks. Shade for the sun, which the willow provided. And the outing ended while it was still a delight, ten minutes and no more, then home by the shaded path, one baby cooled through and half asleep, toes still curling.

Freddy Frog, on a lily pad downstream, had watched it all without once being seen.

"New swimmer," he said, to nobody. The brook went on downhill, carrying the news.
