Night 108

Three Naps, More or Less

Month 4: Rolling and Laughing

Out of the rebuilding, a shape came, and the shape had three rooms.

The morning nap came first and stayed most faithful, an hour or so after waking, deep and dependable, the one you could invite the plumber around. Then the midday nap, the long anchor when it held. And then the late-afternoon catnap, brief and moody, the small bridge to bedtime without the collapse at dusk.

Three naps, more or less, is the usual shape now, once the dust settles. On the little chalkboard in the nursery Mama wrote it up in chalk, which wipes off: morning, middle, little. Times as neighborhoods, not addresses. Wake windows still ran things, two hours and a bit, and the signs still beat the clock in any argument.

The grown-ups had two jobs. Guard the anchor: the midday nap got the full treatment, dark room, white noise, the same wind-down verse, because the anchor holds the day. And forgive the catnap: thirty minutes in the stroller, twenty on a shoulder, a bridge crossed however it gets crossed, since chasing a perfect one is chasing fog.

Days with a shape. Nights with a shape. Four months in, the sleeping was almost predictable.

"Almost," said Mama to the chalkboard, and wrote nothing in ink anywhere.

The first room filled at nine. The plumber was due at ten.
