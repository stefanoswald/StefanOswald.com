Night 121

The Tripod Holds

Month 5: Nearly Sitting, Nearly Everything

Olivia planted both hands on the rug, stacked herself up over them, lifted her head into the sun, and stayed.

The pillows had been teaching her for weeks. This was an ordinary morning, set down for the usual wobble, and there was no slow lean this time, no sideways settle into the cushions. Mama, kneeling an arm's length off, did not move. Neither did Olivia. The clock on the shelf did its small work, and the tripod held.

This is the shape sitting takes first, in the fifth month or the sixth: two hands down, spine up, head high, like a small camp stool. The hands are busy holding the world still. Soon, in flashes, one hand will lift to reach for the ring, and the body will find it can balance without it. That is the whole point of sitting. It sets the hands free.

The pillows stayed in their horseshoe, since new sitters go over backward without warning, and the rug stayed the classroom, a rug being firm and honest in a way a sofa is not. The session ended the way they all end, when the first wobble slurred.

Olivia looked around the room from the new height of herself, found the cat, and told it "Ba."

Then she went over backward into the pillows, like an opinion, and lay there looking at the ceiling.
