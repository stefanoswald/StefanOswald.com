Night 95

Feet, Discovered

Month 4: Rolling and Laughing

The morning Olivia found her feet, she greeted them like cousins off a long train. They had been down there all along, waving from the far end of her, out of reach. Now her hips had loosened and her middle was strong enough to fold, and she closed up like a deck chair: hands down, feet up, a meeting in the middle.

She caught both, one in each hand. She held on and rocked, a round little boat on a calm sea, immensely pleased with the catch.

Then, the mouth being the mouth, one foot went toward it. The toes reached. The tasting was slow and serious.

The pose has a name. Happy baby. The yoga people borrowed it from actual babies, fair and square. Under the comedy there is work going on. Folding up strengthens the very middle she will need for sitting, next month or the one after, and finding her feet stretches the map of herself out to its edges. No equipment. No lessons. Bare feet help, since socks are fog down there, and a few minutes on a towel with no diaper is first class.

She let go. The feet stayed where they were, loyal. She caught them again after lunch, and at bath, and the next morning, rocking on the rug with both feet in both hands.
