Night 192

Arms Up

Month 7: Explorers and Guardrails

The morning speech from the crib had been running since May, a long babbled address to the ceiling, and lately it had a new syllable in it. Ma-ma-ma. Also da-da-da, and ba, and a sound like a small engine.

Papa was sure da-da was for him. Mama pointed out it had been said that week to the lamp, the sleep sack, and a spoon. Papa kept score for a while anyway. The lamp was winning.

None of it was a name yet. That is months off, and the book will say so when it comes. What arrived instead was quieter and, if you were the one it was aimed at, better.

Mama came in on Thursday, and the speech stopped, and both arms came up. Straight up off the mattress, hands open, at her. Not a stretch. A request.

Pick me up.

It is the first thing she ever asked for without crying, and it is aimed. Mama stood at the rail one second longer than she needed to, being asked, and then did it.

"Ma-ma-ma," said Olivia, into her neck, which may have been for the lamp.

The arms came up every morning after that, and every time the door opened, and once at the mail carrier, who was not allowed.

Papa got them on Saturday, and said nothing about the lamp all day.
