Night 96

Everything Is a Teether

Month 4: Rolling and Laughing

Everything within reach went to work that month. The wooden ring was gummed. The cloth block was gummed. So were Papa's knuckle, Mama's chin, the ear of Bobby Bear while nobody was looking, the edge of the good blanket, and once the mirror.

Was it teething? Possibly. Eventually. Who can say. First teeth mostly show up around six or seven months, and the road from fuss to tooth is long and foggy. What was certain was the itch: gums that wanted pressing, and a girl pressing them on the world.

The plan works either way. Cold, but never frozen. Mama opened the freezer, looked at the spare rings, and moved them down to the refrigerator door, since cold soothes and frozen is hard enough to hurt. A washcloth corner, chilled, to chew. Bumps and ridges over smooth. A clean finger rubbed firmly along the gum, received like the kindest thing anyone had ever thought of. Bibs went into rotation, the chin got patted dry, and balm went on at the first pink.

And the old rule for anything that goes near a mouth: nothing small enough to fit through a toilet-paper tube, nothing that can snap, nothing on a string.

The blanket never fully recovered. The teeth, wherever they were, took their time. Olivia worked the cold ring like a craftsman, sighing through her nose.
