Night 284

The Echo Season

Month 10: First Snow

In the third week the house acquired a parrot, small, with improving aim.

The tail of every sentence got caught and thrown back.

"Time for your bath." "Ba!"

"Look at the snow." "No."

That one stopped the kitchen. Papa put his toast down. "She's refusing the snow."

"She's echoing," said Mama, but she checked the window anyway.

"Say goodnight to the bear." "Beh."

An echo is a whole day's work done in a second. Heard, taken apart, planned by the mouth, delivered, and all of it at the speed of a kitchen. The year of copying, the brushing and the phones and the waving, had arrived in her mouth, and that is where copying was always going.

The only thing to do with an echo is to take it as speech. So the Beh got an answer. Bear. And the No got, after some thought, a yes, it is snowing, look at it go.

It was not all echo. She had her own things to say, at length, to the spoon, in a language of one speaker, and she said them with the confidence of somebody who has never been misunderstood.

Then, on the last night of the week, from the crib, after the lamp.

"Goodnight, Olivia."

"Nigh-nigh," came back over the rail, and then nothing but breathing.
