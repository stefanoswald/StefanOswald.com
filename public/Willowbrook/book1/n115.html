Night 115

The Very Large Dog

Month 4: Rolling and Laughing

Across the street lived a dog of great age and volume, gold going white at the muzzle, whose evening patrol passed the porch as reliably as dusk. Tonight Olivia met her first dog.

It ran on a plan, because babies and dogs deserve one. Distance first. The dog sat at Mrs. Henderson's heel a lawn's width off, Olivia stayed up in Mama's arms, and the two parties surveyed each other.

Mrs. Henderson ran her half: a calm dog, a held leash, no lunging love. Mama ran hers: no small hands in the fur or the ears or the tail, not this year, the reaching fist turned into a wave. Watching, at this age, is the visit.

Olivia watched, and did not blink. The size of him. The tail keeping slow time. The huff of dog breath across the grass. The vast pink yawn that finished the performance. Her arms pumped once, the fourth month's standing ovation.

Every animal met calmly now is one more quiet lesson stacked up for later. Creatures are interesting. Creatures have rules. The rules keep everyone's ears comfortable. She will need the stack in the reaching years, when a toddler's hands arrive well ahead of a toddler's judgment.

The patrol moved on, nails ticking the sidewalk, tail still keeping time.

"Dog," said Mama.

"Ba," said Olivia, which everyone on the porch agreed was remarkably close.
