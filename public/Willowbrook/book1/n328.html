Night 328

The Willow Walk

Month 11: Mittens and the Early Dark

The Great Willow had spent the year on the skyline. Pointed at from the lane. Black against the harvest moon. Faintly gold at dusk over a town that mostly did not look up.

On the last Sunday of January they walked up to it. The hill was white and the path was somebody else's boot prints, and they climbed at the pace of a hill in snow, with a stop at the frozen brook so Olivia could see the ice from Papa's shoulders.

It had not been called the first of anything. It was a walk. But Mama had put the good hat on her, and Papa had said "the willow" in a certain voice, and when the tree came up over the crest, bare and enormous, the light going gold in its branches, all three of them had stopped talking.

Olivia's mitten went up. Not pointing. Reaching.

"Willow," said Mama.

They stood under it while the town lit up below, the library's lantern first and then the lane. Papa lifted her down and let her put one mitten on the bark. She patted it, twice, as she pats the duck book.

"We'll come every year," Papa said. He had not planned to say it.

Mama looked at him, and then up at the tree, and said nothing at all.
