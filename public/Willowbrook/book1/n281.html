Night 281

The Animal Orchestra

Month 10: First Snow

The moo came in the second week, and it came loud.

Not quite a word, not quite not, and used from the first night exactly as words are used. The barnyard page in the bear book. A jab at the cow. Papa opened his mouth to say cow, as he had said it two hundred times, and was beaten to it. "Moo."

A jab at the dog. "Woof." A jab at the duck, that founding bird. "Kack." The k was new, the hardest edge her mouth had made yet, and she made it again to hear it.

So the page went two-voiced. The sounds were hers and the names were his, and Papa found himself demoted, at ten months, to a supporting role in a book he had chosen.

A moo aimed at a cow is carrying everything a word carries, and the doctor counts them, so the family did too, and did not argue about this one. They were rehearsed everywhere. At the fence line on Sundays, where the real cows disappointed her by saying nothing back. At the gold dog across the street, who did.

The moo ran four seconds when she was enjoying it. Papa timed one.

"Moo," she said at lights-out, to the ceiling.

"Moo," said Papa from the door, and pulled it to.
