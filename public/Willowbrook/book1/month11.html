Month 11 · Mittens and the Early Dark

For the grown-up

Nights 305–334

January is the month she lets go. Not all the way. She stands at the couch with one hand, then with none, then takes a step or two into open air and sits down hard and looks at her own feet, astonished. Some babies walk this month. Most do not. Both are on time.

You will see her start to keep things: a sock, a spoon, the bear on the dresser, who moves closer to the crib this month, though not into it until the birthday. You will see her know what comes next. The bathwater runs and she yawns. You say "book" and she hands you the duck. That is your bedtime routine paying you back.

The stories are about mittens and the dark at four o'clock and a spoonful of yogurt that went everywhere, and also the cup, the bottle's slow retirement, and a page on weaning that asks nothing of you but gentleness. Read them for the sounds, the tock of the mitten on the stroller wheel, the hiss of the sled. No questions yet. She will answer with a point.

One thing to try: fill the snack cup, set it on the tray, and sit on your hands.

One thing not to worry about: when she walks, and whether you nurse past the birthday or stopped in June. Walking keeps its own calendar. So does she.
