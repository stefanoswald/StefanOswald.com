Night 243

The Last Crickets

Month 8: The Velcro Weeks and the Leaves

October nights on the brook are quiet ones. The summer chorus has thinned to a few last crickets in the bank grass, and the water runs under the bridge with leaves turning on it.

Freddy Frog sat on the bank in his lily-pad hat and did not conduct anything. He was too sleepy to. The cold gets into a frog's ideas.

Stella came down low over the water. The Sleepy Moon leaned in above her and hummed, and the crickets hummed back.

"Leaves," said Stella, watching them go by. "She pointed at every one."

"Mmm," hummed the Moon.

"The house on Maple Lane has a window she waits at now. And a wagon. And a hat, some of the time."

"Mmm."

"I'll be going down for the winter soon," said Freddy, to nobody. "Tell the willow." Nobody had asked him, and the willow already knew.

The wind moved through the willow up on its hill, and a few more leaves let go and came down the dark water, and the crickets sang the last of their song.

Stella took her post low over Maple Lane. In the house below her a baby slept with one fist still closed on nothing, as it had closed on a leaf that afternoon.

Stella twinkled once, for yes.
