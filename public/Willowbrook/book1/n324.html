Night 324

Papa's Page

Month 11: Mittens and the Early Dark

He stood in the doorway for this one, late, as he has most nights since the first week. This page is his.

The jobs he took this year were the three a.m. ones. The swaddle, learned off a video and folded ten thousand times before it retired in May. The pinch test on the car seat straps, every single time, with a patience that grows old bones. The calm face over a bump on the head in the autumn while somebody screamed at him. The wrong words to every song, on purpose, and the whistling nobody asked for.

None of that is in a book, and he knows it, and this is the book, so here it is.

And the other thing, the one he would not say in daylight. The doorway itself. The extra minute at the end of the night, watching a small back go up and down long after there is any need. It started at a bassinet in March and it does not stop. Fathers of far older children still do it. They only stand further back.

In the crib she turned over, one fist caught in the seam of the sleep sack.

"Night, little one," he said, in the rumble most of her year had been built on.

He gave the doorway its extra minute. Then he went to bed.
