Night 293

Mine

Month 10: First Snow

Thursday is the library, the only warm floor in town with other babies on it.

Rosa came straight from the ovens with the flour still on one cheek. The two girls have shared a blanket since summer and never once fought.

The block was one red block from the library's basket, and they arrived at it together. Two fists closed on it at the same moment. The great question of the next several years got asked, in silence, nose to nose. Whose?

Their age settled it. The tug was mutual and brief. Then the block was let go of when the interest ran out, and picked up by the other one, and put down, and picked up again, and it went round like that until it stopped being interesting.

The mothers watched from the low chairs and did not referee, harder than it sounds, and most of the job.

Mine is not greed. It is the same self that shook its head at the broccoli, out sorting where she stops and Lily starts. And the whole value of the block was that the other one was holding it.

"Bye-bye," said one at the door.

"Buh-bye," came back.

The block went back in the basket. Neither of them looked at it again.
