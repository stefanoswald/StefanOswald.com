Night 150

A Person Who's Busy

Month 5: Nearly Sitting, Nearly Everything

Golden hour, the oak, the blanket, and Olivia sitting between them on her planted hands, looking at the park from the top of herself.

"Sitting," Papa said. "That was first."

"The wedding was second," said Mama.

"And the night after the wedding."

"We're not counting the night after the wedding."

A jogger went by and got the long look and nothing else. Mama said her name and the head came round. The smiles had gone choosy this month, kept for people she knew, and worth more, they had agreed.

The rest came in no order. The hen who finally crossed the run. The Tuesday mornings that belonged to nobody. The hallway at ten o'clock, and the list that beat the guilt: fed, held, answered, delighted in. Twelve nights of gravel, walked on the same road. The rocking that went nowhere yet. The coffee table, and the house on its knees. The long stare at the end of the dusk feed, which nobody mentioned.

Olivia took the ring in one hand, passed it to the other, growled at it, and went back to work.

"She's not a baby who does things anymore," Papa said. "She's a person who's busy."

The person, busy, looked up at her name, gave one smile to each of them, and went back to the ring. The oak moved its shade to keep them.
