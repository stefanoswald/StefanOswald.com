Night 182

Hands and Knees

Month 7: Explorers and Guardrails

For a week the crawl was a scoot. Belly to the floor, one elbow doing all the work, the other arm along for the ride, the whole small ship dragging itself toward the cat's dish at the speed of a slow tide. Papa timed it. Rug to hallway, four minutes. A parent could finish a sentence.

Then, on a Tuesday, she pushed up onto her hands and knees, rocked twice the way she had rocked all August, and went.

Rug to hallway, four seconds. Papa did not time it. He was in the hallway on his own hands and knees behind her, and she looked back over her shoulder at him and squealed and went faster, and the chase, which will run in this house for years, had its first morning. The rule of the chase is simple. She goes, you follow, she checks that you are coming, and you are always coming.

A crawler wants the floor. The bouncer seat that had held her all summer was a parking space now, ten minutes for a pot to boil and not more. The rest of the day she was loose in the rooms that were ready for her, and by evening she had been to every one of them.

One sock stayed on the rug, where the scoot had ended.
