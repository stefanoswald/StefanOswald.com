Night 94

Ninety Minutes Becomes Two Hours

Month 4: Rolling and Laughing

Nobody announced it. One week Olivia lasted an hour and a half before the yawns came, and the next week it was two hours, sometimes a little more, and the day moved over to make room.

Half an hour does not sound like much. It is the difference between half an errand and all of it. Between the park or the bath, and the park and the bath. Play got long enough to have parts: mat, mirror, lap, airplane rides, all inside one stretch with time left over.

Wake windows grow all through the first years, from the newborn's forty-five minutes to the toddler's whole afternoons. This is the first stretch a parent feels, because it hands back something they had forgotten owning. Slack. The gap between one thing and the next.

The signs still ran the day, not the clock. The stare at nothing. The ear rubbed with a fist. The yawn that drifts through like a leaf. Windows shrink on the off days and after a big outing, and no chart has ever argued a tired baby out of being tired.

Today the window held wide open to the last minute and closed on time. Mama sat on the floor with the second cup of tea, still warm, both hands around it, doing nothing whatsoever with the half hour.
