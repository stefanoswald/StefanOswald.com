Night 223

Where's Your Nose?

Month 8: The Velcro Weeks and the Leaves

Two games ran the fireside that month, and both of them were about the same thing.

Pat-a-cake came first. Her palms met Mama's on the beat, clapping with somebody else's timing in it, dancing, seated. The rolling verse rolled the forearms over each other at about the skill of a small cement mixer. The B was marked with conviction on whatever was nearest. And the throwing in the oven was flung with both arms wide, applause supplied by the thrower.

Where's-your-nose came second. The question was put, and the small hand flew. To the ear, at first. Then, week by week, the map filled in. Nose found. Head found. Belly found and drummed on. Toes found, old friends from June, saluted again. And the advanced item the family kept in purely for the comedy: where's Papa's nose? That one was answered by seizing it, with the full pincer, and holding on.

Sharing a beat with another person is the whole trick of talking, slowed down and made into a game. Nobody in the room was thinking about that. They were laughing, the only fuel it runs on.

"Where's Mama's nose?" Mama asked, the last round, by the fire.

The hand came up slowly and landed on her own belly. She drummed it. Close enough, for the last round.
