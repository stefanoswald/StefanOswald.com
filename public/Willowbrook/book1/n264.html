Night 264

So Big

Month 9: One Step, One Word

The Tuesday call is the only screen in the house, and it is the best theater in it.

Copying had come on that month like a switch. Clap, and she clapped, late, flat-palmed. Pat the table, and she patted. Papa's eyebrow thing, nine years old and permanent, she attempted with her whole forehead. And the big one, "How big is Olivia?", both arms straight up, "So big!", which had taken two weeks of Papa doing it alone to an empty kitchen.

Her far grandfather, in his own kitchen, clapped for the walking, and the clap arrived a second late. So she clapped, and hers arrived to him a second late, and the two of them kept it going, a round of applause that never quite met, for a full minute. Papa timed it.

"How big is Olivia?" asked her grandfather.

Nothing. She looked at Papa. Papa did the arms. She did the arms.

"So big," said everyone, at three different times.

Her grandfather did the arms too, and knocked his lamp over on his end, and the call finished on a view of his ceiling.

He called back. She was under the table with the bear by then, so he was shown the top of her head, which he said was a good head.

The screen went dark. She clapped for that too.
