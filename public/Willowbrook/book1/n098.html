Night 98

The Audience Eats

Month 4: Rolling and Laughing

Dinner had an audience now, and the audience missed nothing. From her chair, Olivia had found the second-best show in the house, after faces. Food, traveling.

The fork rose off Papa's plate. Her eyes rose with it. It crossed, arrived, the chewing began, and her whole head had followed the trip like a spectator at the smallest tennis match ever played. When Mama passed the bread, Olivia's head went with the bread. When Papa lifted his cup, her chin came up.

Then, this week, the reach. One open hand out toward the passing potatoes, with plans.

Watching is not idle. It is the first sign of being ready to eat, and it ripens like this, at the table, weeks ahead of any bite. She was studying how the thing is done: things go in mouths, the big people do it constantly, the tools flash and travel. The parents had one job, which was to eat in front of her, plainly and often.

"Papa is eating soup," said Papa. "Warm soup. Mmm."

Mama looked at him.

"What," he said. "It's advertising."

No tastes yet. Milk holds the border until six months. But the border had a crowd at it tonight, on tiptoe.

"Soon," Papa told the reaching hand, and moved his plate a careful inch. The hand went back to watching the potatoes go by.
