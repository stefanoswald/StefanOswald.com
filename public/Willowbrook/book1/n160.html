Night 160

Halfway

Month 6: The Feast Begins

No cake marked it. Halfway is not a party. It is a porch at dusk at the end of an ordinary Tuesday in August, the heat finally off the boards, a baby on a knee, and the counting arriving unasked: six months down, six to go before the candle.

The kicks Mama once read through her own skin were public property now, performed in high chairs, applauded at fence lines. The heartbeat Papa first heard through a monitor in a bright, terrible, wonderful room thumped against his collarbone nightly at the burping hour, punctual, his. Behind them, the fog of the first weeks, the smile, the laugh, the roll, the feast, each impossible at the time and folded now into what their hands knew.

Ahead lay the crawl, the pull to stand, the words, the steps. The loud half of the year, arriving to a household that had, without anybody once announcing it, become good at this. Not always. Not smoothly. Good enough.

"Six months," said Papa to the porch.

"Six months," said Mama, and left it there, since the whole of what she meant would not fit on a porch. The light was going by eight now. Mrs. Henderson's porch lamp came on across the street.

Olivia held onto his sleeve with one fist.
