Night 169

Supper from the Farm

Month 6: The Feast Begins

Grandpa Joe came on Thursday with a basket and stayed for supper, which he announced by sitting down. Carrots with the dirt still on. A squash. Tomatoes warm from the vine. He put the basket on the table, took off his hat, and said nothing whatever for the next hour, which is his way of saying a great deal.

The feast's newest member found the basket built for her. The carrots went in the pot until they gave up all resistance and came out as coins she could gum to nothing. A potato was mashed at the table with a fork and no fuss. The chicken, shredded soft, got the frown of a critic and the seconds of a convert.

Family food, adapted, beats baby food, bought. The pot's carrot beats the pouch on texture, since a pouch gives nothing to practice chewing on. And she was eating what the family ate, at the same table, part of what the house cooks from night one.

The adaptations were brief. Salt went in after her portion came out. The shapes were checked against the choke chart. The spice went in the way the allergens had, early and gently and often. Bland is a habit.

Grandpa Joe watched her work a carrot coin flat.

"Good dirt," he said, and put his hat back on.
