Night 266

Two Blocks High

Month 9: One Step, One Word

The stacker on the Thursday blanket had planted something in Mama's eye, and the blocks came out of the rotation basket to the front of the rug.

For a week, nothing. The blocks were mouthed, banged, carried around. Then on a grey morning with no one watching, she set one block on the rug and held a second over it a long time, and let go.

It fell off. She looked at it. "Uh-oh."

Again. Off. Again, and this time she kept the bottom one still with her other hand while she set the top one down, and let go, and it held.

Two high.

It is the first thing she has ever made. Everything before this was taking out and knocking down, and here was the other thing, the putting up, wobbling and standing.

She sat back and looked at it with the gravity a builder has. Then she looked for someone, and Mama was in the doorway, and got both arms at the tower and a "Duh!" that meant the whole idea of it.

"Two," said Mama. "You kept the bottom one still with your hand."

Then it was knocked down, rebuilt, knocked down, and the work ran until nap. Three high fell every time. Three high would fall for weeks yet.

Two blocks stood on the rug all through the nap.
