Night 215

The Other Lap

Month 8: The Velcro Weeks and the Leaves

The plant-house mornings had been peaceful since summer. Now they were not. The handoff at the door was wailed, the leg was clung to at a new address, and Mama drove to work with the sound in her ears and the guilt reciting its old speech.

The three beats went with her to the plant-house door. Kiss. Wave. Window, and the plant house had a fine front window. The wails shortened.

Then, on a Thursday, Mama came to collect her early and stopped in the doorway.

Olivia was deep in the caregiver's lap for the before-nap book. It was the bear book, the plant house's own copy. She was leaning back against a chest that was not family with the whole boneless trust Mama had thought was hers alone. The pauses were being held correctly. The gasp was being delivered.

It is a strange thing to see, and it means the arrangement is working twice over. Love is not a ration. A baby who settles this deeply into a second lap has not divided anything. She knows exactly who her people are, at both addresses.

Mama stood there one full minute. Then she was spotted. The lap was vacated at crawling speed, and the reunion arrived against her knees at its true size.
