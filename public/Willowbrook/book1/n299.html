Night 299

The Dark at Four

Month 10: First Snow

By the week after the holiday the day had a new shape, and the shape was short.

Light at half past seven. The walk at noon, when the sun is as high as it gets, and that is not high. The nap. The lamp on at three. And at four, the window gone blue, and the porch lights coming on across the street.

The house did not fight it. Bath at half past five, in the dark, which is not the dark, only December. Down by half past six, and the long evening left for the grown-ups, who spent it asleep on the couch by eight.

The night bottle was going slowly and then all at once. Every night had become some nights. Some nights had become a question, asked at one in the morning, and lately the answer was sometimes a head shake and a turn back toward the mattress. Nobody pushed it. Milk is still her main food until one, and a night when she wants it again is fine, and a night when she does not is fine.

No one said the word. The tap did the talking.

She yawned on Mama's hip at the sound of it and pointed at the porch lights across the street with her whole arm.
