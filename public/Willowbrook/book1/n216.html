Night 216

The Restaurant, Unchaperoned

Month 8: The Velcro Weeks and the Leaves

The evening at the farm in May had answered the small question. The eighth month asked the larger one: a sitter, the good restaurant, a Friday night out for the first time since the door on Maple Lane opened for three.

The sitter was the plant-house caregiver's grown daughter, sworn to at the bench. The routine was written out on two pages and rehearsed once, the Tuesday before, for money.

Friday ran the way those Fridays run. Kiss, wave, window. The wail was brief. The car pulled out under the weight of two adults not circling the block. They went to the tablecloth restaurant, and raced no fuse. Somewhere in the second course they remembered how to talk about something that was not sleep, and then talked about sleep anyway.

They were home by ten to a silent house and the sitter's notes. Bath: splendid. Book: four readings. Song: performed from the transcription, wrong notes kept as written. Then the pilgrimage in stocking feet to the crib rail, to stand over the breathing of somebody who had needed them zero times in four hours.

The night went into the kitchen notebook and was underlined. A second Friday was booked before bed, while the courage held.

The sitter kept the two pages. The wrong notes, she said, were the best part.
