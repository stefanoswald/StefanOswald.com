Night 88

What the Hands Know Now

Month 3: Rhythm

Take the measure of two small hands.

They open in advance now, shaped to the size of the thing they are going for, wide for the cloth block, narrower for the ring, so ordinary nobody notices and so complicated that engineers sigh. They steer. Left hand to the left country, right hand to the right, and lately across the middle to raid whatever the other one is holding. And, newest trick of all, they let go on purpose sometimes, dropping the block to the floor and studying the result like somebody running her own experiment on gravity.

They know Mama's hair, and just how hard to pull it. They know Papa's beard, the hem of the burp cloth, which corner of the block has the crinkle in it. They work straight through every feed, one hand resting on the warm bottle or opening and closing slowly against skin, reading the whole meal by touch.

Three months ago they were startled by their own passing. Now they are hers.

The grown-up job goes on, unglamorous: floor time, things within reach, no rescuing the fumbles. The fumbles are how hands get good.

Tonight the left hand held the block while the right one patted it, twice, gently, the way you pat a thing you approve of.

Both hands went into the bath still holding their opinions.
