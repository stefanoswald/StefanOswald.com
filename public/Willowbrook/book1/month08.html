Month 8 · The Velcro Weeks and the Leaves

For the grown-up

Nights 213–243

This is the month she holds on. Somewhere around now the separation feelings that started at the door in September reach their peak, and the peak has hands. She will cling to your leg at the stove and wail when you leave the room, because she has worked out at last that you are a person who can go. That is not spoiling and it is not going backward. It is love that has noticed what it could lose.

In these stories you will see the family build a goodbye and keep it the same at every door, and you will see the strange good news of the other lap, the sitter's notes, the night out. You will also see the busier side of eight months: the pincer grasp closing on one oat circle, the spoon dropped on purpose with eye contact, the couch climbed, the push wagon, the first shoes, a hat that will not stay on.

She will not follow these stories yet. Read them for the sounds. Tap the horn with her. Say uh-oh. Clap the pat-a-cake. Save the questions for another year.

One thing to try: the same three beats at every goodbye, kiss, wave, window, and never sneak out. It feels slower and it is faster.

One thing not to worry about: how long the clinging lasts, or whether she stands or walks this month. Both keep their own calendar.
