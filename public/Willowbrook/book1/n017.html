Night 17

The Carrier

Month 1: Arrival

Papa's arms were never empty anymore, and his coffee had been cold since Tuesday. Then the carrier came out of the closet. He read the instructions out loud, twice. Close enough to kiss, her head right under his chin. Face visible, never pressed into the fabric. Chin off her chest, so the airway stays straight. Back supported. Knees tucked up like a frog's.

Then he stood, and there it was: a warm, breathing backpack worn frontwards, and two free hands. He made a sandwich. He answered the door. He picked a sock up off the floor for the first time in a week. He was, briefly, a wizard.

The real discovery was hers. Held upright against him, Olivia rode the sway of his walking like a boat at anchor, his heartbeat under her ear, his voice rumbling through his ribs into her back. Carried babies often cry less. Being held and being moved is the world they came from.

They walked to the corner and back, both in hats, while the string lights on the porches, still up from winter, came on one by one. Olivia slept the whole way with her cheek on his shirt. At home, his coffee went cold again.
