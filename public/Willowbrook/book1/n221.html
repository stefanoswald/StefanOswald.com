Night 221

Uh-Oh

Month 8: The Velcro Weeks and the Leaves

The dropping game, founded in the fourth month and staffed ever since by one large unpaid assistant, got its anthem that month. The anthem had two syllables.

"Uh-oh."

She had heard it at every spill and topple since the tastings began. It is the small noise the tribe makes when gravity wins. This month she took delivery.

First as an echo: spoon down, Papa's uh-oh, her two syllables trailing after like a small cover version. Then, within the week, the upgrade. The uh-oh moved to before the drop.

The spoon was lifted and held out over the edge of the tray. Eye contact was found and kept, the theater being strict on this point. And then, with the relish of a singer at the big note: "Uh-oh."

Release. Clatter. House down.

The kitchen notebook did not count it as a word. The kitchen notebook has standards, and the first real word was still somewhere ahead. But it came at the right moment every time, at every fall and spill in the house, and by the end of the month at Papa's dropped keys as well, which is when Papa began counting it privately.

"Uh-oh," said the whole family now, at everything, in her exact tune. At the spilled coffee. At the keys. At the spoon going over the edge again, on purpose, with eye contact.
