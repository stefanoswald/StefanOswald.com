Night 335

The Last Month

Month 12: The Brook Comes Unstuck

The kitchen calendar had been drawn by Mama in May, on twelve sheets of butcher paper, with a picture at the top of each one. The February sheet had a candle. On the first morning of the month she turned to it, and pinned it flat, and there were no more sheets under it.

"Last one," she said.

"Then what?" said Papa.

"Then I draw another one."

Papa's pencil stars ran up both edges of every sheet, one for every bedtime story, small and crooked and never missed. He put the pencil behind his ear and started the coffee.

Outside, the snow had gone the color of the sidewalk. It had been white in December and proud of itself in January, and now it lay in the yard like something that had lost an argument. On the sill above the sink sat three pots of dirt Mama had planted in the dark part of January. Nothing showed yet.

Olivia stood at her own cupboard, the low one, and took out the funnel and put it back. Then she let go with both hands, took two steps toward the table, and sat down hard.

"Uh-oh," she said, and looked at Papa.

He drew a star for it. It was not a bedtime, but he drew one anyway.
