Night 246

The Two Gaits

Month 9: One Step, One Word

The walking was for occasions. Three steps, five, a wobbling nine at the week's best, brought out for crossings to open arms and for the Tuesday call. Upright travel cost her every step, and she spent it where it paid.

Crawling was for getting anywhere. The kitchen, the hallway, any trip with a deadline, ran on the old four-beat at the old speed. Papa watched it daily: three proud steps, a drop to all fours mid-room like a commuter changing trains, the rest at twice the pace. Then the last three feet to the cupboard taken upright, for the dignity of the door.

He wanted, badly, to stand her back up. He sat on his hands instead, more or less. The drop to crawling is not going backward. It is what a changeover looks like from inside: the crawl gives up its routes to the walk one hallway at a time, as the walking gets cheap, and hauling her upright argues with a judgment better than his.

The push wagon, rice still rattling in its hold, made a third gait, good for the whole hallway. The cat took to leaving rooms early.

By the end of the month the kitchen went upright. The hallway held out longest. Nobody saw the last crawl. Nobody ever does.

The wagon stood parked at the hall's end, rice settled.
