Night 70

The Pumping Chapter

Month 3: Rhythm

Some pages are less poetry and more plumbing. This is the pumping page.

Mama was building the freezer stash for the working weeks, and finding out the trade's unglamorous truths. That the pump tells you about the pump and nothing about her: what it draws says little about what Olivia gets, babies being better engineers than machines. That the milk follows calm more than it follows effort: a photo of Olivia on the counter, a warm drink, shoulders down from the ears. That morning is the generous hour, and one extra pump then builds a stash faster than heroics at midnight.

The rest ran on labels. Date on every bottle. Oldest to the front, first in and first out, like Grandpa Joe's hay. Small portions frozen, two and three ounces, because thawed milk cannot be frozen again and nobody wants to pour out four.

Papa took the whole second half of the job: the washing, the drying rack like a small plastic forest, the freezer count kept in his head with the seriousness of a harbor master. "Nineteen," he would say, unasked, most evenings.

Tonight Mama labeled and Papa washed, and the fridge door held a row of small bottles like lanterns.

"Twenty-one," Papa said, and closed the door on them gently, the way you close a door on somebody sleeping.
