Night 269

The Nine-Month Checkup

Month 9: One Step, One Word

The nine-month visit came round on a grey Friday, and the doctor asked her questions with the folder shut, watching the floor.

"Is she pulling up?"

"Since October," said Mama. Olivia pulled up on the doctor's stool.

"Any steps?"

"Wednesday, three weeks ago," said Papa. "Before lunch." Olivia let go of the stool, took four steps toward the doctor's hands, sat down hard, and got up.

"Falls?"

"Hundreds," said Mama. "Bottom first. We do the face." The doctor knew the face.

"Does she point? Look where you look?" Olivia pointed at the doctor's tie, which had ducks on it, and said so.

"Words?"

"That one," said Papa. "And a sound for more."

"Games?" She clapped at the ducks. She did so-big. She signed all done at the tongue depressor, and was right.

"Meals?" "Three and a snack. Milk still the main thing." "A cup?" "Open, at the table. Mostly collar." "Sleep?" "Eleven nights of walking the crib. It stopped." "Shoes?" "At the door. Barefoot inside."

No shots this visit. The next round waits for one.

At the door the doctor said what she always says. "She's exactly herself, on her own clock. See you at one."

In the car Mama said, "We didn't tell her about the worm."

"Or the geese," said Papa. "Or the cows."

"Next time."

Olivia slept before the second light.
