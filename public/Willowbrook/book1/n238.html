Night 238

Ten Seconds, Standing

Month 8: The Velcro Weeks and the Leaves

The first let-go from the couch, earlier in the month, had lasted about as long as a sneeze. Mama and Papa had privately set a marker for the era, and the marker was ten seconds, standing free, in the middle of the floor, no furniture involved.

It happened on a Tuesday morning, from a squat.

She was down among the blocks, and then the knees pressed, the deep press the couch had taught her, and there was no couch. Up she came. Arms out at half-mast, the balance arguing with itself and winning. Nobody moved. Two coffee cups were held very still.

Ten. Eleven. At thirteen she noticed her audience, and the noticing cost her the balance, and she sat down hard on the bottom, which is what the bottom is for.

The applause had been held for thirteen seconds. It went off all at once. She joined it, seated, clapping for herself.

For households counting: this is the last door before the door. What remains is one foot lifted and put down somewhere new, and that can come within days or sit at the door for months. Every one of those calendars is right.

"Thirteen," said Papa, to nobody.

Mama put her cup down on the floor, well out of reach, and stayed there.
