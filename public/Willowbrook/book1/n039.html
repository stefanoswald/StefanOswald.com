Night 39

The Hour That Has No Reason

Month 2: Smiles and Sleep

Some evenings had a reason. Hungry, gassy, overtired. Some evenings there was only crying, a train with no stations, and every trick bounced off.

The doctor's rule of threes: three hours of crying, three evenings a week, three weeks running, in a baby otherwise thriving. That is colic, and it is nobody's fault. Not the milk, not the parenting. Crying peaks around six weeks in most babies, colic or not, and truly fades by three or four months. It is a season, and it ends.

That night the train ran and ran. Papa was on his fortieth lap of the rug when he said, too loud, "What do you WANT," and Olivia flinched and cried harder. He heard himself. He handed her to Mama. "I need a minute," he said, and stood on the porch until his hands unclenched.

That is the plan. Trade off before the fraying. When both pairs of arms are spent, she goes on her back in the crib, safe, and the parent goes into the hall for five minutes. That is allowed. A baby is never shaken, not once, not ever.

Papa came back. "Sorry," he said, to both of them. Mama put the quiet music on. The train ran another twenty minutes and stopped, as it always did in the end. They stood in the silence like people out in rain.
