Night 114

The Tuesday Blanket

Month 4: Rolling and Laughing

Tuesdays had become the walking day, and this Tuesday the walk pitched camp. One blanket under the oak, two mothers, two babies laid side by side like loaves set out to rise.

Olivia and Lily regarded each other.

This was not friendship. Real playing together is years off. A baby this age treats another baby mostly as fascinating weather. But it was something. The long mutual stare. The slow reach of one hand toward another. The moment Lily got hold of Olivia's sleeve, and Olivia consulted the sky about it.

Babies notice babies. The face at matching height, the sounds at matching size. They stare at another baby longer than at almost anything, tucking away the first faint idea of a thing that will one day have a name. Someone like me.

The mothers had their own business. Sleep totals traded. A teething rumor examined. Miss Rosa's cinnamon rolls handed round, and Rosa herself with flour on one cheek that nobody mentioned, because nobody ever did.

"She's eleven days behind yours," Rosa said, "and she's already got a grip like a bricklayer."

Lily fell asleep first, mid-grip. Olivia stayed awake another minute, keeping an eye on her neighbor, and then followed.

Four of them under the oak, two out cold, nothing decided and nothing needing to be. Same time next Tuesday, said everyone who could talk.
