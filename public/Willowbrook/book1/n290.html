Night 290

Kiss It Better

Month 10: First Snow

The holiday cards were late, which is when they get written. Mama sat at the table with the stack and the list and the pen, and the fourth envelope took its small toll.

"Ow," she said, honestly, and shook the finger.

Olivia came over. She had been at her cupboard with the spoons, and she left the spoons, and stood at Mama's knee, and looked at the finger, and pressed her mouth to it. Solemn. Unprompted. Exactly the way it has been done to her, at every bump since the spring.

Then the pat. The flat-palmed there-there of a hundred consolations, received and now, for the first time, issued.

"Beh."

Mama held very still.

All year the care has gone one way, down, into her, and the first sign of it coming back up was not expected. It came back up at a paper cut, at a kitchen table, on a Tuesday, in a cardigan with a spoon in the pocket.

"Better," said Mama, when she could. "You fixed it."

The card she had been writing said, in the end, nothing but the family's names, because the finger needed both hands to be looked at properly.

Olivia went back to the cupboard and the spoons. Mama sat a while with the finger held up in the lamplight, looking at it.
