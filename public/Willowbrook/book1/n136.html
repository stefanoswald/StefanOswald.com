Night 136

The Second Summer Walk

Month 5: Nearly Sitting, Nearly Everything

Olivia rode upright now, strapped tall in the stroller's new recline, canopy back, and the evening walk was hers.

It had been a rescue once, wheels for the witching hour, scenery as a sedative. Not anymore. The world came to the wheels and she received it. Mrs. Henderson's old gold dog, tracked across a lawn with a full head-swivel. The porch waves, answered lately with an arm-pump that stopped traffic. The string lights coming on down Maple Lane, greeted each evening as the best news of the day.

Papa narrated the town at walking pace, mailbox, maple, moon up early, and the town narrated back: the bakery's day-end smell, a sprinkler ticking, the brook heard under its small bridge, running low and quiet in July. Every porch a flashcard. Nobody looked at a phone, because the town was too interesting on both sides of the conversation.

At the corner Papa whistled at the lights. It came out as a sort of kettle noise. The dog looked up, concerned.

"That was on purpose," Papa told it.

They took the long way past the park, because one pumping arm insisted. Home at full dark, one baby fed on the world and heavy-lidded, one father lighter than he had left. The bath was already running as they came in, and she yawned at the sound of it.
