Night 210

The Streetlamp

Month 7: Explorers and Guardrails

In July the evening walk had come home in daylight, with the light still up at eight. This week it came home under the streetlamp.

The bench with Rosa ran later than it meant to. Rosa had flour on one cheek and a story about Lily and a spoon, and the two strollers sat nose to nose, the two babies eleven days apart studying each other in their sweaters like people at neighboring desks. Lily grabbed Olivia's foot. Olivia allowed it. The light went yellow, then grey, and both mothers looked up at once and said "Bath."

Home along the lane, a few leaves on the pavement now, dry enough to sound. The streetlamp outside Mrs. Henderson's came on while they were under it, with a click and a hum, and Olivia's face went up to it and stayed there, and the stroller stopped so it could.

"Light," said Mama, who would be saying it every night now.

The dark comes early in September, a minute or two sooner each evening, and a house with a baby in it feels the minute. The routine does not. Bath, quiet, book, song, lights out, at the same hour, whatever the sky is doing.

Mrs. Henderson waved from her window. The porch lights came on down the lane, one by one.

Olivia was asleep before the porch.
