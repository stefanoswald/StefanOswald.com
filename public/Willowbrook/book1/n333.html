Night 333

The Thank-You Workshop

Month 11: Mittens and the Early Dark

The applesauce from September's orchard morning had been put up in small jars, a whole pantry shelf of them, on Rosa's advice. "Jar the fall. You'll want it in January." Rosa is right about January.

The jars came out on a Sunday afternoon, and the kitchen table became the thank-you workshop, with Olivia on the staff.

Mama did the labels, and talked her way through each one, because the talking is the lesson and the jar is only the jar.

"This one's Mrs. Henderson's. She shoveled our walk before we were up, in the dark. That was cold work." A name went on. "This one's for Rosa, for the soup in the sniffle week. She had a sick one of her own that week."

"This one's the library's. This one's Grandpa Joe's. He'll say he doesn't need it and eat it standing up."

Olivia's job was the scribble. A fat crayon, one mark per label, held out afterward for inspection. Some marks were on the label. Some were on the jar. One was on Mama.

Nobody said thank you. A baby does not have the word yet. But she watched Mama's face do the remembering over every jar, and made her mark, and the jars went in a row by the door.

The row waited there for the morning's walk. The crayon went upstairs in a fist.
