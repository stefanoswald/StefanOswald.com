Night 175

The Lap Spring

Month 6: The Feast Begins

The lap developed a spring in it, and the spring had plans.

Held standing on Papa's knees, her grip on his thumbs like flight controls, Olivia no longer stood. She bounced: knees dipping, legs firing, the whole undercarriage pumping in a rhythm nobody taught her, grinning at the ceiling on every launch.

She did it in sets, in long shifts, delighted by her own machinery. The old rumor about bowed legs confuses this with propping up a baby who has not asked, and the asking is the whole distinction. Legs that fire want firing, and this pair had asked, loudly, by way of the thumbs.

What the spring was building had a rough calendar. Pulling to stand, the headline of the coming months, the crib rail's destiny visible from here. Then cruising along the furniture coastline. Then the thing nobody in the house was ready to say, somewhere past the year line or before it.

None of it concerned her. She worked her shift for the pleasure of the shift, up, down, the thumbs steady, the biggest laugh in the house rumbling up through his knees.

"She's going to be trouble," said Papa, meaning the opposite, meaning yes.

Bounce. Bounce, bounce. The floor could wait a while yet.
