Night 201

The First Sweater

Month 7: Explorers and Guardrails

The first cool morning came on a Thursday, the kind where the step is cold through your trousers and the air has an edge to it. Mama went to the cedar chest and came back with the green sweater she had worn herself at one, too big by a season.

Putting a sweater on a six-month-old is a job for two adults and a small prayer. Papa did it alone on the step, which was a choice.

Head first. The head does not want to go through. There is a moment, every time, with the neck hole around the face and the face inside it, mouth open, furious, eyes covered, where all of babyhood is present. Then the head pops through and the curls stand straight up and the face is astonished to find the world still there.

Then the arms, one at a time, each hand fished up the sleeve like a sock lost in a pipe.

She sat in it on the step, cuffs turned back twice, both hands in her mouth, and looked at the maple across the street, which had one branch gone yellow since Sunday.

Papa whistled at it, which did not help the maple.

The hat, offered, was declined, as in June. Some wars are not over.

She was warm and the step was cold, and they sat.
