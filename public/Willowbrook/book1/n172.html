Night 172

The Restaurant Trial

Month 6: The Feast Begins

Six months of kitchen-table date nights had built up the courage, and on Friday the family went public: the corner place on the square, half past five, party of three, one of whom would be dining from a bag.

The rules came by text from Rosa, who had done it the week before, with a photo of Lily asleep in a bread basket as proof. Timing decided everything. The early seating, restaurant dawn, the room half empty, their own small diner's witching hour still two hours off. The corner booth.

The kit: bib, spoon, a bag of puffs, one silent toy, and ordering everything at once, the whole meal pulled forward toward the fuse. The bread crust, issued by a waiter of evident fatherhood, was gnawed through two courses. The puffs came out with the main course in a slow scatter, each one a minute of quiet bought a pinch at a time.

Mama and Papa ate hot food, with both hands, in public, and said so twice. Never bargain with the fuse.

Ice cream on the walk home, shared in specks with a first-timer who voted with her entire face. The trick is not to make the baby fit the old evening. It is to build a new one her shape, and eat it hot.

Vanilla on her chin, all the way down Maple Lane.
