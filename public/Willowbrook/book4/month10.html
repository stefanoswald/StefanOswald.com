Month 10 · Words Are Magic

For the grown-up

Nights 274–304

This month Olivia finds out that the marks on things mean something. She spots her letter everywhere, writes her name with one letter facing backward, and one night puts a finger under each of the last three words in her favorite book. That is not reading yet, and it doesn't need to be. Most children read on their own between five and seven. What counts now is being read to, by someone glad to be there.

She plays the parent at bedtime and tells books from the pictures, which is practice with turns, rules and feelings, not a test of cleverness. When she asks why the dark comes early, a short true answer is plenty.

Theo is seventeen months, then eighteen. He walks well and runs stiffly, feeds himself with a spoon, mostly, and points to a picture when you ask. His words come one at a time, not two together yet, and this month he finds one for himself.

December brings the first snow and the longest night, the year's Maybe night. The grown-ups get things wrong twice, and say sorry.

Let her finish the lines she knows and tell you what comes next. After the last line, one small question, then quiet.

One thing to try: write down something she says, word for word, and read it back. One thing not to worry about: backward letters, which nearly every child makes for a while.
