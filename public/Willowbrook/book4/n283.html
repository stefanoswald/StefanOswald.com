Night 283

The Letter to Lily

Month 10: Words Are Magic

"Papa. Can I send Lily a letter? In da mailbox?"

"You could walk over and tell her," said Papa.

"No. I want da letter to go by itself."

So Papa got a pen, and Olivia told him what to say, and he wrote down every word.

"Dear Lily. It snowed. Fee-oh sat in it. I have a library card. It's warm. Do you want to come play? I love you. Da end."

"The end?" said Papa.

"Letters have da end."

Papa wrote THE END.

Then it was her turn. She took the red crayon and wrote her name at the bottom, very big. It used up half the page. Under it she drew Lily and herself holding hands, and a snowflake the size of a dog.

In the morning they walked the letter to the mailbox and put the red flag up.

For two days Olivia checked the mailbox after breakfast, after lunch, and once in her pajamas.

On the third day an envelope came back. Inside was a drawing of two girls and a very large cake, with a floury thumbprint in the corner. Underneath, in Miss Rosa's writing, it said, "Yes. I love you too." And at the bottom, big, it said LILY.

Then it went under Olivia's pillow.
