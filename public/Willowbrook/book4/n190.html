Night 190

The Maybe That Took All Month

Month 7: The Maybe Lessons

A big wind came in the night and knocked the tomato plant flat. In the morning there were tomatoes all over the grass, hard and green, not one ready.

Olivia stood in the wet yard in her pajamas. "Dey're not red yet! Dey're ruined!"

"You waited all summer for red ones," said Mama. "And now they're on the ground."

Olivia sat down in the grass. Mama sat down too, even though it was wet.

"Maybe," Mama said, when the wet had soaked through both of them.

They picked up every green tomato. Olivia carried them in her pajama top, held out like a basket, and they lined them up on the kitchen windowsill.

"Now dey'll get red?"

"Maybe. Tomatoes take their time."

The next morning Olivia checked. Green.

The morning after that, she checked. Green.

After a week, she checked with her nose almost touching. "Dey're still green."

"Not yet," said Mama.

"Not yet is SO long."

"It is."

Olivia said good morning to them anyway, every morning, one by one. Mama drank her coffee and let her.

Then one tomato had a pale spot on its side, the color of a peach. Olivia put her nose right up to it.

"Dis one's thinking about it."

She turned it so the pale side faced the sun.
