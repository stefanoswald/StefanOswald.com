Month 9 · Brave Is Scared Plus One Step

For the grown-up

Nights 244–273

This month is about being brave, and in this house brave means something small: you are scared, and you take one step anyway. Just one. At the tall slide, at the rope in the pool, at circle time. Olivia is three and three quarters. She can tell you what happened today in order, and she can also freeze solid with a line she practiced all week.

Not every brave works. Her words go away on the morning of her line, and she only gets the front wheel up Mateo's ramp. The grown-ups don't turn those days into wins. They say what happened, stay warm, and help her plan the next try. Her fears come back, too. A fear met once is not a fear gone.

Theo is sixteen months old. He climbs the low step by himself, pushes things along, sweeps with anything that looks like a broom, and says one word at a time. He is not scared of high places, which is why his sister is scared for him.

Read these slowly. Let her ask "Dat counts?" with you, and count the thunder together. If she's close to four, one "What do you think helped?" after the last line is plenty, then quiet.

One thing to try: when she's scared, ask for one step, not the whole thing, and count that step as done. One thing not to worry about: the scared that comes back.
