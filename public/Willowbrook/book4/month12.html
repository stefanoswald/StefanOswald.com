Month 12 · Almost Four

For the grown-up

Nights 335–365

This is the birthday month. Your almost-four-year-old is waiting for a day she cannot hurry, and she has her own ideas about time: that tearing off extra paper links will bring it sooner, that she will wake up on the day a size taller. The grown-ups here give short, true answers and let her find out.

She is starting to know what other people think. She pretends not to see her brother's secret so he can surprise her, and she picks a picture each friend will like. At her own party the noise gets to be too much and she hides behind the coats. Plenty of birthday children do.

Theo is nineteen and twenty months old. He runs, kicks, climbs, and cannot keep a secret for one minute. Near the end he puts two words together. When his sister is upset he brings her his cow, the best thing he has.

Read slowly and leave room for her parts: the horn in the snowplow story, the stars on the sweater. She may ask about Buttons again, and the same answer is still the right one. After the last line you can ask one "why do you think," then let it be quiet.

One thing to try: ask her the same few questions every year and keep the answers. One thing not to worry about: tears at a party. They do not mean the party went wrong.
