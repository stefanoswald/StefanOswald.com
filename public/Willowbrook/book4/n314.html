Night 314

The Mitten Tree

Month 11: Winter Wonders

The mitten tree stood by the round window at the library, under the lantern light. It was a small pine hung with mittens and hats and scarves, and none of them had names.

"Who are dey for?" said Olivia.

"Anybody whose hands are cold," said Mama. "They just take some."

"Without asking?"

"Without asking."

On the lowest twig hung one mitten no bigger than a thumb. Nobody knew who had hung it. Up on the poetry shelf, Molly Mouse was busy washing her whiskers.

At home, Olivia opened the mitten drawer. There were the red ones, and there were the stripey ones, the best mittens in the house. She sat in front of the drawer for a long time. Mama folded laundry and let her.

"Dese," said Olivia at last, and held up the stripey ones.

"Those are your favorites," said Mama.

"Dey're da warmest."

Two days later, she hung them on the tree herself, as high as she could reach.

On the walk home, she looked back at the library twice.

"I miss dem a little," she said.

"That makes sense," said Mama. "Somebody's hands are going to be very warm."

Olivia walked to the corner with her red mitten in Mama's coat pocket, holding Mama's hand.
