Month 4 · The Dark Is Just the Day Resting

For the grown-up

Nights 91–120

This is the month the dark gets big. The same imagination that turns a box into a castle can turn a coat on a chair into something with a face, so fears of the dark, storms, dogs and bad dreams peak around now. That is a mind growing, not a step backward. Her pretend play does the same work in daylight, with her in charge.

The grown-ups in these stories do a few plain things over and over. They name the fear without arguing with it. They go and look together, flashlight in hand. They offer one small step and stay close while she takes it. They add no scary detail, and when a fear comes back another night, nobody calls it failure.

Theo turns one at the end of the month. He may stand alone for a breath, walk holding your fingers, and say "mama" and "dada" to the right people. His first fear may be the vacuum, and your calm face will tell him more than any words.

Read slowly and let her finish the lines she knows. If she brings up her own dark, listen first.

One thing to try: the next time something is in the room, pick up a flashlight and say, "Show me." One thing not to worry about: the nightlight, the open door, the nights in your bed. They can stay as long as she needs them.
