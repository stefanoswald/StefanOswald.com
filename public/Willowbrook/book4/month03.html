Month 3 · Why? Why? Why?

For the grown-up

Nights 60–90

This is the month of why. A three-year-old can ask more questions in an hour than you can count, and nearly all of them are real. She wants a true, short cause she can carry around: the toaster is hot, the air bounces the blue.

In these stories the grown-ups answer first and then point her back at the world: "What else do you see?" Papa says "I don't know, let's find out," which is a fine answer. Some whys are worries wearing a question, like the ambulance one. Name the worry, stay close, and add nothing scary. When Papa makes the spinning earth sound frightening, he notices and says sorry. One why is hard, about a friend's grandmother far away who is very sick, and Mama answers honestly and promises nothing.

Her pretend games keep running under all of this. That is where she practices rules, turns and feelings, so let them run.

Theo is ten months old. He cruises along the couch, claps, turns at his name and knows "no." Near the month's end he waves bye-bye. He babbles, and he doesn't point to ask yet. He pulls up on anything he can reach, so fasten tall furniture to the wall.

Let her stop you with her own whys. Try writing one down this week, with the date. And don't worry when the same why comes back five nights in a row. She likes the answer.
