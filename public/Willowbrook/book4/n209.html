Night 209

Towns Are Jars Too

Month 7: The Maybe Lessons

The windowsill tomatoes were turning, one by one. Mama held Olivia's hand, and they crossed the street to bring Mrs. Henderson the first two red ones.

Mrs. Henderson was in her rocker, as usual. In front of her porch, by the sidewalk, was a stump as wide as a table. Olivia climbed up on it, as usual.

"Why is dis tree so short?"

"It was the biggest maple on the lane, once," said Mrs. Henderson. "A big wind knocked it down, long before you were born."

"Was it sad?"

"Very. It fell right across the street, but nobody was hurt."

"Den what?"

"Nobody could drive anywhere for a week," said Mrs. Henderson. "So everybody walked, and talked, and carried branches together."

She rocked. "I met half this lane that week."

Olivia stood very still on the stump. "Dat's a maybe!"

Then she had to explain about the jar, and the milk, and the bus, and Mr. Socko on the post, and the green ones still thinking. Mrs. Henderson listened to all of it, rocking.

"Well," she said at the end. "Towns are jars too, honey."

Olivia looked up and down Maple Lane. Then she stood on the stump a long time, being tall.
