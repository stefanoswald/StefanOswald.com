Night 312

The Winter Feeder

Month 11: Winter Wonders

On cold mornings the feeder came first, before oatmeal.

It hung from the thin branch by the kitchen window, with Olivia's O under its roof. Papa lifted her up, and she brushed the night's snow off the tray with her mitten.

"Double," said Papa, and poured.

"Why double?"

"Birds burn a lot of food just keeping warm. Cold is expensive when you're that small."

The chickadees came before Papa's coffee was cool. They were puffed up as round as plums.

"Dey're wearing deir feathers puffy!"

"Puffy holds in warm air," said Papa. "Like your coat."

Then the squirrel came. He ran down the branch, hung by his back feet, and ate out of the tray upside down.

"Papa! He thinks it's his!"

"He does think that," said Papa.

Olivia watched him eat. "He's cold too."

So they poured a pile of seed on the old stump by the fence, far from the branch. The squirrel looked at the stump. He looked at the feeder. He went to the stump, because it was easier, and squirrels are practical.

Back inside, Olivia ate her oatmeal at the window, and everybody out there ate theirs.
