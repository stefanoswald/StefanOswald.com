Month 6 · Preschool, Turns, and Shares

For the grown-up

Nights 152–181

Preschool begins this month, at the tail end of summer, and the stories walk the whole road with her: the red backpack, visit day, the night before, the first morning, and the third, when the tears come. The third-day wobble is common.

She is practicing life in a room of twelve: waiting for the line leader pin, trading crackers and living with the trade, making up after a fight about who gets to be the mama. That pretend game is where she rehearses rules, turns and feelings. Midway through, Buttons the class hamster dies. The grown-ups say "died," give a short true answer, and give the same answer when she asks again, because she will.

At home, Theo is thirteen months old. He points at what he wants, hands you a toy and wants it straight back, drinks from an open cup with your hand under it, and takes his first steps alone. He has single words, no sentences. Some babies walk sooner and some later.

Just read these. At three and a half she needs no questions after the last line. She may tell you her own day instead, first this, then that. Try one goodbye, short and warm, and use it every morning. Don't worry about tears at the door. They are not a sign you chose wrong.
