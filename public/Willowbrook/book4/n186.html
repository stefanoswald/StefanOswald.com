Night 186

Mr. Socko on the Post

Month 7: The Maybe Lessons

Mr. Socko went for a walk on Saturday and did not come home.

Olivia found out at bedtime. Her hand was empty, and so was her pocket.

"Where's Mr. Socko?" Then louder. "WHERE?"

Papa was sure he knew. A sock goes where the socks go, so he looked in the laundry basket. He looked in the washer and under the dryer. He found two plain socks and a raisin.

"He's not DAT kind of sock!"

Olivia cried with her whole face. Mama sat on the bed and rubbed her back. "You miss him. He's your talking sock."

"He tells me stuff."

"He does." Mama kept rubbing. When the crying had slowed down, she said, "Maybe."

"Maybe," Olivia said into her pillow.

On Monday, walking home from preschool, there he was. Somebody had set him on top of the Hendersons' fence post, facing the street, the way people in Willowbrook do with lost things. A red leaf was stuck to his head like a hat.

"MR. SOCKO!"

Mrs. Henderson waved from her porch. "He's been watching the traffic since Sunday."

Olivia put him on her hand. He looked up and down the lane.

"I saw a bus," said Mr. Socko, in his scratchy voice.

He rode home on her hand, looking at everything.
