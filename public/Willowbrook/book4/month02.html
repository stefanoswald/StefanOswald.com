Month 2 · Bobby Bear Wakes Up

For the grown-up

Nights 32–59

This month the bear gets up. In Olivia's dreams, Bobby Bear walks and talks, and she brings the news to breakfast. Your own child may have someone like him: a stuffed animal with opinions, a friend nobody else can see. This is not confusion. Three-year-olds know pretend from real better than it looks, and a bear who walks gives her a safe place to practice big things, like being scared, helping someone else be brave, and the new idea that other people know different things than she does.

Theo is nine and ten months old. He crawls fast, pulls himself up at the crib rail and has no idea how to get down, bangs two blocks together, and picks up soft food between his finger and thumb. He babbles. He has no words yet, whatever his sister tells you.

Read these plainly and let the jokes land. If she wants to do the bear's voice, let her. When she tells you a dream, ask "And then what?" and follow it. Papa gets the storm wrong before he gets it right, and you will too, some nights. Going back in is allowed.

One thing to try: at breakfast, ask "Did you go anywhere last night?" and then just listen.

One thing not to worry about: a dream that changes a little every time she tells it. That is how a three-year-old tells anything, and it is how stories get made.
