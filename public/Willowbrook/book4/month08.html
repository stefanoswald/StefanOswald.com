Month 8 · Helping Hands

For the grown-up

Nights 213–243

This is the month of real jobs. A three-and-a-half-year-old wants to help with everything, and her help is slower and messier than doing it yourself. When she waters the fern or lines up the shoes, she finds out she belongs to this house and that the house notices when she forgets.

Nobody in these stories is paid, and nobody gets a sticker. The jobs are part of living together, and the grown-ups say what they saw and say thank you. Helping also goes wrong here: Olivia finishes her brother's puzzle for him, Papa takes over her socks to hurry, and both of them give it back. Late in the month she has a heavy day and lets herself be helped, which is harder than anything on the list.

Theo is fifteen months old. He walks well, climbs onto the couch, hands you things one at a time and has a handful of single words. His first real tantrum comes this month, a short loud storm that ends with him reaching for Mama.

Read plainly and leave room for the sounds: sheets snapping, the recycling truck, frost that crunches. She will say the lines she knows.

One thing to try: give her one job that is truly hers, with something that shows when it is missed, like a plant. One thing not to worry about: the job done badly. For a while, help makes more work, not less.
