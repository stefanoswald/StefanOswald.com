Night 114

The Night Walk

Month 4: The Dark Is Just the Day Resting

In June the dark comes so late that Olivia had never once been out in it. So one warm night, after pajamas and teeth, Mama said, "Shoes on. We're going out."

"In da dark?"

"In the dark. All of us."

They went out the front door in pajamas and sneakers. Theo rode on Papa's chest in the carrier. The lane was the same lane, but every window was lit gold, and the sky over the roofs was going from deep blue to black.

Then the lawns started blinking.

"Fireflies," said Papa.

There were hundreds. They rose out of the grass slowly, on and off, on and off. Olivia stood in the middle of the sidewalk with her mouth open. Theo waved bye-bye at them. The fireflies did not wave back.

They walked on. Crickets. A far owl. The brook, which you can hear at night once the cars stop. In the gold dog's window, the gold dog lifted his head and gave one soft woof. Mrs. Henderson was sitting out on her dark porch, and she waved.

"Da dark is busy," said Olivia.

Overhead the stars came out, first one at a time, and then too fast to tell which was next.

Theo was asleep by the Hendersons' gate.
