Month 1 · Pretend!

For the grown-up

Nights 1–31

Olivia has just turned three. This month a spoon becomes a dragon, the rug becomes the sea, and an invisible guest named Franklin takes two sugars. If your child is doing the same, their hold on the real world is fine. Pretend is where three-year-olds practice rules, turns, and the feelings they cannot yet say straight out. A doll who keeps leaving is often a child working on a goodbye.

The grown-ups here play along without taking over, and sometimes they get it wrong. Papa keeps reading while the store is open. Mama snaps about mud. Both come back, say what they did, and say sorry. When a game tips into fear, Mama does not argue about whether the bear is real. She names the fear, stays close for one step, and lets Olivia send him home.

Theo is eight months old. He sits, babbles, starts to crawl, and cries when his mama leaves the room. He does not talk or stand yet. In the games he is the dog, the fish and the cargo, and he is delighted.

At three, just read. Let your child do the rawr and the beep beep, and save the questions for later.

One thing to try: join a game in the smallest part, the customer or the patient, and take the orders. One thing not to worry about: the invisible friend. Set out a cup.
