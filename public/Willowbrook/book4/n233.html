Night 233

Carrying for Wren's Grandmother

Month 8: Helping Hands

On Tuesdays Wren's grandmother picked Wren up from preschool. She walked with a cane, and she carried Wren's backpack and her own market bag, and home was two blocks away. Olivia and Mama walked the same way.

"I can carry dat bag," said Olivia. "I'm strong."

Wren's grandmother looked at her over her glasses. Then she handed it over. It had a loaf of bread in it and two lemons.

Olivia started off fast. After a while she looked back. Wren's grandmother was far behind: cane, then foot, then cane.

Olivia came back and walked at cane speed.

"Are your knees slow today?" asked Olivia.

"My knees are slow every day," said Wren's grandmother. "They are very old knees."

At cane speed there was time to look at things. The gold dog behind his fence. A red leaf shaped like a hand. A pumpkin somebody had set on a wall.

Wren's grandmother told them about the bakery where she worked when she was a girl, far away, where the bread was round like a wheel.

"Bigger dan Rosa's?" asked Olivia.

"Much bigger," said Wren's grandmother. "Not as good."

At her door Olivia gave back the bag.

"Thank you, little one." Wren's grandmother smiled with her whole face, all the lines at once.
