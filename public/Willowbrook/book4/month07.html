Month 7 · The Maybe Lessons

For the grown-up

Nights 182–212

This is the family's Maybe month. Every night something small goes wrong: milk spills, a crayon snaps, rain cancels the farm. In every story someone names the feeling first and waits, and only after that does anyone say "Maybe." What she is growing is the room between a disappointment and whatever comes next. Said too soon, "Maybe" sounds like "stop being sad," and Papa finds that out and says sorry.

Not every Maybe turns. The green tomatoes on the windowsill take weeks, and one never turns at all. Good news gets a "Maybe" once, which she may find very funny. And when Buttons comes up, Mama says plainly that he died, and that Maybe is not for that.

Theo is walking with his arms up, sitting down hard, and getting up again. He has a few words, one at a time and never two together yet, and he points, and brings you things to show. He never says the word. He teaches it anyway, in a puddle.

Read these plainly and let the turns land on their own. When a why comes, give a true short answer first. If she wants to talk, "What happened next?" is plenty.

One thing to try: when a plan falls apart, say what she wanted before you say anything else.

One thing not to worry about: if she shouts "No maybe!" at you. It means she knows the word, and the order.
