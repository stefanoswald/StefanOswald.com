Night 216

The Leaf-Raking Crew

Month 8: Helping Hands

The big maple in Mrs. Henderson's yard dropped all its leaves in one windy night.

In the morning her whole lawn was orange. Mrs. Henderson stood on her porch with her rake and looked at it for a long time.

Mateo saw her from two porches down. By lunch there was a crew: Mateo with his tool belt and a rake taller than he was, Lily with her mama's garden rake, and Olivia with the little rake from the shed. Papa walked them across the street and sat on the curb with his coffee.

"Lily's Plan Maple," said Lily. "Step one, rake. Step two, pile. Step three, muffins."

"Dere's no muffins," said Olivia.

"Step three is still muffins."

They raked. Mateo raked in straight lines. Lily raked in circles. Olivia crawled under the hedge after the leaves that were hiding.

The pile grew as high as Mateo's chest.

"Now what?" said Mateo.

Mrs. Henderson came down her steps. "In my experience," she said, "that's what piles are for."

They jumped. The pile went everywhere.

Then they raked it all up again, slower this time.

Mrs. Henderson looked at her lawn. "You got the ones under the hedge," she said. "I never get those."

There were no muffins. Olivia went home with a leaf in her curls.
