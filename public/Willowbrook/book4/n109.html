Night 109

The Meet-at-the-Mailbox Drill

Month 4: The Dark Is Just the Day Resting

On a sunny Saturday, with nothing wrong anywhere, the family practiced what to do if there was ever a fire. Nobody likes to learn a thing in a hurry.

"If the smoke alarm ever beeps for real, we get low," said Papa. "Smoke lives up high. Good air lives down low."

"Then we get out," said Mama. "Nothing else. Not toys. Not even Baba."

Olivia held Bobby Bear tighter.

"Bears are replaceable. You are not," said Mama. "He'd tell you the same."

Olivia thought about that. Then she nodded, once.

"Then we meet at the mailbox," said Papa. "Everybody goes there."

"Nobody goes back in," said Mama. "The firefighters do the going back in."

"Who gets Fee-oh?"

"Papa gets Theo," said Mama. "I get you. Every time."

Olivia crawled across the living room, fast, out the front door and down the walk. Theo crawled too, the wrong way, toward the kitchen. Papa scooped him up.

At the mailbox, Olivia put her whole hand on it and counted the family. "Mama, Papa, Fee-oh, me." Then she counted again, to be sure.

"Dada," said Theo, right into Papa's ear.

They ran it once more, faster. Papa put the next one on the calendar, for fall. Then everybody went back to Saturday.

On the way in, Olivia thanked the mailbox.
