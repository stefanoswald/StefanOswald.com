Month 11 · Winter Wonders

For the grown-up

Nights 305–334

This is a month of waiting. A chrysalis turns up on the kitchen windowsill, and Olivia checks it every day and tells it her news. Waiting is hard at almost four, and checking is how she carries it.

Her whys are about butterflies, frost and the dark at supper, and a true short answer does more than a long one. When the big sled hill scares her, a grown-up names the fear, offers one step and rides along. The fear may come back. That's fine.

You will see her comfort her brother when his snowman melts, give away her favorite mittens and miss them, and run a library under the table for days. Pretend like that practices rules, turns and feelings. She gets things wrong and tries again. So do the grown-ups, who come back and say sorry.

Theo is a year and a half. "No" and "mine" have arrived, and he climbs whatever holds still. He can follow one small direction, like "put it in the bowl," and he still says one word at a time.

She can answer a simple question about a story now. After the last line, ask one, then let the page be quiet.

One thing to try: a carrot top in a saucer of water on a sunny sill, checked together each morning.

One thing not to worry about: if she misses what she gave away. That is part of giving.
