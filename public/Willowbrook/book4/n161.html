Night 161

The Sand Timer

Month 6: Preschool, Turns, and Shares

There was one easel, and everybody wanted it. Olivia and Lily got there at the same time.

Lily already had the brush. So Miss Dana gave Olivia the sand timer.

"Lily paints until the sand runs out," said Miss Dana. "Then it's your turn."

Olivia held the timer. The sand ran down very slowly. It was the slowest sand in Willowbrook.

"Is it done?"

"No," said Lily, painting a sun.

"Is it done now?"

"No."

Olivia had an idea. If she turned the timer over, the sand would have to hurry. She turned it over.

The sand started again from the top.

Lily looked at the timer. Then she looked at Olivia. Then she laughed, and her brush slid a stripe right through her sun.

"You gave me more!"

Miss Dana didn't fix it. "You turned it over, and it started again," she said. "What could you do next time?"

"Hold it still," said Olivia, in a very small voice.

So she held it still with both hands until the sand ran out. Then it was her turn, and Lily held the timer, perfectly still, which is harder when the turn belongs to somebody else.

Olivia painted a horse. It had waited through two whole timers.
