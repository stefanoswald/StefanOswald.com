Month 6 · Yum

For the grown-up

Nights 152–181

She is seventeen months old and the words are coming faster, still one at a time. Banana is "nana," apple is "appo." She feeds herself with her fingers, tries the spoon and gets some of it in, and drinks from an open cup with both hands. Ask her to bring you the cup and she will, most days.

This month the stories are food, so the book is a plate. Point at the banana in its coat, the corn in its leaves, the orange in its boats, and name each one, then wait. The sounds are hers: the apple's crunch, the whoo-whoo of blowing on soup, Henrietta's bok. If she wants the same page ten nights running, read it ten nights. That is learning, not a rut. No questions after the last line, not yet.

A new food gets one small piece beside the things she already likes, and nobody says a word about it. A poke counts. A lick counts. It can take ten tries, spread over weeks. When she pushes the plate away, that is the meal, no one more bite. "Mine" over the orange is the age, not the child.

One thing to try: let her hold the spoon at soup, with a towel under the chair. One thing not to worry about: the day she eats three bites of dinner and nothing else. She eats across a week, not a meal.
