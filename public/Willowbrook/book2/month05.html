Month 5 · All About Me

For the grown-up

Nights 121–151

This month she is finding out that she has a body, and that it is hers. Sixteen months: she points to her nose when you ask, then yours, then the duck in the picture. She eats with her fingers and makes a study of the spoon. She climbs the stairs with your hand, pulls off a sock, and carries heavy things, which she prefers to light ones.

The stories go part by part: fingers, feet, belly, ears, teeth, the laugh that lives down deep. Point at the part in the picture, find it on her, then on you. No questions afterward. The sounds inside the story are hers: boom boom on the belly, hic, boing, the grrrrl of a hungry stomach, thum-thum with an ear on your chest. If she wants the same page ten nights running, you are not stuck. That is a word going in.

You will hear no a great deal, and mine. That is the age talking, not the child. She has noticed she is a separate person.

One thing to try: when she says no to tickles, stop at once, hands up where she can see them, and wait. She will ask for more, and she will have found out her no works.

One thing not to worry about: the wary look she gives the mirror the first time she catches on. Being somebody is a lot to take in before breakfast.
