Month 2 · Animal Friends

For the grown-up

Nights 32–59

This month the animals come to her, one a night. She is thirteen months old, new on her feet, and everything that moves is worth a stop: a worm, a cat on a wall, five ducks in a row. You will see her stop dead for something you would have walked past.

She is growing names. She understands far more than she says, and says one word at a time: duck, egg, no, more, up. Point at the animal and name it, then wait. A point from her is an answer. So is a sound. Woof, bok, quack, buzz, whoo, hmph: the pages leave room for them. The Duchess says no. So will she, more and more, and "mine" is on its way. That is the age, not the child, and the stories treat no as a word she owns, not a battle.

Keep the joining-in inside the reading. No questions after the last line, just the lamp. If she wants the same page ten nights running, read it ten nights. She is not stuck. She is learning it.

One thing to try: at your own window before bed, say goodnight to whatever is out there. The dog, the tree, the car. She will start adding to the list.

One thing not to worry about: she will not share yet, and nobody in these pages asks her to. Bobby Bear takes turns in a dream. That is plenty for now.
