Month 9 · One, Two, Three!

For the grown-up

Nights 244–273

She is near twenty months now, and the numbers have arrived as words before they arrive as amounts. "One" is real. "Two" is real and means anything more than one. Everything past two is "lots," or a wild string of sounds. These stories count the way she counts: stairs, hens, kisses, ducks.

The other arrival is two words at once. You will hear them in the stories ("One Livy," "Boot dere," "Bear kiss," "Two songs") and then, some morning, in your own kitchen. They are two things she wants you to know, put side by side. Say them back whole, and leave it there.

Count inside things you already do: socks into the drawer, scoops into the bowl, steps up to the gate. Never ask her how many. If she says two and it was four, two is fine. The rhythm is the point. Sounds and refrains inside the reading, no questions after the last line. She can say "One!", "Two!", "Lots!", "Hop!" and "Off!" Let her.

If she wants the piggy toes ten nights running, that is how the words get in. "No" and "mine" are loud this month too. That is the age, not the child.

One thing to try: count backward at the lamp, five to one, and let her do the pats. One thing not to worry about: she will not share the last cracker, and she does not need to yet.
