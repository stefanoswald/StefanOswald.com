Month 1 · My World

For the grown-up

Nights 1–31

She is one, and the house is hers. She is walking, or nearly, and into everything at knee height, so these stories stay home: the cup, the sock, the shoes in their line. Outside it is March, mud on the walk and the brook running high. She is looking at the spoon.

She understands far more than she says. Expect one word at a time: more, hot, mine, uh-oh. She points to ask what a thing is, and points to show you she found it. Both deserve an answer. She copies what you do. The little broom and the big pot are her joining in.

Point at the picture and name what is there, the cup, the duck, the bear, then wait. Make the sounds together: the creak of the door, the click of the lamp, boom on the pot. The chants with a hole in them, where is the sock, where is the bear, are hers to fill with a point or a shout. No questions after the last line. Just the lamp.

The same story ten nights running is not a rut. It is learning. Listen for the night she says the word before you do.

"No" and "mine" are the age, not the child, and not you. Try letting her turn the pages, backward if she likes. Do not worry about the oatmeal on the floor. It goes there for everyone.
