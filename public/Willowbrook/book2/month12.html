Month 12 · Bedtime Itself

For the grown-up

Nights 335–365

She is twenty-three months old and the words are coming in pairs now. "No bed." "Dog gone." "Da truck." Two words, never three yet, and each pair is a whole speech. She points to the duck in the book when you ask, follows "get the bear and bring it here," pats a sad face, and puts her own bear to bed with the whole routine done to him first.

This month's subject is bedtime itself. Bath, pajamas, milk, teeth, story, song, crib, in that order every night. The order is the thing that works. She will start the next step before you name it. You will also hear "no bed," and that is the age talking, not the child.

Leave the sounds open for her: the icicle's drip, the peep from the brook, the "check" as she pats the sill museum. On Maybe night she shouts the family word. The same story ten nights running is not a rut. It is how she learns it.

One thing to try: say "I'll be back" when you leave the room, and be back before she calls. One thing not to worry about: the second call after lights out. Go in without the light, few words, out again.

A year ago she had one word and a wobble. Now she has two candles, a word in the willow bark, and a bear who has told her what the next book holds, starting tomorrow.
