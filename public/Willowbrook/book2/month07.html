Month 7 · Outside!

For the grown-up

Nights 182–212

She is eighteen months old and the words are coming faster now. Still one at a time, but more of them, and some are animal sounds that stand in for the animal. "No" is a whole sentence. So is "mine." Both are the age, not the child. She runs. She wants to climb.

This month is outside, and outside is where she learns by touching. In these stories the grown-up is always close and almost never lifting: Papa's hands wait beside the low branch, Mama waits at the top of the slide until Olivia pats the slide. Notice how much happens when the adult is one step back. That is the point of this month.

Read the lists slowly and let her point: the ants, the dog's nose, the porch lights coming on one by one. The sounds are hers to make: the stomp in the puddle, the plip on the hood, bok for the hens. Do not ask her anything after the last line. Just close the book. If she wants the same story ten nights running, that is not you stuck. That is her learning it.

One thing to try: a walk to the corner where she sets the pace and you stop where she stops. One thing not to worry about: she plays beside Lily, not with her, and she keeps her cup. Sharing is years off. Nobody in this book asks her for it.
