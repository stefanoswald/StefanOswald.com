Month 4 · A World of Colors

For the grown-up

Nights 91–120

She is fifteen months old and she has words now. A few, one at a time, and each one comes with a point. The point is doing most of the work. Say the whole thing back, "Yes, the red cup." She scribbles now, and climbs stairs with your hand.

The stories this month are colors, so they are pointing games. Read the lists slowly and let her find each thing on the page: the cup, the boots, the bow. When a story says "There!" wait for her finger. The sounds are hers too: the frog's plop, the fwoo of the dandelion, the click of the lamp, the ohhhh at the rainbow. She will not make them the first night. By the tenth night she will make them before you do. That is not you stuck on one story. That is her learning it.

This month a first tantrum may arrive, fast and loud and over in a minute. In story 104 it comes over a crayon. Watch what Mama does: gets low, says almost nothing, keeps the crayon out of the mouth, offers two colors. The burst is the age, not the child. So is "mine." So is "no."

One thing to try: a color hunt through the house with one block in her hand. One thing not to worry about: whether she says any color names yet. She knows them long before she says them.
