Month 8 · Splash

For the grown-up

Nights 213–243

She is nineteen months old and the words are coming faster now, still one at a time, each one a whole sentence. "No" is the biggest, and it is the age, not the child. So is "mine." She runs. She pulls her own sock off. She bends and pushes, and both feet stay on the ground. The jump comes after two. Beside another child she plays alongside, not with. That is the shape for now.

This month is water. Point at what she points at and say the word back: duck, cup, drain, boots. The sounds are the game. The gurgle of the drain, the ho ho ho through a bubble beard. Leave a gap for her. She will not fill it the first night. By the tenth night she will fill it before you turn the page. That is learning, not a rut.

Watch the small fears. The drain is loud, the car wash louder, the brook cold through the boots. Each time the grown-up stays close, keeps an easy face, says a few plain words, and waits. Nobody argues her out of it. Someone is within arm's reach for every bath here, a hand on the rim.

One thing to try: say "two more minutes" before the bath ends, then mean it, with one small choice on the way out. One thing not to worry about: tears over a banana broken in half. They pass faster when nobody hurries them.
