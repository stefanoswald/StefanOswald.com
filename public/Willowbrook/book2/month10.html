Month 10 · Opposites

For the grown-up

Nights 274–304

She is close to two, and the words are starting to come in pairs. In the stories you will hear "Boot gone," "Livy down," "Mama back," "Livy turn," and then over your own cereal some morning. Two words is a whole thought for her. Say it back whole.

The opposites are not a lesson. They are the day, noticed twice: the bowl of snow and the warm cup, the boot inside the boot. Nobody asks her which is which. Point, name, make the sound. She can do "Up!" and "Down!", "In!" and "Out!", "Boo!", and "Shh." Refrains inside the reading, no questions after.

If she wants the box or the bear in the basket ten nights running, let her. The tenth night is where it goes in.

"No" and "mine" are loud this month, and the first block fight arrives. That is the age, not the child. The turns are yours to hold for a long while yet.

Together and apart is the story about you leaving. Say where you are going and that you will be back, one kiss, and go. The crying after the door is real. So is the coming back.

One thing to try: let her call the game, big voice and little voice, and get it wrong once so she can fix you. One thing not to worry about: she will say "sad" while smiling and building the tower again. Both are true.
