Month 11 · Shapes Everywhere

For the grown-up

Nights 305–334

She is almost two, and this month you may hear it: two words at a time. A thing and what it did. A name and a want. In these stories she says "Big circle," "Moon gone," "Papa hand," and that is what the first two-word lines sound like. They come one at a time, weeks apart, and then in a rush. Fifty words or more are in there now.

The month is shapes, but shapes are not a lesson. Nobody in these pages asks her what shape the plate is. Shapes are things to point at, to stack and knock over, to trace with one finger in the frost on the glass. She will start to tell the round blocks from the square ones long before she says circle.

Read the sounds with her, inside the story, not after it. Bump, bump, point on the bear's foot. Press, wiggle, lift. She can do the last word of the star rhyme and the tickle at the end of the garden one. Then the lamp, and no questions.

If she wants the puzzle story ten nights running, she is not stuck. She is learning it.

One thing to try: put your hand flat on paper and let her draw round it. Your hand will jump. Keep the bump.

One thing not to worry about: no, and mine, and mine again. That is the age, not her. The birthday is a month off.
