Month 4 · Waiting for Baby

For the grown-up

Nights 91–120

This is the month of waiting. Your two-year-old knows something big is coming and cannot tell how far off it is. Next week and next month are the same size to her, so waiting she can see helps: a paper chain, a strawberry turning red, a bag by the door.

In these pages she talks to the tummy, packs her own bag, and asks what most firstborns ask: will you still love me? The worry comes back on tired days, sometimes as a storm over a broken cracker. The storm is loud first, then smaller, then a hand reaching for you. Stay close, say very little, and name the worry afterward. You don't have to argue it away. Answering it again is the answer.

The grown-ups get things wrong too. Mama snaps when a knee lands on her belly, and Papa misses the big jump watching his phone. Both come back and say sorry. Nobody makes Olivia share, not her toys and not her mother.

Read plainly and let her interrupt, ask for the same page, and fix the words in Papa's song. At two, the sounds are the point. Save the questions for later.

One thing to try: a small just-us time now, before the baby, kept after, even when it shrinks. And if she asks the same worried question five nights running, don't worry. She isn't stuck. She's checking that the answer still holds.
