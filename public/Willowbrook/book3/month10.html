Month 10 · Try Again

For the grown-up

Nights 274–304

This month she tries again. At nearly three her eyes run ahead of her hands. She knows what a duck looks like and cannot draw one. She hears that "pasketti" is wrong and cannot fix it. That gap makes her furious, and the storms are short: a loud burst, tears, then reaching for you. Stay close, and name the feeling once it has passed. The grown-ups here give her one new word, yet, and then step back.

Theo is five months old, then six. He sits propped on his hands and topples, usually laughing. He passes a block from hand to hand, then to his mouth. Near six months he tastes his first food, mashed soft, strapped into a high chair with a grown-up beside him. He moves to his own crib, on his back, with nothing in it but him. He babbles "ba" and "buh," and Olivia counts "buh" as duck. Let her. True words come nearer his first birthday. He is not crawling, standing or talking yet.

Read with the sounds in: the bell, the flump, the click. She will say "yet" before you do. Let her. Nobody makes her share, and the bread she offers Lily is her own idea.

One thing to try: when she says "I can't," add "yet" once, then leave it. One thing not to worry about: crumpled drawings. There will be more.
