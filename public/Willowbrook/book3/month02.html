Month 2 · Me Do It

For the grown-up

Nights 32–59

She is twenty-five months old this month, and the words you will hear most are "Me do it." April is puddles and boots, and the stories follow her through the sock, the small jug, the crooked sweater, the big bed, the jacket flip, and the day the whole jug goes over.

What she is growing is the feeling of being someone who can. It grows in the doing, not in getting it right. Your job is mostly hands: keeping them in your lap, or sitting on them. When she truly cannot, as with laces, the grown-ups here shrink the task to one part she can do and wait with an open hand. Two choices work better than an order. The storms are short: loud, then whimpery, then reaching for you. Stay close, say one thing, and name the feeling after, not during.

Read these as they are shaped, in short lines, and let her say "Me do it" with you when it comes. She will interrupt, ask for the sock page again, and correct you. Let her. Nobody in these pages makes her share her spreader or her jug, and nobody needs to yet.

One thing to try: a small jug she can lift, half full, and the cloth on her chair. One thing not to worry about: the third trip to the doorway after bedtime. Boring wins, in a week or two.
