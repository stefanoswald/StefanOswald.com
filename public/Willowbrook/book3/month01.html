Month 1 · Big Feelings Have Names

For the grown-up

Nights 1–31

She turned two on the first of March, and this month her feelings get their first names. Happy she already has, and uses for nearly everything. Sad and mad come next, and scared last, once she has heard it from you a good many times. Her own lines are two or three words long.

You will see a storm or two. At two the shape is loud first, a short burst of stamping or throwing, then a fade into whimpering and a reach for you. Most are over inside five minutes. The grown-ups here stay close through the loud part, say almost nothing, name it afterward, and hold the line. That is the whole job, and it is hardest at seven in the morning.

Read these pages with your face and your voice. Make the faces in the name game. Do the thump, the ding-dong, the zzzip. She will interrupt, want the same page again tomorrow, and correct you when Papa gets the balloon wrong on purpose. Let her. Questions can wait a year.

When she hands Lily the bucket, no one told her to. Sharing comes on its own after doing things together. Nobody here makes her.

One thing to try: one feeling each at dinner, and no fixing. One thing not to worry about: the dark corner will be scary again the next night. Bringing the lamp again is the point.
