Month 5 · Theo Arrives

For the grown-up

Nights 121–151

This is the month the baby comes home, and your two-year-old meets him the way she meets most things: up close and all at once. Mostly she wants to help, to look, and to ask. The green pinch of jealousy comes too, often while you are feeding him. When it turns into a storm, stay close, say little, and name it afterward. Nobody here asks her to share her mother nicely.

Theo, in these pages, does what a newborn does. He sleeps, feeds, cries, and turns toward a voice he heard all spring, hers included. He grips a finger because every new baby does. He sees best at about the distance of your face. The smiles he makes in his sleep are real, but the one meant for a person usually comes a few weeks later. He sleeps on his back in a bassinet by his parents' bed, with nothing else in it.

Read these plainly. She will stop you to tell you what Theo does, and she will be right about half of it. Let the short lines and the sounds carry the page. Questions can wait.

One thing to try: give her a real job, like the diaper run, and keep a small just-us time, even an hour. One thing not to worry about: the night she asks to be rocked like the baby. She is checking that your arms still fit her.
