Night 288

The Kite That Wouldn't

Month 10: Try Again

At the back of the closet was a kite.

Red. A diamond. A tail of little bows.

Papa said it was his when he was small.

On the kite hill the trees were bare

and the wind was lazy.

First the kite dragged through the grass

like a dog that doesn't want a walk.

Then a gust lifted it

up over Papa's head,

and it dove straight down on its nose.

Then it spun.

The tail tied itself in a knot.

Down.

Again. Grass.

Again. Nose.

Olivia threw the string down.

"No more kite!"

She stomped both boots. Then she cried.

Papa knelt in the cold grass beside her.

After a while she leaned on him.

"Mad," said Papa. "You wanted it up high."

"It not fly!"

"Not today. It needs a big wind."

He untied the knot. He wound the string.

The kite rode home under his arm.

Every night she checked the treetops.
