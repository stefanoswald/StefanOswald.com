Month 6 · Big Sister School

For the grown-up

Nights 152–181

This month your two-year-old goes to work as a big sister. She wants to touch him, show him off, guard him, and tell you everything he does. Now and then she wants him gone. When she says it, hear tiredness, not a plan. Stay close, say one thing, name the feeling after the loud part, and let the morning do the rest.

Theo, at about six weeks, gives his first real smile to a face he knows, and in these pages it goes to her. He coos, "ah" and "goo," and his eyes follow a face or a toy across the room. On his tummy he lifts his head for a moment. His hands are mostly fists, starting to open. He is not rolling, sitting, laughing out loud, or eating anything but milk, and he still sleeps on his back in the bassinet by his parents' bed with nothing in it but him.

Read these slowly and make the sounds. Coo back when Theo coos. She will stop you to say what her brother does. Let her. Nobody here makes her share. The doll she lends at the play mat is her own idea, and the bear stays hers.

One thing to try: let her be the first hello in the morning. One thing not to worry about: the day she needs her own corner. Quiet is not the same as leaving him.
