Month 11 · Kindness Is a Muscle

For the grown-up

Nights 305–334

At nearly three, kindness is mostly something she does with her body. She holds a door with her whole back, pulls a casserole across the street in a sled, and pats a space on the blanket for a girl she doesn't know. On a day with no nap she also snatches and pushes. That storm is loud, then small. Stay close, hold the limit, and name it once she is quiet. Then answer with what she did, not who she is: "You pushed. Then you went back."

Theo is six months old, then seven. He sits up alone now and topples, and a first tooth may appear. A teething ring should be cold, not frozen. He babbles strings like "da-da-da," which mean nothing yet, whatever anyone at the table claims. He may cry in arms he doesn't know, even kind ones, and reach for his own people. He is not crawling, standing or saying words yet.

She is still under three, so give her sounds and lines to say, not questions. She will interrupt and correct you. Let her. Nobody here makes her share or give. When she gives, it is her choice, and it costs her.

One thing to try: when she does a kind thing, say plainly what it did for someone. One thing not to worry about: "Me bad" after a spill. Skip the argument and show her how she talks to her doll.
