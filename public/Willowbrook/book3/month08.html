Month 8 · Tantrum Weather

For the grown-up

Nights 213–243

This is the month of storms. At two and a half, her wants have outgrown her words, and when a big want meets a small no, the storm comes: a loud, hot burst that fades into crying and reaching for you. Most are over in a few minutes. They feel longer.

Your part is small and hard. Stay close, keep her safe, and ask nothing, because her words are out of reach just then. Name it after she comes back to you, then the limit, then what to do next. Nobody in these pages is made to share or to say sorry.

Theo is three months old, then four. He laughs, squeals, grabs and holds on, and drools on his fists with no tooth yet. At the very end of the month he rolls from his tummy to his back, once, and only his sister sees it. He is not sitting, crawling, or saying words, and he still sleeps on his back in the bassinet by his parents' bed, with nothing in it but him.

Read these with the sounds in, and when she stops you to roar along, let her. One thing to try: two choices you can live with, offered before the hard parts of the day. One thing not to worry about: a storm in a public place. Most of the people watching have had one, or raised one.
