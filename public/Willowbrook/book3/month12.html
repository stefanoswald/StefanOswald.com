Month 12 · Almost Three

For the grown-up

Nights 335–365

This is her last month of two. She talks in short sentences now, asks why, and remembers. She can tell you about the day the carts crashed and the morning her brother was born, and she will correct you if you get it wrong. Borrow the remember game from these pages: tell her a true story about her own year and leave gaps for her to fill.

Theo is seven months old, then eight. He sits by himself, gets up on his hands and knees and rocks, blows raspberries and babbles strings: "la-la," "li-li." This month his sister hears her name in it, and the family decides it counts. Let them. True first words usually come nearer his first birthday. He is not crawling, pulling up, standing or talking yet.

Birthday weeks are big, and big days often end in a storm: a hot burst, tears, then reaching for you. Stay close, say little, and name it once it has passed. Nobody makes her share her new things. Turns come on their own.

Read slowly. She knows these people now and will say their names before you do.

One thing to try: write down one thing she said this year and keep it somewhere safe. One thing not to worry about: tears at the end of the party. They often come with the candles.
