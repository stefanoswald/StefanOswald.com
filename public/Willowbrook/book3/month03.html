Month 3 · A Friend Called Lily

For the grown-up

Nights 60–90

She is twenty-six months old, the lilacs are out, and this month belongs to Lily. The two of them have known each other since the bench, but this is the month the friendship becomes their own: a cookie, a run, a crash in the cereal aisle, two carts swapped below your sightline, and the spaghetti dinner that came of it.

What she is growing is a first real friendship, and at two that looks less like sharing than like being there. She plays beside Lily and then, for a minute, with her. They copy each other, laugh at a word for no reason, pull on one handle, fall out and build again. Fights at this age are loud and short. Stay near, say little, and name what happened once it is over. Nobody in these pages makes her share. The grown-ups narrate the turns and hold the clock, and the rest comes later.

Read it in its short lines. She will want the crash every night for a week, and she will want to say the bonk herself. Let her. Near the end there is news: a baby is coming, when the strawberries come. Expect questions about belly buttons.

One thing to try: when she says she misses someone, say it back to her before you fix it. One thing not to worry about: the sleepover that ends at nine. First sleepovers mostly do.
