Month 9 · Counting to Five

For the grown-up

Nights 244–273

This month she counts everything: stairs, crackers, geese, your fingers. She can say one to five like a song, and she can truly count about three things, touching each. Past three the song runs ahead of her finger, and for a week every number on the street is a five. Count with her slowly, and let her be wrong.

Theo is four months old as the month begins and five as it ends. He rolls from tummy to back, and by the end, on his own clock, the other way too. He grabs his feet, reaches with both hands, mouths everything, and laughs when your face comes back. He is not sitting alone, eating anything but milk, or saying words. A rolling baby belongs on the floor, and small things belong up high.

Read slowly and leave room for her numbers. Make the scrunch, the click and the bloop, and skip the quizzing. She will correct Freddy, Papa and you.

Storms still come, short and loud. Stay close, say little, and name it after. The finger squeeze comes once she is in your lap, never to stop the crying. Nobody here makes her share, and with a baby, a trade works better than a count.

One thing to try: when you give five more minutes, count them aloud and keep them. One thing not to worry about: the week every number is five.
