Month 7 · Potty Power

For the grown-up

Nights 182–212

This month she starts listening to her own body. The potty arrives like a new chair, and she comes to it when she is ready. Nobody here names an age or hands out a prize. What she gets is her own delight and your plain noticing: you felt it, and you went.

Expect accidents, a rainy week when it all seems to slide back, and a fear of the big flush. Meet wet pants with dry ones and no sigh, and if a sigh gets out, say sorry. Dry nights often come much later, sometimes years later. The bath-time talk about her body is meant to be said again, in your own words. Nobody makes her share. The duck pants she lends Lily are her idea.

Theo is two months old, then three. He holds his head steady, pushes up on his forearms, finds his hands, bats at a ring, and near the end of the month his first laugh goes to his sister. Many babies laugh later. He is not rolling, sitting, or crawling, and he still sleeps on his back in the bassinet by his parents' bed, with nothing in it but him.

Read these with the sounds in, and let her say the wet-pants line with you. One thing to try: spare clothes in a bag by the door. One thing not to worry about: a week that goes backward. Something else has usually changed.
